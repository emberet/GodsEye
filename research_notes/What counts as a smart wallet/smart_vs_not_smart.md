# Smart wallets vs leaderboard impostors on Solana memecoins (research note, Oct 2026)

Legend: **[S]** sourced claim (URL given) · **[I]** my inference · **[V]** vendor-affiliated or self-interested source. "Not published" means I searched and found no stated threshold.

The two strongest primary sources are both from the same UCL/HKU group (Luo, Xu et al.). One is a WWW '26 paper on bot-resistant meme-coin copy trading, arXiv 2601.08641. The other is an on-chain manipulation study of 6,000 pump.fun coins, Ding/Luo/Xu, SBFC 2025 / SSRN 5953738. Their main finding is the key point for GodsEye: **statistically significant "winners" on pump.fun are disproportionately creators and snipers, and a copier pays an "imitation penalty" big enough to turn a profitable leader into a losing copy.**

---

## 1. Wallet types that fake or inflate "smart" stats

**Takeaway:** Most high-win-rate, high-PnL wallets get their edge from structural access: same-block entry, deployer funding, or self-trading. None of that is copyable. The fingerprints are mostly about *timing relative to launch*, *funding links* and *trade symmetry*, not about PnL.

- **Bundlers / dev (rat) wallets.** [S] A non-creator wallet that buys *in the token's creation block* is treated as creator-controlled. Solana has no public mempool, so independent front-runners can't get into that block ([arXiv 2601.08641 §4.3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08641); [SBFC paper §3](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_4G3_P154.pdf)). About 24% of the 6,000 sampled projects had rat bots ([SBFC](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_4G3_P154.pdf)). Mobula [V] tags a bundler cluster at "3+ wallets" with a common funder, buys in the same block, and similar sizes (example: 0.5 SOL each) ([Mobula docs](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers.md)). Bundled wallets hold an average of 36.5% of supply across 41k+ launches, per MELT/arXiv 2602.13480 as summarised by [Coinpaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/education/launchpad-red-flags/) (secondary).
- **Launch snipers.** [S] Academic definition: a buy within the first **1–5 blocks** (about 0.4–2 s) after creation, "well below human reaction time". Algorithm 2 uses K=5 ([arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08641)). 84% of sampled pump.fun projects were sniped ([SBFC](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_4G3_P154.pdf)). Mobula [V] uses "typically 0-3 blocks", plus a fresh wallet funded shortly before launch and high priority fees or bundles ([Mobula](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers.md)).
- **Deployer-funded snipers (insiders).** [S] Pine Analytics used a same-block snipe plus a *direct SOL transfer between deployer and sniper before launch* (one hop). That found 4,600+ wallets across 15,000+ launches in one month, profitable on 87% of snipes. More than 55% fully exited in under 1 minute, about 85% within 5 minutes, and over 11% in 15 s or less. Over 90% sold in 1–2 swaps. Pine says multi-hop funding (often 5–7 hops) evades its filter ([Pine, "Exit Liquidity Machines"](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)). Your memory notes that insiders are acceptable to copy. The point here is that **their PnL is earned in a window you can't reach**, so it shouldn't count toward a "smart" score [I].
- **Wash traders / bump bots / self-trading.** [S] A "flip" is two consecutive opposite-direction trades with *identical token quantity*. Bump score = flips / (|net position change| + 1). A wallet is a bump bot for a coin if the score is ≥ **50** ([arXiv 2601.08641 Alg. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08641)). The pump.fun "factories" paper defines **WT1** as the same address buying and selling the same amount on the same bonding curve inside one transaction, and **WT2** as the same address with the same amount at about the same price within 1 s, 5 s or 1 day. WT1 flags 8.26% of coins, and WT2 flags 12.38% at 1 s and 44% at 5 s. The authors estimate about 17% of trading transactions are wash trades, and about 50% for coins with more than 10k transactions ([arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246)). On DEX tokens generally, a maker whose same-day buy and sell volume match within **2%** is flagged as "zero-risk" wash trading. That rule caught 96% of suspected tokens ([arXiv 2507.01963 §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v2)). [I] Self-trades book many tiny "wins" net of nothing, so they inflate both trade counts and win rate.
- **Coordinated multi-wallet sellers.** [S] Factories paper: **DP1** is several senders transferring tokens to one dumper that sells in one transaction (median 7 senders). **DP2** is a dumper receiving from 2 or more senders within a window (5 s, 1 h or 24 h) and then selling at least that amount ([arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246)). [I] A dumper wallet shows huge realized PnL on tokens it never bought.
- **Multi-wallet operators / funding clusters.** [S] Factories paper clusters addresses by a shared first funder at up to **3 hops**. It drops Arkham-labelled exchanges and bridges to avoid false merges, and gains saturate at 3 hops ([arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246)). Helius [V] defines "funder" as the sender of a wallet's first incoming SOL ([Helius funded-by](https://www.helius.dev/docs/wallet-api/funded-by.md)). Funding graphs alone fail on CEX-funded wallets and deep hops. MadeOnSol [V] proposes *co-buy* clustering instead: first buys within **2 s** on at least **3** shared tokens (10+ counts as strong), average time delta under 0.5 s, and size difference under 0.01 SOL ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-linked-wallets-detection-co-buy-clustering)).
- **MEV / sandwich bots.** [S] Sandwich fingerprint: a front-run and a back-run by the same signer around a different victim signer, buying and then selling the same token, often in a Jito bundle ([Helius MEV report](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report) [V]). Wide sandwiches span several slots and need accounts, direction and balance changes linked ([Solscan](https://info.solscan.io/mev-sandwich-attacks-solana) [V]). One program (vpe…) did 1.55M sandwiches in 30 days, averaging 0.0425 SOL each with an 88.9% success rate ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report)). [I] Fingerprint for a leaderboard: hundreds of round trips a day, each held for seconds, tiny profit per trade, and win rate near 90%.
- **Arbitrage bots.** [S] Intra-chain arbitrage is *atomic*: both legs in one transaction, with profit in the same transaction. Jito counted 90M successful arbitrages averaging $1.58 each ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report)). [I] Fingerprint: the transaction starts and ends in the same token (SOL or USDC), touches two or more pools, and has a hold time of 0.
- **Volume bots / market makers.** [S] Bot setups use many fresh wallets funded from one source, with randomized size and timing. Heuristics: volume-to-liquidity above 20:1, fewer than 100 holders on a "high-volume" token, alternating buy/sell of identical amounts ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-volume-bots-explained) [V]). Wallet-level thresholds for MM vs trader: **not published** for Solana memecoins.
- **KOL wallets.** [S] The WWW '26 paper models a KOL who bundles at creation and dumps into copiers ("naïve bundle", "bundle bot", "gradual bundle"). Gradual bundles separate creation from buying across multi-layer funding and are "much harder to detect" ([arXiv 2601.08641 §3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08641)). Bloomberg reporting describes KOLs getting cheap early allocations in exchange for shilling ([CryptoSlate/Bloomberg](https://cryptoslate.com/solana-has-become-a-breeding-ground-for-pump-and-dump-memecoin-cabals-bloomberg/)). No rigorous on-chain study measuring KOL "dump on followers" was found.
- **Copy-traders of copy-traders, airdrop/farm wallets, exchange/aggregator/pool accounts.** No specific published thresholds found. [I] A copier shows a consistent positive lag (N slots) behind one leader on most entries. The co-buy method above with a 1–10 s window and a directional lag would find it. CEX hot wallets, LP vaults and aggregator PDAs should be removed with label sets (Arkham, Solscan). Odaily's study did this before scoring ([Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5197131)).

## 2. Statistical traps

**Takeaway:** On memecoins, PnL is dominated by a few trades and win rate is easy to fake. Use significance and concentration tests, not raw win rate.

- **PnL concentration.** [S] Among Odaily's top-100 wallets (GMGN/Cielo data [V-sourced]), the **top-3 trades made about 91% of total profit** ([Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5197131)). Its #1 wallet had 100% win rate on just 42 trades ([Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5197131)). That shows how a small sample inflates win rate [I].
- **Win rate vs drawdown.** [S] Odaily found win rate correlated 0.61 with its composite score and max-loss ratio −0.495. Trade count was essentially unrelated ([Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5197131)). [I] The score was built from those metrics, so this is circular, not predictive.
- **Bots inflate win rate.** [S] One practitioner guide says win rates above 70% on memecoins "almost always" mean insider or bot sniping (vendor heuristic, cited in [GMGN blog](https://gmgn.ai/blog/how-to-track-copy-solana-smart-money/) search snippet [V]; I couldn't fetch the page). Pine's snipers hit 87% ([Pine](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)).
- **Inability to copy (imitation penalty).** [S] The authors prove that a copier "strictly overpays on every replicated buy" (Theorem 2). Empirically, statistical models picked profitable leaders, but **copier returns stayed negative** after friction. Only their best model reached about +3% per coin ([arXiv 2601.08641 §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08641)).
- **Skill vs luck / multiple testing.** [S] If you rank thousands of wallets and pick the top, some look skilled by chance. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio corrects for the number of trials and for non-normal returns ([Bailey & López de Prado, SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). Fama–French find few funds beat chance net of costs ([JF 2010 PDF](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/bespeneckbo/default/AFA611-Eckbo%20web%20site/AFA611-S8C-FamaFrench-LuckvSkill-JF10.pdf)).
- **Base rates.** [S] In a Dune dashboard of about 304k Solana meme traders over 90 days, only about 6% were profitable ([Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/6-solana-meme-traders-profit-in-90-days/), secondary). [I] Given that, a "positive 30-day PnL" filter mostly selects survivors.
- **Wash trading corrupts token-level signals too.** [S] Wash-traded coins graduated at 2.0% vs 0.90% ([arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246)). Wash and comment bots raise trader participation by about 98% and 116% ([SBFC](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_4G3_P154.pdf)). Wallets that farm these flows look "smart" in hot markets [I].
- **Realized vs unrealized, regime change, recency.** Some public Dune rankings mix realized and unrealized PnL. For example, [Dune 5585656](https://dune.com/queries/5585656) ranks by "Total PnL (realized + unrealized)" per its search snippet. No memecoin-specific regime or recency studies found [I/not published].

## 3. Positive criteria and persistence evidence

**Takeaway:** The best-supported positive signal is a **significant per-token mean-return t-stat over many tokens, with bots and launch-block entries excluded**. Persistence exists in traditional speculator data, but only for a tiny top tail.

- **t-stat of per-token returns.** [S] Ding/Luo/Xu compute each single-wallet trader's t-stat across all coins traded (for traders with more than 2 and fewer than 1,000 coins, excluding algos). Winners are t > 2.65 and losers t < −2.65. More traders robustly lose than robustly win ([SBFC §4.3](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_4G3_P154.pdf)). Their copy-trading conditions require **t > 1.645**, return std < 1, purchase amount above the 25th percentile, mean return > 0 over recent windows (coins 1–5, 6–10 and 11–15 back), no bundle bot, and no "mechanical" candlestick ([arXiv 2601.08641 Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08641)).
- **Who the significant winners are.** [S] Among winners, 19.2% are creators and 16.0% are snipers, against 4.4%/3.3% for normal traders and 0.8%/1.0% for losers. Winners **avoid manipulated, crowded coins**, pick smaller earlier-stage projects, and are the dump initiators in more than 50% of pre-mature projects ([SBFC](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_4G3_P154.pdf)). [I] After you remove creators and snipers, the residual "skill" signal is selectivity (coin choice) and exit timing.
- **Persistence of behaviour.** [S] Winners' stay-or-quit decisions don't respond to recent performance. Losers react to recent losses and quit, and their learning "plateaus" ([SBFC](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_4G3_P154.pdf)). This supports your "quitter" tag as a loser marker.
- **Persistence of returns (traditional markets).** [S] Taiwan day traders ranked on year-y returns: the top 500 earned 61.3 (37.9 after fees) bps/day in year y+1, but less than 1% predictably earn net positive returns ([Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, JFM 2014](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)). On social-trading platforms, flows chase past winners within weeks ([Deng et al.](https://wiwi.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin-wiwi/cetar/TAF_Working_Paper_Series/TAF_WP_074_DengYangPelsterTan2023_rev.pdf)). **No published month-to-month persistence or alpha-decay study for Solana memecoin wallets was found.** White Whale Labs reportedly found 450–2,000-wallet launch clusters with no consistent profit advantage ([KuCoin summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/white-whale-labs-solana-meme-coin-trading-driven-by-cluster-wallets), secondary, not fetched).
- **Which features matter.** [S] In the WWW '26 ablation, removing the wallet-history agent hurt most: AUC −11.3% and copier gross return −9.4%. The coin agent (bot/candle checks) came next at −8.5% copier ([arXiv 2601.08641 Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08641)).
- **Profit factor, Sortino, drawdown, copyable hold time, edge after delay.** No memecoin-specific published thresholds. MadeOnSol [V] says a win rate above 55% on 30+ trades is "well above baseline" and above 65% on 50+ is "exceptional" ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-wallet-analyzer-win-rate-roi-verdict), search snippet).

## Suggested detection rules for GodsEye (all [I])

1. **Launch-window exclusion from scoring:** drop any trade whose entry is ≤5 slots after the token's creation slot, or in the creation slot. Keep copying such wallets if you like (per your insider policy), but compute the "smart" metrics only on trades entered after slot +5 or later. Better still, enter at your measured copy delay.
2. **Insider link:** flag a wallet if it is linked to the deployer within 3 funding hops (excluding CEX/bridge-labelled funders) on 2 or more tokens.
3. **Bot filter:** exclude a wallet if its median hold is under 15 s, *or* more than 50% of its round trips close within the same or next slot, *or* it has any atomic same-transaction buy+sell, *or* its per-token bump score is ≥50 on more than 10% of tokens, *or* more than 30% of its transactions carry a Jito tip together with more than 200 round trips a day.
4. **Wash/self-trade:** drop round trips with identical token quantity within 5 s. Recompute win rate without them.
5. **Cluster de-dup:** merge wallets whose first buys co-occur within 2 s on 10 or more tokens. Score the cluster once and cap the cluster's weight.
6. **Concentration guard:** require top-3 tokens to be under 60% of realized PnL, *and* positive PnL after removing the single best token.
7. **Significance, not win rate:** require 25 or more distinct tokens in 30 days and a per-token mean log-return t-stat above 2 (1.645 at minimum). Deflate for the number of wallets screened, for example by raising the bar until a shuffled-label null passes fewer than 5% of wallets.
8. **Copyability:** rank on *replayed copier* return at your real delay and size, net of fees (you already have the copy-fill replay). Require it to be positive on both halves of the window. Reject wallets whose median position size exceeds about 2% of pool liquidity at entry.
9. **Selectivity signal:** reward wallets whose coins mostly lack bundle, sniper or wash flags at entry. This matches what the winners in Ding/Luo/Xu do.
10. **Persistence check:** select on month t−1 and evaluate on month t, and keep only rules whose top decile beats the median out of sample. No public memecoin study shows this persists, so measure it yourself.
