# Wash trading and fake-volume signals for new Solana tokens, computable from a raw swap stream

Research date: 2026-10-27. About 25 tool calls. Source tags: [P] academic paper or preprint (peer-review status given where known), [V] vendor or marketing, [D] practitioner or independent forensic write-up, [N] news or secondary report, [U] could not verify or search-snippet only. "Inference" means my own reasoning, not a sourced claim; every threshold labelled "proposed" is mine unless a source is cited.
Not redone (already in sibling notes): Chen et al. rug taxonomy and lifecycle, MemeTrans bundle shares, Bitquery sniper/bundle census, Pine Analytics, SolRPDS, Kamat cohorts (withdrawn), Bubblemaps cluster thresholds, funder-clustering heuristics, Marino base rates. Where I re-use them I only add a new angle.

Headline findings for the developer:
1. There is no published, ground-truth-validated wash-trading detector for new Solana tokens. Every Solana paper (Meme Coin Factories, Mongardini and Mei, Wiedenmann thesis) reports counts under conservative rules and none reports precision/recall. The only error-rate-like statements are qualitative ("lower bound", "false positives grow with window").
2. The most Solana-specific and most useful measurement is Meme Coin Factories (arXiv 2609.10246, CCS'26): on pump.fun, a strict same-transaction round trip (WT1) touches 8.26% of coins and 1.32% of volume, but a loose 5-second matched round trip touches 44% of coins; wash trading is strongly associated with graduation (0.90% -> 9.64% for 500+ wash txs). So "volume" and even "migration" are both gameable.
3. The signals that survive across papers are the same few: same-wallet buy and sell of near-equal size within seconds to minutes (tolerance 1-2%), repeated cycles by the same account, a tiny set of accounts carrying nearly all volume with net flow near zero, common funding of the trading wallets, and metronome-like timing. Each has a published tolerance; none has a published false-positive rate.
4. Jupiter's Organic Score (blog dated 2026-03-26) is the one industry system that treats copy-trading and sniping platforms as non-organic. This matters directly for a smart-money feed (see Q4).

## Q1. Which academic and industry methods exist for detecting wash trading on DEXs and in memecoins, which signals do they use, and what accuracy do they report?

### Takeaway
Methods fall into three families: (a) distribution tests on trade sizes (Cong et al.), (b) position-netting plus graph-cycle detection (Victor and Weintraud, Wiedenmann, Chainalysis, Mongardini and Mei, Meme Coin Factories), and (c) activity/liquidity ratio screens (Solidus, Flip Research, vendors). None reports precision/recall against ground truth; the best honest statements of error are "lower bound" and "false positives rise as the time window grows".

### Cited Findings

#### Family A: size-distribution tests (academic, centralised exchanges)
- Cong, Li, Tang and Yang, "Crypto Wash Trading", Management Science 69(11), pp. 6427-6454, 2023 (DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2021.02709; NBER w30783 Dec 2022; arXiv 2108.10984 Aug 2021). They test 29 exchanges with three diagnostics: first-significant-digit (Benford-style) distribution, rounding of trade sizes, and tail distribution of trade sizes. Regulated exchanges look like normal financial markets; unregulated ones do not; estimated wash trading averaged over 70% of reported volume on unregulated exchanges. The public abstract pages give no test statistics, thresholds or per-exchange figures (those are in the online appendix I did not open). [P, peer-reviewed] — [NBER w30783](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30783), [arXiv 2108.10984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10984)
- Transfer to DEX swap streams is untested in anything I found: Cong et al. studied centralised-exchange trade prints, not on-chain swaps. [U on transfer]

#### Family B: netting and cycles (DEX and memecoin)
- Victor and Weintraud, "Detecting and Quantifying Wash Trading on Decentralized Cryptocurrency Exchanges", WWW '21 (arXiv 2102.07001; IDEX and EtherDelta, limit-order-book DEXs on Ethereum). Method, from the paper text: (1) candidate sets from iterative strongly-connected-component (SCC) counting per token trade graph, keeping SCCs seen at least 100 times (top 3% of SCCs on IDEX, top 1% on EtherDelta); (2) volume matching: within a time window a set of accounts is wash trading if each account's net position is zero within a margin of 1% of the mean trade volume; windows tested are 1 hour, 1 day and 1 week. Results: 31.54% of IDEX tokens and 42.12% of EtherDelta tokens were subject to at least one wash set; at least 10% of tokens on both had a wash share of at least 20% of volume; almost 10% of EtherDelta tokens were entirely wash traded (under 1% on IDEX); wash trading is most frequent at the start and end of a token's life; self-trades dominate EtherDelta and two-account structures are next; no branched structure with sub-cycles longer than 3. The authors call the result a lower bound; the search summary gives about $159M of wash volume [U on that figure]. No precision/recall. [P, peer-reviewed] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.07001)
- Chainalysis, 2025 Crypto Crime blog on manipulation (published 2025-01-29, corrected 2025-02-13), EVM only (Ethereum, BNB Chain, Base), 2024 data. Heuristic 1: an address makes a buy and a sell within 25 blocks, USD volume difference under 1%, and at least 3 such trades: about $704M, 23,436 addresses, 0.035% of Nov-2024 DEX volume. Heuristic 2: a controller funds at least 5 managed addresses whose first ETH/BNB deposit came through a token multi-sender, and buy/sell USD difference in a pool under 5%: about $1.87B, 0.046% of Nov-2024 DEX volume, called an upper bound. Combined $2.57B (may overlap). Pools with suspected wash trading were 0.2-0.3% of about 500,000 monthly active pools. Chainalysis states it "tracks patterns of behavior and not intent". No precision/recall. [V, but primary and detailed] — [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-market-manipulation-wash-trading-pump-and-dump-2025/)
- The same Chainalysis page also gives the pump-and-dump count I could not verify before: 2,063,519 tokens launched in 2024, 74,037 suspected pump-and-dump tokens (3.59%), 74,312 DEX pools in suspected schemes, about 94% rugged by the pool creator. This resolves the earlier gap in `rug_taxonomy_timing.md` (EVM chains, not Solana). — same source
- Mongardini and Mei, "A Midsummer Meme's Dream: Investigating Market Manipulations in the Meme Coin Ecosystem" (arXiv 2507.01963, v1 July 2025, revised Jan 2026; 34,988 tokens on Ethereum, BNB Chain, Solana, Base). Two-stage rule. Stage 1: day with volume up more than 500% versus the previous day while absolute price change is under 5% (3,264 candidate days across 377 tokens). Stage 2 on maker-level Dune data: (i) "zero-risk position": makers bought and sold almost identical amounts of the same token on the same day within a 2% tolerance (958 events, 1,777 makers, 271 tokens); (ii) "circular volume": at least 99% of the day's volume came from makers who both bought and sold that day (219 events, 670 makers); (iii) the same makers present on every flagged day (37 events, 55 makers, 14 tokens). Combined: 1,014 events and 282 tokens (74.8% of the 377 suspected). Solana accounts for 181 of the 282 tokens, and Solana tokens were targeted a median 62 days after creation versus at least 101 days elsewhere. The authors report no precision/recall or ground truth, call thresholds conservative and the totals a lower bound, and justify 500%/5%/2% qualitatively without sensitivity analysis. Pump-and-dump rule: RSI above 80 peak, price up more than 50% and volume up more than 500% within 24 h, then a drop above 30% with volume under half of pump-phase volume; 91 operations on 60 tokens, 54 on Solana. [P, preprint] — [arXiv 2507.01963v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v1)
- Meme Coin Factories (arXiv 2609.10246, submitted 2026-09-09, accepted CCS'26; pump.fun, 1% sample and 5-day sample). Two heuristics. WT1 (conservative): one address buys and sells the same amount against the same bonding curve inside a single transaction (excludes MEV/arbitrage, only catches trades outside the pump.fun interface). WT2 (broad): same address buys and sells approximately the same amount at approximately the same price within a window, tolerance 1.25% per trade plus 15,000 lamports for fees (appendix D). Results in the 1% sample: WT1 = 2,221,734 wash transactions on 8.26% of coins; WT2 with 1 s window = 12.38% of coins (appendix quotes 10.94% for a sample; the two figures are not reconciled in what I read), 5 s window = 44.05% of coins, 1 day window = 88.5% of coins; the authors say longer windows add few extra instances and that WT2 "can produce false positives". Lower bound across both samples about 4 million wash transactions, "17% of all trading transactions". WT1 volume is only 66,636 SOL = 1.32% of volume (median 1.00% per coin), which the authors read as manipulators favouring transaction count over volume because pump.fun fees scale with volume. Wash-trade ratio rises with coin activity: 0.41% of txs for coins with 1-10 txs, 2.69% (11-100), 13.91% (101-1,000), 21.65% (1,001-10,000), 50.31% (more than 10,000). They searched for a buy -> transfer to second address -> sell pattern and found none. No precision/recall. [P, accepted CCS'26] — [arXiv 2609.10246v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1)
- Wiedenmann, TUM master's thesis (submitted 2025-06-10): "Analysis of Market Manipulation on the Solana Blockchain with a Focus on Wash Trading" (AMM tokens, curated set of high-risk tokens, not a market census). Detection: per token, SCC candidate sets then cycle search over a transaction graph with a 1% amount tolerance (copied from Victor and Weintraud), a sequence window and a depth limit. Three window configurations: very strict (no intervening trades, at most 3 minutes), strict (up to 5 intervening trades, 18 minutes), lenient (up to 20 intervening trades, 1 hour); all require a net loss for the participants, and an account only counts if it participates in multiple wash cycles. Arbitrage is deliberately excluded because it cannot be separated from losing wash trades. Findings: strict/lenient configurations roughly double or triple the hourly wash share on the three focus tokens; two accounts reached about 5,000 SOL each and the remainder sat near baseline; major wash accounts trade almost only wash cycles ("pure manipulators") while larger tokens show "opportunistic manipulators" mixing wash cycles with real trades; wash intervals clustered in a narrow band near 10^2 seconds with intervals above 10^4 s rare; tokens with a high wash share tend to have few liquidity providers; the same wallets appeared across several small tokens with synchronised volume spikes (on/off pattern); for the heavily traded token "gorecats" the flagged cycles lacked the timing signature and the author concedes some may be false positives from failed MEV. Limitations: pump.fun swap-amount handling is unresolved because it moves native SOL not WSOL; multi-token transactions unresolved. Not peer reviewed. [P, thesis] — [TUM thesis PDF](https://www.cs.cit.tum.de/fileadmin/w00cfj/sebis/thesis/250610_Wiedenmann_Thesis.pdf)
- Chen et al. (arXiv 2603.24625) name wash trading as one of three promotion tactics (with multi-account holdings to fake low top-holder concentration; Solana fees average about 0.00025 USD per transaction) but define no detector and report no wash counts, volume share, thresholds or accuracy. Its 0.26% false-positive rate belongs to the rug label, not to wash trading. [P] — [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625)

#### Family C: activity-to-liquidity screens (vendor and practitioner)
- Solidus Labs wash-trading report (page date shows March 2025, body text says part two of a 2023 series with data to 2023-08-31; dates inconsistent). Ethereum CPMM pools; pool flagged when first LP supplies more than 90% of both sides throughout, liquidity never locked, more than 5 and fewer than 10,000 swaps, and either malicious token code or a trades-to-traders ratio of at least 2. Result: at least $2B of LP-driven wash volume since Sept 2020 across over 20,000 tokens; 67% of almost 30,000 pools in the sample and 13% of those pools' volume; the authors say the true figure is probably an order of magnitude higher. No Solana numbers. [V] — [Solidus](https://soliduslabs.com/reports/crypto-wash-trading)
- Flip Research via Cointelegraph (2024-07-30): Solana averaged 217 transactions per user on 2024-07-26 versus under 3 on Ethereum; one Raydium project had $10.8M of volume from $48 of liquidity across 2,845 unique wallets generating $28,000 in fees; a pool with $3 liquidity and over $5M 24 h volume; over 50 rug-pull projects with $200M volume and $500,000 fees in 24 h. The article gives no bot percentage. [N/V] — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bots-pushing-solana-over-ethereum-research)
- Bitquery open-source wash detector (docs, undated): rule labels used to train an XGBoost model: self-trade (buyer = seller, exact match), repeated pair (same buyer/seller pair more than 5 times), loops (two-trade reciprocal A->B, B->A only, no time or size tolerance), spoofing (buy-side and sell-side USD price differ by more than 2.0). The docs publish no features, accuracy, precision or recall. [V] — [Bitquery docs](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/usecases/wash-trading-detector/prepare-data/rules/)
- Bitquery blog "solana volume numbers are a lie" (data pulled 2026-04-27/28; 12 h of Solana DEX data): token OpenLie had $532,461 volume from 233 wallets and 40,523 trades; 96% of wallets both bought and sold; top-10 wash wallets round-trip ratio 0.95-1.00; average 174 trades per wallet at about $13.14; trade sizes arrived in "tight $13 increments"; funding trace: the dominant first-inbound-SOL sender matched 30 of 30 wallets flagged independently, having seeded at least 200 bots with 0.5 SOL each in a 52-second burst (21 April 2026). The composite wash score's weights are not published. Attribution is statistical, from a data vendor with a commercial interest. [V/D] — [Bitquery blog](https://bitquery.io/blog/solana-volume-numbers-are-a-lie)
- Jupiter, "What is Organic Score" (developer blog, 2026-03-26): 0-100 score from organic volume, organic holders, organic traders, organic buyers, normalised against the rest of the ecosystem; wallets are classified organic or not once per wallet, non-organic being those on "known accounts, pipelines, or automated platforms (sniping, copy-trading, automated execution) and identifiable on-chain patterns", with everything not caught treated as organic ("conservative"); weights, formula and bot rules unpublished; fresh tokens give volatile scores; going from 10 to 100 organic buyers matters far more than 10,000 to 10,100. Sample 24 h values: SOL score 98.9 with $36.6M organic buy volume of $7.36B total buys (0.5%); TRUMP score 85.3 with 52 organic net buyers out of 2,947 traders; ASPEN score 45.1 with $1.0K organic of $1.61M buy volume. [V] — [Jupiter dev blog](https://developers.jup.ag/blog/what-is-organic-score)
- MadeOnSol "Solana Volume Bots Explained" (2026, undated): volume-to-liquidity above 20:1 "red flag" (organic roughly 1:1 to 10:1), under 100 holders on a high-volume token a warning (with $1M daily volume and 50 holders "almost certainly" wash traded), price pinned in a 1-2% range for hours, regular intervals (30 s, 1 min), identical amounts, same wallets on both sides, funding of many wallets from one parent in a short window, flat 24 h volume. No methodology; the site sells its own tools and uses affiliate links. [V, conflicted] — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-volume-bots-explained)

#### Accuracy summary (what is actually reported)
- Cong et al.: none in public abstract. Victor and Weintraud: none (lower bound). Chainalysis: none (patterns not intent; heuristic 2 an upper bound). Mongardini and Mei: none. Meme Coin Factories: none; qualitative false-positive warning for WT2. Wiedenmann: none; qualitative false-positive cases (failed MEV, gorecats). Bitquery: none. Jupiter: none; says "conservative". Chen: 0.26% FP but for rugs, not wash.
- Large disagreement on prevalence is definitional: Cong >70% of reported volume (unregulated CEXs), Solidus 13% of volume in a filtered sample of LP-controlled pools, Chainalysis 0.035-0.046% of all DEX volume (pool-level heuristics on a whole market), Meme Coin Factories 1.32% of pump.fun volume but 17% of transactions. These are not comparable measures. — sources above

### Inferences
- A builder should treat every published cutoff as an unvalidated starting prior; the realistic way to get an error rate is to label a few hundred tokens by hand and score each signal against that, since no public ground-truth set exists (the Bitquery rules and Meme Coin Factories WT1 could serve as weak labels, but WT1 is by construction a high-precision low-recall single-transaction pattern).
- The fact that WT1 (same-tx, 1.32% of volume) and WT2-5s (44% of coins) differ by a factor of about 5 in coin prevalence shows the window parameter, not the concept, determines the headline number. Pick windows deliberately and log several (seconds, minutes, an hour).
- Cong's Benford and size-rounding diagnostics are weak candidates for a single new token: pump.fun and bot users choose round SOL amounts (0.1, 0.5, 1 SOL) so rounding is normal behaviour there, and a chi-square first-digit test needs roughly 109 swaps just to expect 5 counts in the rarest digit (5 / log10(1+1/9) = 109; my arithmetic). Use size-distribution tests only as a token-level aggregate with at least a few hundred swaps, never as a per-wallet signal.
- On a pool or curve venue every swap's counterparty is the pool, so Victor-style multi-wallet cycles collapse to per-wallet netting plus group-level netting after clustering (Wiedenmann makes the same point: in AMMs the counterparty is not critical). The cheap implementable version is: per wallet (and per funder cluster) net position change over the window near zero while gross volume is large.

### Gaps
- No ground-truth-labelled wash-trading dataset for Solana, no precision/recall for any detector above.
- Cong et al.'s online appendix (test statistics, thresholds) not opened; Victor and Weintraud's $159M figure only from a search summary; Falk, Tsoulalas and Zhang (2023, direct vs indirect estimation of wash trading) appeared only as a citation in a search result and was not read.
- MELT (follow-on dataset to MemeTrans with "wash trade" record types) appeared in a search summary only; I did not locate it. [U]
- Meme Coin Factories' discussion section and its exact WT2 false-positive discussion were not in the pages I read; the 1-second figure differs between body (12.38%) and appendix (10.94%).
- Solidus's Solana-specific wash numbers: its Solana report (May 2025) has an unfinished wash-trading section per the sibling note; nothing new found.
- Chainalysis report is EVM only; I found no Solana-specific Chainalysis wash-trading figures.

## Q2. Which signals are computable from a swap stream, with definition, threshold or distribution, evidence and evasion?

### Takeaway
Fourteen signals are listed below. Only a handful have a published number from Solana work: round-trip tolerance (1%, 1.25%+fees, 2%), time window (seconds to 1 hour), swaps per wallet (Solidus ratio of at least 2; Bitquery 174 on a pure wash token), funding concentration (Bitquery 30 of 30; Chainalysis 5+ managed addresses), timing regularity (Wiedenmann ~10^2 s band), and volume-to-liquidity (vendor 20:1). Everything else is a proposed threshold that must be calibrated on your own data. The table separates what is sourced from what is proposed.

### Inferences
Proposed definitions and starting thresholds (mine, to be calibrated against labelled tokens; each should be logged as a raw number before any gate is applied). "Real swap" means after removing known protocol/agent flow (Mayhem agent, BOOST buys), MEV/arbitrage-signature transactions (same-tx multi-pool, Jito-tipped atomic round trips) and the launch bundle.

1. Swaps per wallet = swaps / unique signer wallets, report median and p90 per wallet and the share of swaps from wallets with at least 10 swaps. Suggest flagging when more than 50% of swaps come from wallets with 10+ swaps in a token under 1 hour old, or any wallet over about 50 swaps in the first hour. Organic memecoin crowds are mostly one-to-three-swap wallets plus a few active traders; the Solidus pool rule (ratio at least 2) is on mature ETH pools and would flag many legitimate Solana tokens if applied unchanged.
2. Volume concentration: compute top-1/3/5 shares of gross volume (buy+sell) and of unique-pair volume, excluding pool/curve/program accounts. Gross-volume top-3 above about 50% on a token with more than 100 swaps is suspicious; top-1 above 30% needs explanation (it may be a market maker, a creator, or a sniper). Also report Herfindahl-based effective number of traders (inverse HHI): 20 equal wallets give 20; one wallet at 70% with 30 minor ones gives about 2. No published cutoff exists.
3. Fresh/co-funded share: share of gross volume by wallets whose first funding transfer came within N hours before their first swap (N = 1, 6, 24) and by wallets sharing a funder or funder-of-funder. A cluster of 5+ wallets with one funder (Chainalysis) trading the same token and each having first funding within a short burst (Bitquery: 200 in 52 s) is high confidence; unique one-time dust funders (0.002 SOL) are invisible to this signal, so absence is not exculpatory. Exclude CEX hot wallets and bot-platform fee wallets from "common funder" using fan-out (sibling note).
4. Round trip: a buy followed by a sell (same wallet, same token) with token amount difference at most 1.25% plus fee allowance, within 5 s (strict, single-pass bot), 3 min (Wiedenmann very strict) and 60 min (lenient). Use the 1-second and 5-second windows only as hard evidence when repeated at least 3 times by the same wallet (Chainalysis requires 3; Wiedenmann requires multiple cycles); a lone round trip is normal for a scalper or an MEV bot. Output: round-trip volume share = 2 x sum of matched volume / gross volume, and the number of wallets with at least 3 matched cycles.
5. Circular flows: on AMM/curve venues replace graph cycles with (a) per-wallet net position near zero over the window and (b) per-cluster net position near zero when the cluster has at least 2 wallets (Victor-style netting on cluster members). Direct A->B token or SOL transfers between trading wallets, or a wallet sweeping to a common destination, are exact links; Bitquery's reciprocal pair count (more than 5) is a cheap proxy for LOB/Phoenix-type venues but not for AMMs.
6. Near-identical sizes: per wallet, coefficient of variation of swap size over at least 10 swaps below about 0.05, or modal-size share above 50%; token-level, top-1 size bin share. Exclude fixed-size copy-trade bots and DCA (they also have constant sizes, so size alone is not wash evidence).
7. Constant timing: per wallet, coefficient of variation of inter-swap intervals below about 0.3 with at least 10 swaps, or more than 60% of intervals inside +-10% of the modal interval; also check synchronised bursts across wallets (identical-second bursts). Wiedenmann's data (wash intervals clustered near 100 s) suggests intervals in the tens of seconds to few minutes; sub-slot-time intervals are more likely MEV.
8. Shared fee payer / bundle / tip: exact link when several wallets' swaps use the same fee payer or co-signer; weaker when they share a Jito bundle or tip. Tip account identity carries almost no information (clients pick randomly; sibling note), and heavy Jito tipping marks MEV (arXiv 2607.28424), so use only as a clustering edge, not as a wash flag.
9. Gross/net: define gross = sum of absolute swap value; net = absolute value of (buys - sells). Report gross/net at token level and "churn" = gross volume / peak SOL in curve or pool reserve. For a bonding curve this is cleanly measurable: an organic early curve is mostly net buying (reserve rises with cumulative buys), so a token with high cumulative gross but little reserve gained has recycled volume. Suggested starting flags: gross/net above about 10 over the first hour while more than 50% of gross comes from wallets with 3+ matched cycles; Mongardini's stricter condition (at least 99% of volume from two-sided makers) is a hard-evidence tier.
10. Price-impact-adjusted volume: effective-volume = gross volume / cumulative absolute price move (or volume per unit of price change); also the Mongardini daily screen (volume +500%, |price| <5%) at hour granularity for tokens older than a day. On a constant-product curve or pool the price impact of each swap is a deterministic function of reserves, so a round trip returns price to roughly where it started (less fees): expect a flat-with-large-volume path, whereas organic one-sided buying moves price monotonically. Vendor 20:1 volume/liquidity ratio is unvalidated, and early-curve tokens can legitimately turn over their tiny reserves many times.
11. Token-age expectation: no published curve exists. Build an empirical reference: for each age bucket (0-5 min, 5-30 min, 30 min-6 h, 6-24 h) take the distribution (p50/p90) of swaps, unique wallets, swaps per wallet and round-trip share across all tokens in your own stream and score the candidate token as a percentile. Mongardini's median-62-day Solana timing is for tokens with at least some history and does not describe minute-scale new launches; Victor's start/end peaks suggest checking dormant-then-burst patterns on older tokens.
12. Known-bot share: share of gross volume from wallets on a bot list (Trojan, Axiom, Photon, BonkBot fee-path wallets; Mayhem agent; BOOST program). The Zenodo replication package of arXiv 2607.28424 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21359451) contains 200 baseline Trojan and SolanaMevBot addresses and 200 validation Axiom and SolanaMevBot addresses and could seed a list; I have not opened it. Remember Jupiter counts wallets not caught as organic, so any list under-counts.
13. Repeat wallets across dead tokens: for each wallet, number of distinct tokens in which it has at least 3 matched round-trip cycles or is in the top-3 volume share, within 7/30 days; store as a persistent "farm operator" flag. Persistence across tokens is the strongest published pattern (Wiedenmann, Mongardini, Chen, MCF); a wallet appearing as top volume in 5+ tokens that each died within a day is a candidate.
14. Few wallets that never hold: share of gross volume from wallets whose closing position is less than 5% of peak holding and whose net SOL flow is within +-5% of gross; and "organic buyer" count = distinct buyers who hold a positive net token position at the end of the window and are not in a flagged cluster. Jupiter's own organic-buyer concept (and its observation that SOL had 12,944 organic net buyers vs 812,709 traders) suggests the organic fraction is naturally small even on healthy tokens, so use it as a ranking, not a binary.

Cross-signal rules:
- Single-signal flags are noisy because arbitrage/MEV looks like round trips, DCA and copy-bots look like constant size/timing, and launch bundles look like common funding. Wiedenmann's checks (several cycles per account, net loss, arbitrage excluded) are the best published way to cut that noise: require repetition and net loss.
- Tier the output: Tier 1 (hard evidence) = repeated near-exact round trips within seconds by funder-linked wallets; Tier 2 = high gross/net plus high concentration with unlinked wallets; Tier 3 = a single soft signal. Only Tier 1-2 should discount a token's flow.

### Gaps
- No published false-positive/false-negative rate for any single signal on Solana; no published size or timing distributions for organic tokens by age; no published Solana top-1/3/5 share cutoff for wash trading.
- No source measured wash trading specifically through shared fee payer, Jito bundle id or tip patterns.
- Whether swap size tolerance should include pump.fun's variable fee (creator fee changes, Dynamic Fees) is not addressed in any source; MCF used 1.25% + 15,000 lamports for the curve.
- MadeOnSol's 20:1, <100 holders and 1-2% band rules are uncited vendor heuristics.
- How Jupiter's classification is built (VRFD) was not opened.

## Q3. What volume-inflation services and farms exist on Solana, what do they cost, how do they evade, and are there public wallet lists or heuristics?

### Takeaway
Volume bots are sold as Telegram-controlled services that run many small funded wallets; the only independently documented operator behaviour is Bitquery's April 2026 trace (a seeding burst of 200 wallets at 0.5 SOL, long laundering chains, aged funder, or dust from unique senders). I found no independent price list; the only cost evidence is fee arithmetic, and no public list of volume-farm wallets. Believe/DBC/Meteora farms and airdrop farming specifically: nothing verifiable found.

### Cited Findings
- Seller side: sellers pitch fresh-wallet bots with randomised size, timing and frequency, controlled via Telegram, to make tokens look active on scanners and trending lists; one vendor page says campaigns run roughly $10k to $1M+ in daily volume (BoostLegends, via gts.ai search snippet) with no tier prices. [V] — [gts.ai BoostLegends listing](https://gts.ai/defi/boostlegends-volume-bot) [U, not opened; page returned error on a sibling URL]

- Meme Coin Factories coded market-manipulation-as-a-service (MMaaS) websites and GitHub repositories without running or buying them: website services all offered low-latency inclusion and some offered creating multiple addresses for bundling; the repository codebook includes anti-detection (proxies, CAPTCHA solvers), fresh wallets, wallet mixing and comment bots (10 repositories showed anti-detection, but tied to account creation and commenting, not trading). No pricing reported. Because bundling and multi-address creation are standard features, the authors argue MMaaS lowers the cost of cluster behaviour. - Cost evidence: MadeOnSol says about $0.00025 per transaction and pool fees of 0.25-1% per swap, and gives "$250-$1,000 per day in pool fees for $100K of fake volume" (unsourced, and fee % of notional gives that per $100K of volume regardless of per-day). [V] — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-volume-bots-explained). Chen gives the same per-transaction fee (about 0.00025 USD). [P] — [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625). ### Inferences
- Fee floor for fake volume (my arithmetic, assuming the wash trader pays all fees and ignores the tx fee): every $1 of swap volume costs the venue fee rate, so $1M of volume costs about $10,000 on the pump.fun curve at 100 bps and about $2,500 on PumpSwap at 25 bps before creator fee; a round trip of $X counts as $2X of volume at twice the fee on $X, so fee cost per dollar of reported volume is the same. If the operator is the creator or LP and recaptures part of the fees (creator fee share, LP fee), the net cost falls, possibly below these figures. Wash volume is therefore not free but cheap relative to the leaderboard/graduation value MCF observed (graduation odds rising to 9.64% for 500+ wash txs).
- The Bitquery pattern implies detection by common funder works only for the crudest farms; the dust-from-unique-sender pattern requires behavioural clustering (same size, same timing, same token set, first-funding amount equal) rather than funder identity.
- "Dead-token farm operators" are best found from your own data by the persistence signal (Q2 #13): the same wallet set producing matched round trips in many tokens that die within a day. No public list can be assumed.

### Gaps
- No independent price per $ of fake volume; no Telegram bot price sheets verified.
- No evidence found on Believe, Meteora DBC launch farms or airdrop-farming wash volume; one commercial page mentions a "Solana volume bot for Meteora and DBC" framed as testing workflows (programminginsider.com via search; not opened). [U]
- No public wallet lists; Bubblemaps/GMGN/Axiom tags for "volume bots" are not documented.
- No data on how much of Solana DEX volume is wash: Jupiter shows only that organic buy volume is a small fraction of buy volume for large tokens (0.5% for SOL, 0.7% for TRUMP), which measures non-bot-platform share, not wash.

## Q4. How should a "smart money" signal be treated when the only buyers are tracked smart wallets, copy-bot loops, or wallets that copy each other?

### Takeaway
Several smart wallets buying the same token is evidence of independent demand only to the extent the wallets are independent of each other and of the token's creator. Copy-trading and bot platforms are explicitly treated as non-organic by Jupiter, and Luo et al. model manipulators who exploit copy traders. I found no empirical study of smart-wallet herding, so the independence rules below are proposed.

### Cited Findings
- Jupiter Organic Score treats wallets reaching trades through copy-trading, sniping and automated execution platforms as non-organic; wallets not caught count as organic; this is relative scoring. [V] — [Jupiter dev blog](https://developers.jup.ag/blog/what-is-organic-score)
- Luo et al. (arXiv 2601.08641, WWW '26; about 6,000 meme coin projects): a sniper's early buy "attracts copier wallets, which replicate the observed buying activity"; bundle wallets appear independent so copiers do not link them to the creator; manipulators gain from "exit liquidity from copy traders"; the adversary-versus-copier game has adversary profit equal to copier loss; bump bots repeatedly buy and sell identical amounts (less frequent than comment bots "likely due to their higher operational cost"); attention-manipulation bots steer copy traders to targeted coins; smart-money trades average +14% but estimated copier return is +3% per trade after frictions (price impact), and statistic-driven models do not give positive copier profit. Bundle bots in about a quarter of projects and sniper bots in the majority. [P, WWW '26] — [arXiv 2601.08641v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- Marino et al. (sibling note): conditioning on "successful traders" buying gives only modest uplift above baseline graduation and stays below breakeven, called a "double-edged signal". [P] — [arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14860)
- Wallet-linking primitives for independence tests and their weights are in the sibling note (`wallet_clustering.md`): first funder, direct transfers, same fee payer or signer, co-buy across multiple tokens, CEX-funded ring pattern, and the advice to separate exact links from probabilistic ones. — sibling note
- A general guide survey notes not every "smart money" wallet is smart; some are bots or wash traders (affiliate-style source). [V] — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-bots)
- I searched for evidence of a self-reinforcing copy loop (copy flow moving price and attracting more copiers) and for work on herding among smart wallets; the results returned vendor copy-trading guides and Luo et al. only. [U]

### Inferences
- Count independent evidence, not wallets. Proposed procedure: build a graph over the wallets that bought the token in the window, with edges for (a) exact control or transfer links (fee payer, direct SOL/token transfer, shared sweep), (b) same funder or funder-of-funder, (c) follower relation: wallet B bought the same token within a short lag (say under 30-60 s, tuned to copy-bot latency) after wallet A, at similar size ratio, and B has done this to A on at least 3 other tokens, and (d) shared bot platform/relay path. Collapse each connected component to one vote. Report independent smart voters = number of components, and effective number of buyers = inverse HHI of component-level volume. Proposed use: treat 1-2 independent components as "one opinion" regardless of how many wallets, and require independence before counting a smart wallet as confirmation for another.
- A follower detected under (c) is not wrong money, it is a repetition of the leader's signal; counting it doubles the vote. On the other side, a leader who is itself followed by many copy bots creates demand that disappears when the leader sells, a reflexive pattern consistent with Luo's exit-liquidity model (inference; not measured).
- "Only smart wallets bought" is ambiguous: it can mean real information (several unlinked skilled wallets) or a closed loop (one operator's wallets mutually reinforced, plus copiers). Distinguish by (i) no overlap in funder or fee-payer graph, (ii) different lag structure (not constant lag behind one wallet), (iii) presence of non-smart, non-bot buyers who are not followers (organic buyers in Jupiter's sense) alongside them.
- Raw smart-wallet counts should never be summed with organic-buyer counts: the former are heavily selected and correlated, the latter are the evidence of broad demand. Keep them as separate fields.
- A cheap sanity metric: share of the token's gross buy volume from wallets that are neither smart-listed, bot-platform, nor in a flagged cluster. If that share is near zero after the first minutes, treat the "demand" as closed-loop (proposed).

### Gaps
- No empirical study of smart-wallet independence, mutual copying or herding on Solana; no measured follower-lag distribution for copy bots; no study of how copy flow changes price (Luo et al. attribute copier shortfall to price impact but I only read summary statements).
- Luo et al.'s copier delay parameters and the effect of excluding bundle-flagged tokens (sibling gap) remain unread.
- No source on how GMGN/Axiom copy-trade infrastructure shows up in fee-payer or route fingerprints.

## Q5. Minimum evidence: how many independent traders over what window before a token's flow is credible, and what statistical care is needed with small samples?

### Takeaway
No source publishes a minimum sample size for judging a token's flow as organic. The published detectors use days, hundreds of transactions and repeated cycles; a new token seen over minutes has far fewer data points, so evidence should be expressed as an upper bound on the manipulated share (which shrinks slowly with the number of independent buyers) and as a monotone, saturating score, as Jupiter does.

### Cited Findings
- Jupiter says the Organic Score has diminishing returns (10 to 100 organic buyers matters far more than 10,000 to 10,100), that fresh tokens have volatile scores because they lack history, and that statistics may be manipulated by sophisticated traders. — [Jupiter dev blog](https://developers.jup.ag/blog/what-is-organic-score)
- Published detectors require repetition: Chainalysis at least 3 matched trades per address; Wiedenmann multiple wash cycles per account and strictness levels; Victor and Weintraud SCC occurrences at least 100 times; Mongardini and Mei the same makers on every flagged day. These conditions are designed to avoid labelling a coincidence as wash trading. — sources in Q1
- MCF: the wash share of transactions rises steeply with coin activity (0.41% for 1-10 txs vs 50.31% for more than 10,000), so activity level must be conditioned on; coins with 1-10 transactions are almost never wash traded by WT2 while very busy ones often are. — [arXiv 2609.10246v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1)
- Base rates: Marino (sibling note): 0.63% of 655,770 pump.fun tokens graduate (2.55% of those with at least 30 swaps) and 92.22% of tokens with at least 30 swaps show a dump; Chen: rug tokens median 9 holders. The large majority of tokens never reach the activity level at which published detectors operate. — sibling notes
- Mongardini and Mei and Wiedenmann caution about extreme-case bias and lower bounds; Wiedenmann's false-positive example (gorecats) arises in heavily traded tokens, so more data does not remove false positives. — sources in Q1

### Inferences
Statistical considerations (my arithmetic; no source applies them to wash trading):
- Upper bound on the share of operator-controlled buyers if none of n sampled buyers is linked: the exact 95% bound is 1 - 0.05^(1/n): n=5: 45%; n=10: 26%; n=15: 18%; n=20: 14%; n=30: 9.5%; n=50: 5.8%; n=100: 3.0% (rule of three, 3/n, is a close approximation from n about 30). "No links found" is weak evidence because linking recall is poor (Q2 #3, evasion), so these bounds are best case.
- Wilson 95% interval for an observed flagged share: 2 flagged of 10 buyers = 5.7% to 51%; 3 of 30 = 3.5% to 25.6%; 10 of 30 = 19.2% to 51.2%. With fewer than 30 independent buyers the interval is too wide to separate "5% manipulated" from "40% manipulated".
- Suggested minimum before a token counts as having independent demand (proposed, to be calibrated): at least about 30 distinct buyers and 15 distinct non-flagged, non-bot, non-follower buyers who still hold after the window; observed over at least 15-30 minutes and at least two separate time bursts (to avoid a single bundle burst). Reasoning: below about 30 buyers the 95% bound on controlled share stays above 10%, and a one-slot burst can be a single bundle.
- Use effective buyers (inverse HHI of component-level volume) instead of raw counts; 20 equal buyers give 20 but one wallet with 70% of volume and 30 minor ones gives about 2.
- Keep scores monotone and saturating in independent-buyer count (Jupiter) and always display the sample size alongside any ratio. For ratio signals (round-trip share, top-3 share) require a minimum denominator (e.g. at least 30 swaps) before applying a threshold; below it output "insufficient evidence" instead of a pass.
- Multiple-testing: running 14 signals over thousands of tokens will yield many spurious flags at any individual cutoff; calibrate cutoffs as percentiles within an age bucket (Q2 #11) and combine signals by tier rather than OR-ing them.
- Benford-style tests need hundreds of swaps (about 109 just for expected cell counts of 5) and are invalid when sizes are user-chosen round numbers; do not apply them to minute-old tokens.

### Gaps
- No published minimum-evidence rule, no calibration of any "N independent buyers" gate against later outcomes, and no data for how long after launch a Solana token's flow becomes distinguishable from a manipulated one.
- My sample-size numbers are binomial arithmetic under random sampling of buyers; they assume the buyers can be independently classified, which the evasion evidence in Q2/Q3 contradicts.
- The calibration needs your own labelled outcomes; the repository's audit/replay data is the natural place (not inspected here).
