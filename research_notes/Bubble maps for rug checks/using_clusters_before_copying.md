# Using bubble-map / cluster information between a leader's buy and our own, and while holding (as of 2026-10-27)

Scope and conventions. This note does not repeat what is already in `Rug detection for copy trading/` (pre_buy_checks.md, post_buy_monitoring_calibration.md, internal_audit.md) or `Memecoin wallet tracking methods/` (copy_edge_and_adversaries.md, wallet_clustering.md, realtime_infrastructure.md); where I lean on them I say "earlier note". Owner's standing decision respected throughout: insider-pattern wallets stay copyable; every cluster signal below is a per-token sizing or exit input, never a wallet verdict.

Source tags: **[Vdoc]** vendor or bot official documentation; **[Vblog]** vendor blog, press release or guide (marketing-adjacent); **[Prac]** practitioner opinion or case study; **[Acad]** arXiv / peer-reviewed measurement; **[Int]** GodsEye's own data, read on 2026-10-27 (local `data/copycow.db`, `npm run replay`); **(snippet only)** = seen only in a search summary, page not opened. Everything under **Inferences** is my reasoning or arithmetic (design suggestion), not a sourced claim. I fetched ~45 pages; many bot docs pages state no defaults at all, and I say so rather than fill in.

Headline results (details below):
1. No bot's documented copy-trading settings include a bundle / cluster / insider veto. Those filters exist only as user-set range filters in discovery screens (Axiom Pulse, Trojan Explorer), with no shipped defaults. The only documented cluster-ish copy-bot exit is GMGN's "Dev Sell". So there is no industry default to copy; the 25/15/50/20 cutoffs are as defensible as anything published.
2. Internal data changes the timing framing: the 10 copied leaders' first buy of a token comes a median 34 s after the earliest pool GeckoTerminal knows (p25 21 s, p75 64 s), and the leader's own median hold is about 10 s. If that pool is the PumpSwap pool, the bonding-curve phase and its bundle are already complete when we decide (to verify, see Q1 Gaps).
3. A flat share-of-supply cutoff is the wrong unit: the same cluster share costs a very different drawdown depending on the pool's base-token reserve, which shrinks as the curve fills. A stressed-exit cutoff gives roughly 5% of supply for a -35% price shock on a freshly migrated PumpSwap pool, versus about 22% of supply when only 5 SOL has been raised on the curve (my arithmetic, Q4).
4. No published or vendor evidence exists on lead time of cluster sells before a collapse, nor on copier outcomes conditioned on leader-in-cluster. Both must be measured in shadow mode before anything blocks.

## 1. Timing: when does a meaningful cluster map exist, what changes in the first minutes, and is it better to buy now with a cheap check or delay and re-check?

### Takeaway
The membership of a launch bundle is fixed within the first few slots, and funding links between wallets pre-date the token, so a "cluster map" is computable almost as soon as a token has trades; what changes in the first minutes is how much of it is still held, because deployer-linked snipers mostly leave within 1 to 5 minutes. GodsEye's leaders buy a median ~34 s after the earliest known pool and hold ~10 s, so a delay of minutes to re-check costs more (measured entry drift 13% at 60 s, 21% at 120 s) than it can plausibly save; check once, fast, from chain data, and use vendor maps only as a second stage or for logging.

### Cited Findings
- Bundle vs cluster definitions: a bundle is wallets that bought "in the same transaction block, or within seconds of each other at launch" (timing-based; seconds to minutes after launch); a cluster is wallets linked by behaviour (shared funding, coordinated transfers, recycled addresses, appearances across tokens), built over "days, weeks, or months". Bubblemaps' blog does not say whether either updates live. — [Bubblemaps: Bundle vs Cluster](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/whats-the-difference-between-bundle-cluster-2/) [Vblog]
- Funding links pre-date launch in documented cases: Pine Analytics' deployer-funded snipers are wallets with direct SOL transfers to or from the deployer before launch; the author notes multi-hop funding (often 5 to 7 hops) was missed by that method. — [Pine Analytics, Exit Liquidity Machines](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines) [Prac/on-chain analysis, Mar-Apr 2025 data]
- Sniper exit timing (deployer-funded snipers only): over 11% fully exited within 15 s, more than 55% within 1 minute, nearly 85% within 5 minutes; over 90% sold in one or two swaps. Share of supply bought by snipers was not reported. — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines) [Prac]
- Funder graphs in pump.fun research are retrospective: the "Meme Coin Factories" paper builds creator clusters by tracing each creator's first SOL funder up to 3 hops (CEX/bridge/contract nodes removed); under 1-hop, multi-address clusters are 48.02% of creator addresses but 62.99% of coins; the paper "does not test when a cluster could be identified relative to a coin's launch" and does not measure exit timing. — [arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1) [Acad]
- 98.7% of pump.fun mint events include a developer buy in the creation transaction; bundled accounts hold 36.50% of supply and 28.13% of holders at migration on average (migrated tokens only); bundle-aware top-10 share is 24 points higher for high-risk tokens vs 6 for low-risk. — [MemeTrans, arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1) [Acad] (as recorded in the earlier notes)
- "Catching the Rug" uses only a 5-minute feature window (target: rug at 1 hour); it reports no feature importance, no holder-concentration or cluster feature, no 1- or 2-minute window, and no holder time series; best MCC about 0.39; authors say it is not sufficient for real-world deployment and list bundle-level entity resolution as future work. — [arXiv 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20271v1) [Acad]
- Graduation timing context: median ~4.4 minutes and ~457 swaps from creation to graduation on pump.fun (Sept 2025). — [arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1) [Acad] (earlier note)
- Vendor map latency is undocumented: the Bubblemaps Data API (v0.3.2) exposes `is_indexed`, a `ts_update`/`dt_update` on each map, a `refresh` flag that bypasses cache, and a `timestamp` parameter that returns holders/map "as of" a time; it lists no latency or readiness SLA and no credit costs. Its map returns clusters with `share`, `holder_count`, `time_nodes`, `magic_nodes`, and supply stats including `bundles`, `fresh_wallets`, `top_10_adjusted`, plus a `bubblemaps_score`. — [Bubblemaps OpenAPI](https://docs.bubblemaps.io/openapi.json) [Vdoc]
- The Sept 2026 Bubblemaps feed claims each new token "is checked for bundles and clusters as it appears" and drops tokens showing either; no latency, threshold or score scale given. — [HackerNoon, 16 Sep 2026](https://hackernoon.com/bubblemaps-rebuilds-its-platform-to-screen-out-rug-pulls-before-traders-ever-see-the-token); [The Defiant press release](https://thedefiant.io/news/press-releases/bubblemaps-sets-a-new-industry-standard-for-catching-rug-pulls-before-they-happen) [Vblog]
- Bubblemaps loads only the top 250 holders and links wallets by any historical gas-token transaction (earlier note, [Find Deeper Connections](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/find-deeper-connections-between-wallets/)); the API's holder `limit` is 80, 250 or 500. — [OpenAPI](https://docs.bubblemaps.io/openapi.json) [Vdoc]
- Mobula's detection definitions: snipers = early buys "typically 0-3 blocks" after launch, same/next block as deployment, fresh wallet funded shortly before, high priority fee or MEV bundle; bundlers = 3+ wallets with atomic/same-block buys, common funder, similar amounts, later consolidation; it publishes no refresh interval or latency. — [Mobula docs](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers) [Vdoc]
- GMGN: "snipers" are "buyers who purchased within the first few blocks" (no block count); "insiders" are wallets that did not buy after trading opened but hold tokens; "first 70 buyers" is a list; holding status "cannot guarantee real-time accuracy" and, on Solana, transferred vs sold tokens cannot be told apart. — [GMGN docs](https://docs.gmgn.ai/index/insider-traders-snipers-first-70-buyers.md) [Vdoc]
- Internal copy-delay measurement (replay of the 10 copied leaders' trades, 2026-10-12 to 10-26, 0.1 SOL copies, 100 bps fee each way, 0.0005 SOL per tx, tape coverage 92%, so drift is a lower bound; 5,743-5,800 positions per delay): entry price drift vs the leader's fill 3.5% at 1 s, 3.8% at 5 s, 5.3% at 15 s, 8.5% at 30 s, 13.4% at 60 s, 21.0% at 120 s; pooled mean return -0.9% / +0.8% / +0.3% / +0.2% / -0.5% / -1.9% at the same delays; leader's own mean return about 21% on the same positions; leaders' median hold 9-11 s. Real copies do not exist yet; these are model outputs, and the wallets were picked for profit in the same period. — [Int] `npm run replay` output, 2026-10-27
- Internal timing of leaders' first buy per token (10 copy-weighted wallets, buys of 0.2-50 SOL since 2026-10-12, mints with a known age, excluding 107 apparent pool-creation entries of about 168 SOL by 3 wallets at age 0): n = 427 mints; median age 34 s, p25 21 s, p75 64 s; 15% within 15 s, 74% within 60 s, 98% within 5 min. Age is "seconds since the token's first pool was created" per the schema, taken from GeckoTerminal's earliest pool; 413 of the 635 PumpSwap-venue first buys are on mints ending in "pump". — [Int] `data/copycow.db` (`wallet_swaps`, `token_info`), `agent/src/market/geckoterminal.ts`
- External delay anchor: a 2-minute-delayed entry after large wallet clusters lost about 41% per token over 93 tokens (unnamed researcher, exits and costs undisclosed). — [TokenPost, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/investing/35409) [N], earlier note
- RPC Fast's copy-bot playbook (vendor) lists pre-trade vetoes (per-token caps, blacklists, liquidity/age thresholds, honeypot checks, "skip the copy" if price already moved beyond a threshold vs the target's entry, duplicate guards) and states "Late is worse than never"; claims Yellowstone gRPC detection of 10-40 ms. It does not discuss rug or bundle checks. — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-build-a-solana-copy-trading-bot) [Vblog]

### Inferences
- What exists when (my synthesis of the sources above, not measured):
  - Creation slot: creator, dev buy and same-slot bundle buys are in the block. A program-wide stream sees them at once; GodsEye's tracked-wallet stream does not, so it reads them back by RPC (`holders.ts` reads the mint's first two slots, reachable only for coins with up to ~2,000 transactions).
  - Funding edges (creator funder, early-buyer funders) already exist at t0, so a funder-cluster check does not need to wait; it needs RPC lookups per wallet.
  - Bundle membership is settled after the first K (about 5) slots. Holdings are not: deployer-funded snipers are ~11% gone by 15 s, >55% by 60 s, ~85% by 5 min (Pine).
- Consequence: a single "cluster % held now" number mixes two things. At a 30 s decision time part of the launch cohort has already sold, so the current held share understates what was bought at launch, and a cluster that has already sold is itself a warning rather than a safe zero. Measure three numbers: launch-bought share, current-held share, and sold fraction (1 - held/bought). `holders.ts` currently reports launch-bought share (`launchBundlePct`) and, separately, current top-10 and funder-cluster shares; it does not combine them or report the sold fraction.
- The internal timing may remove the "seconds-old token" problem for most candidates: if the earliest pool is the PumpSwap pool (likely, since the 168-SOL age-0 entries look like pool-creation/migration transactions, and a median ~34 s age on PumpSwap is incompatible with a ~4.4 min median graduation time for a curve launch), leaders are buying about half a minute after migration. The curve phase, its bundle, its sniper wave and its funding links are then minutes old at decision time, so a fuller map is available than "creation block + 10 s" suggests. If instead the age is since the pump.fun curve was created, the opposite holds. This must be checked (Gaps).
- Buy now with a cheap check vs delay and recheck: delaying to re-check costs measured drift of 5.3% / 8.5% / 13.4% / 21.0% at 15 / 30 / 60 / 120 s, against a leader who holds ~10 s. A re-check delay of 1 to 5 minutes is longer than the leader's median hold and costs more than most vetoes can recover. So: (a) run the chain-derived cluster check inside the existing window, in parallel with the quote, and size by its result; (b) do not hold the buy for a vendor map; (c) use delay-and-recheck only as a second chance for the narrow case "first check was inconclusive (holders_failed / timeout / too busy to reach the launch)", and then no later than the existing 60 s lateness limit; (d) run vendor maps (Bubblemaps API, RugCheck) at +1 and +5 min in shadow mode to learn what they would have said and how late they are. Earlier note reaches the same placement for vendor reports (second stage at +1 to +5 min); the new element here is the measured drift and hold time.
- A cheap first-stage check from data in hand: creator == leader (zero RPC, pump `TradeEvent.creator`), leader funded by creator cluster (cached per leader), launch-bought share and funder-cluster share from the existing `measureHolders`, and the stress number in Q4 from the latest reserves. Those take milliseconds-to-seconds; `screen_ms`/`holders_ms` are already recorded in the shadow table, which will show whether the holders read fits in the window.

### Gaps
- Whether `token_info.pool_created_at` (GeckoTerminal earliest pool) equals the PumpSwap pool creation time or the bonding-curve creation time for pump-suffix mints was not verified; a 20-mint spot check (first signature of the mint vs `pool_created_at`) settles it. The 107 age-0 entries (3 wallets, 168-170 SOL) look like parse artefacts of pool creation; not investigated.
- No source documents how much of a typical pump.fun launch cohort has sold by +10 s / +60 s / +5 min measured as supply share (Pine gives wallet-exit shares for one subset, deployer-funded snipers, in Mar-Apr 2025).
- No measurement of Bubblemaps API time-to-map for a seconds-old token; no credit price found. RugCheck's lag is partly measured by the shadow sampler (first two marks), not yet run.
- Axiom, Photon, Padre: no official definition of when bundle/insider % are computed or refreshed found.
- The internal replay excludes rug and holder checks and uses a tape that misses untracked wallets' swaps, so its delay costs are lower bounds and say nothing about which delayed entries were avoided rugs.

## 2. What bots, terminals and risk dashboards document as anti-rug / bundle / dev settings, and their shipped defaults

### Takeaway
Across Trojan, BonkBot, Maestro, GMGN, Axiom, Banana Gun, Photon, Padre and Sol Trading Bot, I found no documented copy-trading setting that vetoes on bundle, cluster or insider share, and no documented default for any holder-concentration filter. Holder-concentration filters (bundle %, insiders %, snipers %, dev %, top-10 %) exist as user-set min/max ranges in discovery tables (Axiom Pulse, Trojan Explorer); copy-trade pages offer market cap, liquidity, token age, platform, tax and creator allow/deny filters. Thresholds published are guide opinion, not shipped defaults.

- One page reporting a "Rug Republic" pump.fun bundle cluster (avoid.net) labels itself AI-drafted and awaiting fact-check; I excluded its numbers. — [avoid.net](https://avoid.net/rug-republic-coordinated-pump-fun-solana-bundle-rug-cluster)

### Inferences
- The industry pattern is: terminals expose cluster-ish metrics as discovery filters the human sets; copy bots gate on liquidity, market cap, age and token-contract traps (honeypot/tax/blacklist), not on holder structure; the one cluster-like copy exit is "creator sold" (GMGN Dev Sell). Competing on holder structure is therefore not table stakes, and nobody's shipped default can be borrowed as a calibrated value.
- Practitioner thresholds cluster at: bundled supply 10-20% as the red line, single connected cluster 20-30%, top-10 30%, dev 5%, creator 15% (one open-source bot). GodsEye's 25% bundle / 15% cluster / 50% top-10 / 20% insider are inside or looser than that range on bundle and top-10, tighter on cluster. None of the ranges is validated (earlier notes).
- Nearest useful adoptions: (i) GMGN's "Dev Sell" pattern as a post-buy trigger (creator sells at least X% of balance in one transaction -> sell part of ours); (ii) Maestro's "liquidity under 1% of supply" block, which is a cheap pool-depth sanity check; (iii) Banana's creator allow/deny list as a place to put a known-farm blacklist; (iv) Bubblemaps' separation of bundle (timing) from cluster (funding), which matches how `holders.ts` already measures `launchBundlePct` vs `clusterPct`.

### Gaps
- Photon, Padre, Sol Trading Bot documentation; Axiom and Trojan definitions of Bundle %/Insiders %/Snipers % (what is counted, when computed); GMGN's bundler definition.
- Defaults inside the apps' UIs (the docs describe no defaults; a UI inspection would be needed and was out of scope).
- Whether any bot's "Safe Search"/Pulse presets ship with bundle/insider defaults.
- Maestro's Token Report details and BonkBot's Security Config pages were not opened.

## 3. Leader-aware interpretation: the leader inside the cluster, and copiers behind insiders

### Takeaway
Nothing published measures what happens to a copier when the copied wallet is itself inside the token's creator-linked cluster, so this is design, not evidence. The relevant facts: deployer-linked snipers exit fast and in one or two swaps (Pine), the largest documented insider dumps are coordinated by the cluster that holds most of the supply (HAWK, LIBRA, Focai), and GodsEye's leaders hold ~10 s. For the "keep copying insiders" policy the useful distinction is how much of the cluster is the leader's own position (which our leader-sell mirror tracks) versus the part controlled by others (which it does not).

### Cited Findings
- Deployer-funded same-block snipers: over 55% exit within 1 minute, nearly 85% within 5; 87% of such snipes profitable; the report's recommendation is adaptive trust scores and tiered risk labels (hard warning for repeated deployer-linked same-block sniping), since attackers rotate wallets. — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines) [Prac]
- Copy-trading paper: wallet-history-only models identified skilled wallets yet gave negative copier returns; adversarial bots (snipers in the first ~5 blocks, creator-linked bundle bots in roughly a quarter of projects) are named as the attack surface; copier return 3% vs leader 14% per coin with zero modeled delay. — [arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641) [Acad] (earlier note)
- Case: at least 15 suspected insider wallets bought over 60% of FOCAI supply for about $14,600 and sold everything for about 94,175 SOL (about $20.5M) per Lookonchain; reports differ on whether the buys were on pump.fun or Raydium, and the insider label is analytics-firm suspicion; who bought from them is not stated. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/focai-memecoin-insider-trading-solana-profit) (search summary only) [Prac]
- Case: HAWK: Bubblemaps flagged 96% of supply in one cluster at launch; the deployer minted 100% of supply, 97% routed through 10 wallets (80% team, 17% to 285 presale wallets); 155 of the 285 sold, realizing $3.3M; no sell timestamps. — [Bubblemaps HAWK](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/hawk-anatomy-of-a-celebrity-rug/) [Prac]
- Case: LIBRA: the cluster held 82% of supply in the first hour; the team exited largely by removing USDC/SOL from existing pools and adding one-sided pools rather than selling tokens into the market ("essentially zero price slippage"), about $87M by the time of the thread; no wallet count or sell sequence given. — [Bubblemaps LIBRA](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/the-libra-playbook-how-one-cluster-drained-87-million-in-a-single-hour/) [Prac]
- Practitioner claims that fast in-and-out wallets leave copiers holding the bag and that heavily followed wallets become self-fulfilling and supply liquidity to the original wallet are opinion pieces without data. — [Trojan blog guide](https://trojan.com/blog/solana-copy-trading-guide-2026), [Medium](https://medium.com/@nathan.baldwin_31153/copy-trading-on-solana-how-to-find-alpha-wallets-without-getting-faked-out-by-bots-0bc550f07290) (search summaries only) [Prac/Vblog]
- Internal: the 10 copied leaders ("insider" pattern in the replay output) show leader own return about 21% vs pooled copy mean return about 0% at 5-30 s delays on the same positions (copy cost about 20 pp, of which entry drift is 3.8-8.5%); leader median hold 9-11 s. — [Int] `npm run replay`, 2026-10-27
- Earlier-note per-token leader context rules (creator == leader, launch-block participant, creator -> leader funding, leader-buy price impact) and their absence of outcome evidence: `pre_buy_checks.md` section 5. Not repeated.

### Inferences
- Four cases to separate per token, because they imply different actions (design):
  1. Leader is the creator (zero RPC: `TradeEvent.creator == user`). The leader's buy is not independent information; the leader controls the sell. Size down hard or skip as a per-token rule; log the outcome either way.
  2. Leader sits in the creator-linked / launch-bundle cluster (funded by or bundled with the creator). The leader's sell is likely the earliest observable cluster sell, so the existing "mirror leader sells" exit is the best available cluster-sell proxy, but the copy lands after the leader's price impact (the replay's copy-cost column). Treat as: smaller size, tighter max-hold, no scale-in.
  3. Leader is an independent early buyer, a launch-block sniper not tied to the creator: the cluster is someone else's. The leader's sell does not tell us when the cluster sells; the cluster-sell monitor (Q5) carries more of the weight.
  4. Leader is outside any cluster: the cluster term in the stress number stands alone.
- Compute the stress number twice: once with all cluster tokens (including the leader's), once excluding the leader's tokens. The excluding-leader version approximates the risk our leader-sell mirror cannot cover; the including-leader version approximates how much the leader's own exit moves price before our mirrored sell lands. Whether this decomposition predicts outcomes is untested.
- Under the owner's policy the output is a size multiplier and an exit setting per token, never a change to `copy_weight`. This is consistent with the earlier note's recommendation to express leader context as per-token fields.
- LIBRA-style exits through LP removal (no token sales) are invisible to a cluster token-balance monitor; for pump-origin pools the earlier note records LP as burned at migration but unverified, so a pool-vault monitor (Q5) should stay in the design alongside any cluster-balance monitor.
- Evidence level: the claim "leader in cluster -> worse copier outcome" is a hypothesis; Pine's exit speeds and the HAWK/FOCAI cases support the mechanism only.

### Gaps
- No study or dataset on copier outcomes conditioned on leader-in-cluster, or on whether a cluster-linked leader sells before, with, or after the rest of the cluster. The shadow log can measure this (Q6).
- FOCAI and HAWK are 2024-25 cases on a different launch mix (celebrity or presale tokens, Raydium/Meteora); their relevance to PumpSwap tokens is unestablished. Cointelegraph's page was seen via a search summary only.
- Whether PumpSwap pool-creator LP tokens are really burned at migration (primary doc not read to the end in earlier notes) remains open.

## 4. Decision-rule shapes: hard veto, proportional size-down, and a stressed-exit cutoff

### Takeaway
Published guidance gives only flat share-of-supply bands (10-30%) with no validation, and the base rate of "high-risk" among fresh migrated tokens is 74-84% by one academic labelling, so a flat veto would block most candidates. A stressed-exit calculation turns the same cluster share into an expected drawdown that depends on pool depth, and it shows the flat cutoff is too loose near migration and too tight early on the curve. Because our position is far smaller than any pool, the binding constraint is our drawdown risk budget, not our own price impact.

### Cited Findings
- Practitioner share-of-supply thresholds are flat percentages (Q2): bundle 10-20% red, cluster 20-30% red, top-10 25-30% green line, dev 3-5%, creator 15%; validated cutoffs do not exist for any of them (earlier notes). — sources in Q2.
- Base-rate pressure: 84.13% of 41,470 migrated pump.fun tokens labelled high-risk; 74.18% in the experimental subset; bundles hold 36.50% of supply at migration on average; a top-100 risk-ranked pick cut average loss from 60.71% to 26.64%. — [MemeTrans, arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1) [Acad] (earlier notes)
- Constant-product pool facts used below: pump.fun curve "based on Uniswap V2" with virtual reserves (initial 1,073,000,000,000,000 virtual tokens in 6-decimal units, 30 SOL virtual, 793.1M real tokens, 1B supply); graduation near 85 real SOL; migrated PumpSwap pool is constant product with LP fee 20 bps plus protocol fee 5 bps. — [PUMP_PROGRAM_README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/main/docs/PUMP_PROGRAM_README.md), [PUMP_SWAP_README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/main/docs/PUMP_SWAP_README.md) [Vdoc, primary program docs]; graduation SOL from [arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1) (earlier note)
- Mayhem-mode coins hold an extra agent-held supply that can inflate concentration metrics, and quote-paired coins are not SOL-denominated (earlier note `pre_buy_checks.md` section 4); relevant because stress numbers need the right base reserve and holder denominators.
- Shared funders such as exchanges generate false cluster links, and a real 2025 case (AVA) was exchange-funded wallets with similar amounts and zero history, so a rule that discards CEX-funded groups misses it (earlier note `wallet_clustering.md`, [Blockchain.News summary of a Bubblemaps post](https://blockchain.news/flashnews/23-wallets-from-bitget-binance-sniped-ava-on-solana-bubblemaps-on-chain-alert-for-traders)).
- Exit-aware copy-bot controls in the wild: per-token and per-trade caps, price-drift skip, liquidity thresholds (RPC Fast playbook); Maestro's liquidity < 1% of supply block (Q2).

### Inferences
Everything in this section is my arithmetic or design.

**Stressed-exit calculation.** If cluster tokens T are sold into a constant-product pool with base-token reserve B (fees ignored), the final spot price falls by D = 1 - 1/(1+T/B)^2, and the cluster extracts quote Q·(T/B)/(1+T/B). The T/B needed for a given drop: D = 20% -> 0.118; 35% -> 0.240; 50% -> 0.414; 70% -> 0.826; 90% -> 2.16. On the curve, B is the virtual token reserve, which shrinks as SOL is raised (k = 30 SOL x 1,073M tokens).

Cluster share of total supply (%) that produces the stated price drop if dumped in full (my computation, verified by script):

| Real SOL raised on curve | virtual tokens left (M) | -20% | -35% | -50% | -70% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 920 | 10.9 | 22.1 | 38.1 | 75.9 |
| 10 | 805 | 9.5 | 19.3 | 33.3 | 66.5 |
| 20 | 644 | 7.6 | 15.5 | 26.7 | 53.2 |
| 40 | 460 | 5.4 | 11.1 | 19.0 | 38.0 |
| 60 | 358 | 4.2 | 8.6 | 14.8 | 29.5 |
| 85 (graduation) | 280 | 3.3 | 6.7 | 11.6 | 23.1 |
| PumpSwap just after migration (B = 206.9M tokens, about 85 SOL) | 207 | 2.4 | 5.0 | 8.6 | 17.1 |

Examples on a freshly migrated PumpSwap pool: a cluster holding 5% / 10% / 15% / 20% / 30% of supply dumping fully gives a -35% / -55% / -66% / -74% / -83% price drop and extracts about 16.5 / 27.7 / 35.7 / 41.8 / 50.3 SOL. A 15%-supply cluster selling 10% / 25% / 50% / 100% of its tokens gives -13% / -28% / -46% / -66%. (The 206.9M base figure is derived from the program constants, as in the earlier note.)

What follows:
- A flat 25% bundle / 15% cluster cutoff passes clusters that cause -66% to -83% on a freshly migrated pool and is also loose at graduation; early on the curve the same 15% cluster is only about -26% at 5 SOL raised. The unit that carries the risk is D (or T/B), which depends on where the token is on the curve or pool. If leaders mostly buy shortly after migration (Q1), the relevant row is the bottom one, where about 5% of supply is already a -35% shock; the MemeTrans average of 36.5% bundled at migration shows why almost every fresh token would fail such a cutoff, which is a sign that the cutoff should be applied to the current held share (after snipers have sold), not the launch-bought share.
- Proposed decision shape (to test in shadow, not to enable): compute D_now from the cluster's current held tokens and the latest reserves; map to a size multiplier m(D) that is 1 below D_lo (about 20%), falls linearly to 0.25-0.5 at D_hi (about 55-60%), and becomes a veto only when a hard structural condition also holds (creator-funded cluster, cluster not yet reduced, D above about 70%, or leader is the creator). D_lo / D_hi are guesses to fit on the shadow data; the shape (proportional, pool-relative) is the design suggestion.
- Why size-down before veto: with a 74-84% high-risk base rate, a veto removes most of the sample that would be needed to learn whether the rule works, while a size multiplier keeps every candidate observable and caps loss. Veto only where size-down cannot help (the cluster can drain more than the pool holds, or an unsellable token).
- Position cap relative to pool depth is not the binding rule here: at 0.05-0.15 SOL copies against pools of 10+ SOL, our own impact is under about 1.5% (earlier note arithmetic). The binding quantity is expected loss = P(dump while held) x D x size. With P unknown, set size so that size x D at the veto threshold stays within a per-trade loss budget, and estimate P from shadow data later.
- Fail-open vs fail-closed when the holders read fails or times out: use a reduced size multiplier rather than skipping, and log the status (the shadow table already carries `holders_failed` / `holders_timeout`).
- Cluster definitions to keep apart in the log: launch bundle (timing), funder cluster (funding), RugCheck insiders (vendor); the stress number should use the union of tokens held by any of them, with the pool/curve, exchanges and, for mayhem coins, the agent vault excluded.
- The AVA-type case (exchange-funded, similar amounts, zero history) argues for a second cluster cue besides common funder: same-block/similar-amount buys by fresh wallets; Mobula lists similar buy amounts as a bundler signal.

### Gaps
- No published loss-vs-threshold curve for any cluster or bundle share; no data on how often a cluster that holds T/B above a given level actually dumps within the copy's holding time.
- The stress model ignores fees (25 bps per PumpSwap leg), staged selling, buy-side refills, CLMM/DLMM-style pools, and the creator-fee path; real drops will differ. It assumes the cluster's tokens are in sellable wallets and that the pool is a plain constant-product one.
- The 206.9M base-token figure at migration is derived, not read from the pool; confirm against a few PumpSwap `Pool` vaults.
- Exchange fan-out and bot-platform fee wallets as false cluster links: no source documents the addresses (earlier note).

## 5. Post-buy use: cluster sells as an exit trigger, alert tools, lead time, and the cost of watching over gRPC

### Takeaway
Cluster, creator and insider selling are the most plausible real-time precursors of the dominant rug type (pump-and-dump by holders, 79% of flagged Solana rugs), and bots already ship the creator-sell version (GMGN Dev Sell), but no source quantifies lead time or false-positive rate, and I found no Bubblemaps or Telegram product that alerts on cluster sells. For GodsEye the use case is narrower than it sounds: the copied leaders hold about 10 s, so a cluster watcher matters mainly for positions that outlive the leader's exit. Watching a few dozen cluster wallets or the pool's swaps for a handful of open positions is cheap over gRPC.

### Cited Findings
- Dominant rug type is holders leaving through sells: 79.0% pump-and-dump (60,402), 20.4% liquidity withdrawal, 0.6% freeze abuse in 76,469 flagged Solana tokens (H1 2025); median creation-to-first-profit time about 17 min (p25 about 3.5 min); pump-and-dump median about 32 min. — [Chen et al., arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1) [Acad] (earlier notes)
- Bubblemaps' guidance lists the precursors: whether a large cluster sold just before a price drop, connected wallets shuffling tokens among themselves, dormant wallets making a large transfer, fresh large wallets, gradual selling by top holders, rising concentration, and mirrored buys/sells; it gives no formal post-purchase routine and says verify locks independently. — [Bubblemaps rug-pull guide](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-spot-a-rug-pull-using-bubblemaps-before-it-happens/) [Vblog]
- No cluster-sell alert product found: Bubblemaps' API/docs list no alerts; the September 2026 revamp text mentions none; the whale tracker is described as monitoring the largest holders over time; a search for a Bubblemaps Telegram alert bot found only an unrelated InsightX mini-app integration. — [Bubblemaps docs](https://docs.bubblemaps.io), [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/bubblemaps-rebuilds-its-platform-to-screen-out-rug-pulls-before-traders-ever-see-the-token), [InsightX Bubblemaps integration](https://docs.insightx.network/docs/telegram-mini-app) (snippet only) [Vdoc/Vblog]
- Creator-sell exit shipped by a bot: GMGN Dev Sell sells a configured share of your holding when the creator sells at least a configured ratio in one transaction; detection and latency undocumented. — [GMGN Dev Sell](https://docs.gmgn.ai/index/auto-trading-dev-sell.md) [Vdoc]
- GMGN states Solana "temporarily" cannot distinguish transferred from sold tokens in its holder status. — [GMGN docs](https://docs.gmgn.ai/index/insider-traders-snipers-first-70-buyers.md) [Vdoc]
- Lead time evidence: Pine's data show snipers exit in 1-2 swaps and mostly within 1-5 minutes; HAWK reports 155 of 285 presale wallets sold with no timestamps; LIBRA exited via pool removal over "early hours"; Chen reports no precursor timing. The earlier note records that no source gives lead time or false-positive rates for any precursor. — [Pine](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines), [HAWK](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/hawk-anatomy-of-a-celebrity-rug/), [LIBRA](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/the-libra-playbook-how-one-cluster-drained-87-million-in-a-single-hour/), [Chen](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1)
- gRPC watching mechanics and prices (earlier note, primary docs): transaction filters AND across fields, OR within arrays; `account_include` matches any listed account; account subscriptions can slice account data; Triton recommends a cuckoo-filter account filter beyond roughly 100 accounts and charges $0.08/GB metered streaming; Helius says explicit lists up to about 10,000 accounts, cuckoo filters (3-4 bytes per account on the wire, under 1% false positives) above, and a transaction filter can take a compressed filter with `matchMints: true` so the server also checks pre/post token-balance mints; RPC Fast's SaaS Yellowstone tier caps at 10 pubkeys and one filter per type; open-source server caps are operator-set. — [Triton docs](https://docs.triton.one/rpc-pool/grpc-subscriptions), [Helius cuckoo filters](https://www.helius.dev/docs/laserstream/cuckoo-filters.md), [Triton pricing](https://triton.one/pricing), [yellowstone-grpc](https://github.com/rpcpool/yellowstone-grpc)
- Internal: leader median hold 9-11 s; at the end of the replay data 0-3 of ~5,800 positions per delay were still open (the replay mirrors leader sells). — [Int] replay output

### Inferences
- What to watch (design), cheapest and most informative first:
  1. The pool: transaction filter on the pool/curve address of each open position, decode every sell and its wallet. Volume equals that token's own trading, which is already what price tracking needs; wallet-by-wallet comparison against the cluster list (from Q4) gives "cluster sold fraction since our entry" without knowing in advance where tokens are transferred.
  2. Creator wallet: GMGN-Dev-Sell-style trigger (creator sells at least X% of balance). Data comes from the same pool decode.
  3. Pool vault balances (earlier note) for LP-style exits that cluster token balances cannot see (LIBRA-type).
  4. Optionally an account subscription on the cluster wallets' token accounts for the mint with a data slice to the 8-byte amount (offset 64, general knowledge, unverified) to catch transfers to fresh wallets; updates are tiny. Because a balance fall can be a transfer, treat "moved to a new wallet" as consolidation (a warning) and add the new wallet to the cluster list.
- Triggers (hypotheses, thresholds unknown): any creator sell -> sell part; cluster sold fraction since entry above g1 (for example 25%) -> sell half and tighten the stop; above g2 (for example 50%) or D_now above about 50% -> sell all; require a price or reserve move alongside to avoid exiting on benign profit-taking. Hard triggers should not wait for confirmation (single-slot lead), per the earlier note.
- Lead-time expectation: a single-transaction dump gives no lead time (we see it after it lands); gradual dumps (Chen's p25 lifecycle about 3.5 min) give some. A sell triggered by the first of several cluster sells can beat the later sellers, not the first one. This is reasoning; no source measures it.
- Cost (my arithmetic, assumptions flagged): 20 open positions x 50 cluster wallets = 1,000 addresses; if each wallet's transactions produce 20 messages/hour of about 3 KB (assumption), that is 60 MB/hour or about 1.4 GB/day, about $0.12/day at $0.08/GB. Pool-level subscription for 20 pools with several hundred swaps each is the same order. Both are small next to the existing wallet stream; the provider's address-count cap (10 pubkeys on one SaaS tier) is a larger practical limit than cost, hence the pool-address approach (1 per position) over per-wallet lists.
- Relevance caveat: with leaders holding ~10 s and the copy mirroring leader sells, a cluster monitor changes outcomes only for the tail of positions that outlive the leader. The shadow log can show the size of that tail (Q6) before any build.
- No alert vendor to rely on: the build would be GodsEye's own stream decoder; Bubblemaps is a data API with `refresh` and `timestamp`, not an alerting service.

### Gaps
- Lead time of cluster sells before collapse, false-positive rate of cluster-sell triggers, and how often benign profit-taking by launch bundles looks like a dump: no source.
- Whether a `FreezeAccount` or token-account `account_include` filter delivers every relevant transaction on all providers; Yellowstone transaction-message size in bytes for pump swaps was not measured.
- Token-account byte layout offset for the data slice is general knowledge, not verified in this pass.
- Time-to-detection from a processed-commitment pool stream versus a shred-derived feed (earlier note covers feeds in general).

## 6. Measurement: testing a cluster rule with the shadow log and replay harness before it blocks anything

### Takeaway
Log cluster state as a time series per candidate (at the decision time, +1 min, +5 min), keep the leader-in-cluster flags and the cluster's sells as separate columns, and evaluate rules offline on the shadow outcomes and the copy-fill replay. Expect 2 to 6 weeks to compare a few severity buckets at the current candidate rate (about 430-600 mints a week, my estimate from the last two weeks of `wallet_swaps`), and read the results through three biases: only leader-bought tokens are observed, held-share measured late understates the cluster, and silent rugs look like missing data.

### Cited Findings
- Existing infrastructure: the shadow log writes `shadow_candidates` on every first copyable leader buy (raw inputs `top10_pct`, `launch_bundle_pct`, `launch_buyers`, `cluster_pct`, `cluster_size`, `cluster_from_creator`, `insider_pct`, RugCheck score/lock, authorities, extensions, cutoffs in force, screen timings `screen_ms`/`holders_ms`) and `shadow_samples` at 1, 5, 15, 30 min and 1, 6, 24 h (price, liquidity, Jupiter sell quote, RugCheck re-asked at the first two marks); `labels.ts` defines collapse50 / collapse90 / unexitable / routeLost / liquidityDrop / dark / neverPriced / rugLike. — [Int] `agent/src/shadow/log.ts`, `sampler.ts`, `labels.ts`, `shared/src/schema.ts`
- State of the local data on 2026-10-27: `data/copycow.db` has no `shadow_candidates` table yet (query failed: no such table), so no shadow rows exist to analyse. — [Int]
- The copy-fill replay models DEX fee, per-tx cost, constant-product impact with depth estimated from the tape, and the engine's stop/take-profit/max-hold on a one-minute grid; it states "Not replayed: rug and holder checks". — [Int] `agent/src/replay/model.ts`
- Bubblemaps' API `timestamp` parameter returns holders/map as of a past time, which allows a retrospective map for tokens that were not logged live (credit cost not documented). — [Bubblemaps OpenAPI](https://docs.bubblemaps.io/openapi.json) [Vdoc]
- Reject inference: standard methods rest on tenuous assumptions; the robust approach is to observe a sample of rejected cases; doubly robust off-policy evaluation combines a reward model and a propensity model. — earlier note `post_buy_monitoring_calibration.md` Q5 (Hand & Henley; Dudik, Langford, Li)
- Sample-size arithmetic and the 3/n rule, label definitions and exploration-buy design are in the earlier note, Q5-Q6. Not repeated except where new below.
- Candidate volume, internal: the 10 copy-weighted wallets bought 877 distinct mints (0.2 SOL or more) between 2026-10-12 and 10-26, and among the 533 of them with a known token age, 177 were bought by two or more copied wallets. — [Int] `wallet_swaps`

### Inferences
**What to add to the log (design).**
- Per candidate, at decision time and again at +60 s and +300 s (a new `shadow_cluster_samples` table keyed by candidate and horizon, so the time series is available without re-reading): launch-bought share, current held share, sold fraction, cluster wallet count, share of top-10 that is clustered, creator-funded flag; the same three shares excluding the leader's own tokens; D_now and T/B for the latest reserves with the pool/curve type and `quote_mint`; reserves themselves (virtual or pool base and quote) so D can be recomputed with other definitions.
- Leader context: `leader_is_creator`, `leader_in_launch_bundle`, `leader_funded_by_creator_cluster`, leader's tokens as a share of the cluster, leader's slot minus creation slot, leader's hold time (later filled from the leader's sell).
- Time since launch in two clocks: since the mint was created and since the first pool (the Q1 uncertainty), both stored.
- Post-entry cluster behaviour: first cluster-wallet sell time, cluster sold fraction at 1 / 5 / 15 / 60 min, creator sell time and size, new wallets added to the cluster, time of leader sell, time of the first 25% / 50% price drawdown; these let lead time (first cluster sell to collapse) be measured as a distribution.
- Vendor second opinions as log-only columns at +60 s and +5 min: Bubblemaps score/cluster share via the API and RugCheck insiders (already sampled), with the request time, to measure vendor lag and agreement with the chain-derived numbers.
- A `holders_status` for every cluster sample (done, partial, too busy to reach launch) so missing data is never silent; `holders.ts` returns `launchBundlePct: null` when a coin is too busy and that must be stored as its own state.

**Evaluating a rule before it blocks anything.**
1. Freeze the rule's definition and cutoffs before looking at outcomes (store `limits` as the code already does).
2. For each candidate compute the counterfactual: rule verdict or size multiplier at the decision time, then outcomes from shadow samples (labels.ts) and from the replay's fills at the observed delay; report loss avoided on rugLike tokens, wins forgone on survivors, share of candidates blocked or shrunk, and the net after costs. Extend the replay with an optional per-token multiplier file (candidate mint -> size multiplier and veto flag) so it replays both policies on identical entries. Because the replay tape lacks untracked swaps, take outcomes from the shadow samples where the two disagree.
3. Compare by buckets of D_now and of cluster share (deciles or 4-5 bands) rather than at one cutoff; pick the cutoff by expected value, using the break-even P* = G/(L+G) from the earlier note.
4. Compare the flat-percentage rule with the stressed-exit rule on the same data; keep the simpler one unless the pool-relative one is clearly better out of sample (chronological split: earlier weeks fit, later weeks test).
5. Add a small randomized exploration sample on vetoed tokens (earlier note: 3-5% inclusion probability, tiny size) so the blocked group's outcomes are not purely modelled.

**Minimum sample sizes (normal approximation, 95% confidence, 80% power; my arithmetic).** To distinguish a rug-like rate of 35% in a high-cluster bucket from 20% in a low bucket: about 135 tokens per bucket; 35% vs 25%: about 325 per bucket; a rate observed at 0 events in n tokens only bounds the true rate below about 3/n (100 tokens -> 3%). A mean-return difference of 0.10-0.20 with a return SD near 1.0 needs about 400-1,600 per arm (earlier note). At ~430-600 mints/week, if the worst-severity bucket holds ~15-20% of candidates (unknown), that bucket reaches ~135 in about 1.5-2 weeks and ~325 in 4-5 weeks; the whole-screen go/no-go on mean returns takes longer. Splitting by venue, token age or leader multiplies this, so pre-register few cuts. Tokens bought by two or more copied wallets are not independent observations; count distinct mints.

**Pitfalls.**
- Selection bias: only tokens a copied leader bought are observed. Leaders may already avoid heavy-cluster tokens or prefer them, so the observed range of cluster share is truncated and a cutoff learned inside it should not be extrapolated to wider universes. If the rule is only applied to leader-bought tokens, this is the right population, but results do not transfer to other leaders added later.
- Hindsight / disappearing clusters: a cluster that sold has current held share near zero, so computing cluster share after the fact makes rugged tokens look cluster-free. Always derive launch-bought share and sold fraction, and sample at decision time rather than reconstructing later.
- Survivorship: tokens that stop being quoted drop out of price feeds and vendor maps; record "no price" as an outcome (the sampler does) and treat unrecorded as unknown, not survived.
- Silent rugs and tape blindness: the swap tape of tracked wallets goes quiet after a pull (earlier note: 93.5% of entries silent within 60 min), so outcomes need the sampler's price/liquidity/sell-quote source or a pool-vault subscription.
- Leader-sell confounding: a leader's own exit moves price; separate "price fell because the leader sold" from "because the cluster sold" by logging both sell times.
- Label circularity: do not label rugs from the same holder/liquidity features that define the rule (earlier note on SolRPDS).
- Multiple testing and regime change: many knobs (bundle, funder cluster, insider, D bands, leader flags) on a few hundred tokens a week overfit; prefer one monotone score with a single cutoff; recheck on rolling windows of 4-8 weeks.
- Vendor map leakage: a map fetched at +5 min contains information a decision at +30 s did not have; use vendor columns for lag study only.

**Suggested order (design).** (1) Run the shadow log as built to collect baseline; verify the pool-age question (Q1). (2) Add the cluster time-series and leader-context columns, chain-derived only. (3) Replay the stressed-exit rule and the flat rule against shadow outcomes; no blocking. (4) If a bucket shows a clear loss-rate gap at n of 300 or more, enable size-down for that bucket only. (5) Only then add the pool-stream cluster-sell monitor, and only if the shadow log shows a material tail of positions that outlive the leader.

### Gaps
- No shadow data exists locally yet, so every threshold, bucket size and rate above is unmeasured; the candidate rate, bucket shares and return SD are assumptions.
- The replay harness currently cannot take a per-token multiplier; the extension is a build item, not tested.
- Bubblemaps API credit cost and historical-map coverage for pump tokens are unknown; whether `timestamp` returns early-state clusters for a token younger than a minute was not tested.
- No published method for evaluating a rule on a population selected by another agent (the copied leader); the doubly robust references are from credit scoring and contextual bandits, not memecoins.
