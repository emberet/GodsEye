# Bubble-map style cluster signals (bundled, connected, insider, funder-cluster supply) as predictors of rugs and dumps on Solana memecoins

Scope: research run 2026-10-27, about 35 tool calls. Source-type tags used throughout: [ACAD] academic preprint or paper (peer-review status stated where known); [FOR] forensic-analyst post (Bubblemaps, a product vendor, publishing its own investigations); [VEND] vendor or tool-seller documentation or marketing; [PRESS] news or secondary summary; [SNIP] seen only in a search-result snippet, page not opened (treat as unverified). Already established in earlier notes and not redone: 79% of flagged rugs are sellable pump-and-dump cliffs (Chen), MemeTrans basics (bundles lift top-10 share ~24 points in high-risk tokens vs ~6 in low-risk; label rule; base rates), the "Catching the Rug" headline MCC, SolRPDS circular labels, Kamat 2607.02795 numbers withdrawn. Where this note repeats one of those it adds a detail or a correction.

Headline of the whole note: **I found no published measurement of the form "tokens with cluster/bundle share above X% went to zero Y% of the time vs Z% for tokens below X%," on Solana or elsewhere, from any source type.** Every threshold in circulation is a vendor rule of thumb with no stated derivation. What does exist is (a) a large industry measurement of how common dev-linked launch bundles are and how fast their owners sell (Bitquery, Oct 2026), (b) one academic dataset showing bundle features carry more predictive weight than raw holder-concentration features (MemeTrans), and (c) a pile of forensic case studies that are selected on the outcome.

---

## Q1. What does the published evidence say about connected-holder, bundled, sniper/insider, top-N and funder-cluster share versus later collapse? Effect sizes, thresholds, base rates, false positives

### Takeaway
The strongest quantitative evidence is indirect: MemeTrans shows bundle features add more predictive lift than holding-concentration features (AUPRC ablation), and Bitquery's October 2026 census shows dev-linked bundles are on roughly 1 in 5 pump.fun launches with a median 10% of supply, with the dev side selling within a minute on most. Nobody publishes risk-by-bucket tables or cluster-signal error rates, and almost every "false positive" statement is qualitative.

### Cited Findings

**Academic measurements**
- MemeTrans (arXiv 2602.13480, Feb 2026, preprint, migrated pump.fun tokens only, 41,470 launches around the $TRUMP window). Bundle detection uses three heuristics: co-purchase (accounts transacting jointly in one transaction), fund-flow (same funding address, CEX funders excluded) and Jito bundle ID (crawled from Jito Explorer for 22,668,222 pre-migration transactions); identifiers are merged into entity clusters. Share of holders / supply that is bundled at migration by method: co-purchase 6.46% / 9.16%; fund-flow 22.57% / 28.22%; Jito 10.39% / 15.96%; all combined 28.13% / 36.50%. [ACAD] — [MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480)
- MemeTrans feature groups (all computed pre-migration): contextual 6, holding concentration 59, market activity 22, bundle statistics 35. AUPRC ablation: full 0.5729; minus contextual 0.5567; minus holding 0.5693; minus market activity 0.5369; minus bundle 0.5451; minus holding and bundle 0.5268. Arithmetic from those figures: removing bundle features costs 2.8 AUPRC points, removing holding features costs 0.4, removing both costs 4.6. Random-forest importance ranks market activity and bundle groups highest. [ACAD] — [MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480)
- MemeTrans limits stated by the paper: the bundle heuristics are not validated and no false-positive rate is estimated; the 36.5% supply figure depends entirely on them; only migrated tokens are covered; the paper does not test whether the heuristics mislabel specific wallet types (shared funders beyond exchanges); "sniper" features are not defined. [ACAD] — [MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480)
- Chen et al. (arXiv 2603.24625, H1 2025, 100,063 tokens, 76,469 flagged): the paper does not report top-holder share, bundling or sniper share; creator-related address clusters exist at the syndicate level (78 large groups, 16 single scammers and 62 multi-party; median 36 members, range 1-169; "star" topology with profits converging on one address, or dense "cluster" topology). Median holders: 9 for rug tokens versus 41,026 for legitimate tokens. The paper says syndicates use wash trading with several accounts holding positions to "forge the illusion of a low top-holder concentration." Manual validation of the flag rule: 1 false positive in 382 sampled flags (0.26%, 95% upper bound about 1.45%; the false positive was a token later revived by a community takeover) and 9 confirmed misses in 100 sampled non-flags. [ACAD] — [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1)
- Li et al., "Catching the Rug" (arXiv 2608.20271): its feature list (Table III) is trading and liquidity-dynamics features from the first minutes (transaction counts, buy/sell percentages, unique buyers and sellers, early price change, price std). The paper states it omits bundle-level and wash-trading features. XGBoost TVL-or-idle label: MCC 0.2914 (Raydium), 0.3562 (PumpFun), 0.3888 (union), 0.3947 (fusion to PumpFun); cross-venue MCC about -0.003 to -0.24; PumpFun test set about 81.9% rug. The authors say results are "not yet sufficient for real-world deployment." Correction to earlier notes: this paper contains no holder-concentration, creator, bundle or funder features, so it gives **no** evidence for or against cluster signals; the label text also contradicts itself (idle over 80% of lifetime vs a ratio "close to 0.5"). [ACAD] — [Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20271v1)
- Luo et al., arXiv 2601.08641 (v2 Jan 2026, WWW '26): defines a "Bundle Bot" flag as any non-creator buy in the creator's launch block and a "Sniper Bot" flag as non-creator buys within 5 blocks. Descriptively, bundle flags occur in about one quarter of 6,000 projects and are associated with lower returns and shorter dump durations; sniper flags occur in most projects with "limited observable impact." Not a causal estimate. [ACAD] — [Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- Marino et al. (arXiv 2602.14860, Feb 2026; 655,770 pump.fun tokens, Sept 2025): graduation rate 0.63% (2.55% among tokens with at least 30 swaps); 92.22% of tokens with at least 30 swaps show at least one "dump" under their rule; creator-history conditioning is ambiguous (section VII vs conclusion disagree). No holder-concentration feature. Useful only as a base-rate: dumps are the norm, so "clustered token did not dump" is the rare class for any signal. [ACAD] — [Marino et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)
- "Meme Coin Factories" (arXiv 2609.10246, submitted 2026-09-09, accepted CCS'26): funding-cluster analysis of all pump.fun coins over two years. Creator clustering by common funder at 1, 2, 3 hops (Arkham-labelled exchanges, bridges and contracts removed). Top 1% of creators own 38.89% of coins unclustered; top 1% of clusters own 52.99% (1 hop), 57.47% (2 hops), 58.57% (3 hops). Multi-address clusters account for 62.99% of coins at 1 hop and 68.78% at 3 hops; 322,620 multi-address clusters at 1 hop. Graduation: wash-traded coins 2.0% vs 0.90%; copycats 0.86% vs originals 9.20%. It does not report holder concentration, bundle share or price collapse by cluster. [ACAD] — [Meme Coin Factories](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1)

**Industry measurement (large, vendor-run)**
- Bitquery investigation, figures verified 2026-10-10, launches 2025-10-30 to 2026-10-07, 8,212,568 coins (Mayhem agent excluded). Someone other than the dev bought in block 0 on 38% of coins and within three blocks on 50%. The dev's own wallets were in block 0 on 21% of coins, sniper bots on 28%, both on 12%. On bundled launches the dev wallets bought a **median 10% of supply** in the first blocks, and more than half of supply on 2% of bundled launches. Dev-bundle detection rule: buyer in the dev's launch transaction, fee paid by the dev, or in the next four transactions (matching the 5-transaction Jito bundle limit); spot check: 185 of 186 such purchases in those positions were in the launch bundle and none of 139 further down were. [VEND] — [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)
- Same Bitquery study, outcomes: devs and bundled wallets made at least $579M after fees on sold tokens (through 2026-06-30), ahead on 71% of bundled launches; the best 1% of bundled launches made 65% of the profit. The dev's first sale came within a minute on 65% of launches and within a day on 90%. Sniper-bot wallets (25,882 wallets, $440M spent): +$31.5M on sold tokens, 54% of wallets ahead; unlinked early buyers +$22.7M. For later launches, 98% had dev wallets and the wallets they funded sell at least 90% of what they bought within three days. Bitquery explicitly does **not** report rug rate or post-launch price of bundled tokens, and does not measure retail losses. [VEND] — [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)
- Bitquery caveats on false positives: a bundle shows packing with the launch transaction, not ownership; a launch service sending bundles for customers looks identical; dev wallets funded via exchanges or buying later are missed; wallets that received tokens by transfer are excluded (profit is a floor). [VEND] — [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)
- Pine Analytics (Apr 2025, one month to 2025-04-21): deployer-funded same-block snipers, 87% of snipes profitable, 85% of positions closed within five minutes. Already in earlier notes; the closure timing is the new detail here. [VEND] — [CoinPaprika summary of Pine](https://coinpaprika.com/education/launchpad-red-flags/) (secondary; primary: [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines))

**False-positive side (what the sources say, none quantify)**
- Bubblemaps: a link shows a transaction happened, not why; clusters arise from presales, marketing payments, friends withdrawing from the same exchange, or one investor's wallets; CEX, DEX and LP bubbles are among the largest and are not insider positions. A 25% cluster could be a locked VC or team wallet. [FOR/VEND] — [Bubblemaps holder guide](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/), [Find Deeper Connections](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/find-deeper-connections-between-wallets/)
- Bubblemaps (19 Mar 2026): "No tool is perfect"; clusters are inferred "likely controlled by the same entity"; healthy distribution can coexist with contract backdoors; off-chain coordination is invisible. [FOR/VEND] — [Bubblemaps, How to spot a rug pull](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-spot-a-rug-pull-using-bubblemaps-before-it-happens/)
- Edel Finance (2025): Bubblemaps alleged a 160-wallet cluster; the co-founder replied that the team planned to acquire 60% of supply. Only the denial is documented, not a verdict. [PRESS] — via [Cointelegraph, MYSTERY article](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-flags-mystery-over-90-wallet-launch-sniping-cluster)
- Bubblemaps' September 2026 revamp (feed that removes any token showing a bundle or insider cluster): the coverage reports no accuracy, false-positive rate or token counts; the article's own stated test is false negatives (tokens that pass and then rug). [PRESS, press-release derived] — [HackerNoon, 2026-09-16](https://hackernoon.com/bubblemaps-rebuilds-its-platform-to-screen-out-rug-pulls-before-traders-ever-see-the-token)
- Mobula, Solana Tracker, Conyr all say a bundle reveals coordination, not intent; legitimate bundles include launch setup (mint plus first buy), disclosed team allocations, and market makers spreading inventory. [VEND] — [Solana Tracker](https://www.solanatracker.io/blog/bundlers), [Conyr](https://conyr.ai/blog/detecting-coordinated-bundles-on-solana)
- Unverified, do not use: the "98.6% of pump.fun tokens rug" figure circulates in Bubblemaps-related press (HackerNoon) and is a died-before-graduating metric (prior-note correction); MadeOnSol's page gives it only in a teaser without method. [PRESS/VEND] — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/how-to-score-pumpfun-token-60-seconds)

### Inferences
- Threshold meaning depends on the bundle definition. Under MemeTrans' combined definition the average bundled supply at migration is 36.5%, so a fixed 25% cutoff on that definition would reject a large fraction of migrated tokens, not a tail. Under Bitquery's launch-transaction definition the median is 10% and more than half of supply is rare (2%), so a 25% cutoff would reject a small tail (the share above 25% is not reported). GodsEye must first fix which definition its "bundle 25%" uses; the two give very different reject rates (my reasoning from the two sources).
- MemeTrans precision/recall of 0.85/0.83 at a 74-84% base rate is a lift of roughly 1.1x over "predict everything is high-risk" (my arithmetic from earlier-note figures: precision 0.8459 vs base 74.18% in the experimental subset). Cluster features therefore help rank tokens (the top-100 selection result) more than they classify them.
- Because 92% of tokens with at least 30 swaps show a dump (Marino) and 74-84% of migrated tokens are high-risk (MemeTrans), the useful question for a copier is the conditional magnitude and exit speed, not whether a dump occurs. No source measures that conditional by cluster share.

### Gaps
- No risk-by-quantile table (cluster share, bundle share, top-10 share) from any source. MemeTrans publishes medians by risk group, not rates by bucket.
- No error rates for Bubblemaps cluster or bundle labelling, or for GMGN/RugCheck/Axiom tags.
- No measured false-positive rate for "clustered but did not dump" tokens; only anecdotes.
- Bitquery does not report price outcomes of bundled tokens; the 21% / 10% / 65% figures describe frequency and owner behaviour only. Its September 2026 jump in bundled-wallet purchases ($56.4M in June to $1.2B in September, 61% of September's in single purchases of 1,000 SOL or more) is unexplained in the article and may reflect volume-botting rather than supply capture.

## Q2. Case studies: what the cluster signature showed before the collapse and how much warning existed

### Takeaway
In every well-documented case the cluster was visible at or near launch, but the warning times are poorly documented: LIBRA gave roughly a day, HAWK hours, and TRUMP's single fresh sniper was not a cluster at minute 1. Several of the 2026 cases show clusters holding supply for days or months without a collapse, so a minute-1 cluster flag does not time the dump.

### Cited Findings
- **LIBRA (2025-02-14/15)** [FOR]: Bubblemaps' first-hour findings were 82% of supply in one wallet cluster, no vesting or treasury, and over $25M of fees in the pools. The exit began early on 15 Feb with one-sided LIBRA pools added and USDC and SOL removed; about $87M extracted, token down 85% at time of thread. The article gives no minute-level bubble-map snapshots, so minute 1/5/30 claims cannot be supported. Cluster addresses dormant for nine months swapped $60M USDC to SOL in Nov 2025 (as reported in the same article). The collapse mechanism was a liquidity pull on a CLMM pool, not a sellable cliff. — [Bubblemaps, LIBRA playbook](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/the-libra-playbook-how-one-cluster-drained-87-million-in-a-single-hour/) (read via a summarising fetch; exact hour of the exit not given)
- **MELANIA (2025-01-19)** [FOR via PRESS]: 82% of supply in one cluster at launch, with a presale wallet holding another 10% that dumped most of it on day one. — [HackerNoon, 2026-09-16](https://hackernoon.com/bubblemaps-rebuilds-its-platform-to-screen-out-rug-pulls-before-traders-ever-see-the-token) (secondary; earlier notes cite the primary Bubblemaps post)
- **HAWK / Hawk Tuah (launch 2024-12-04)** [FOR]: first Bubblemaps chart on launch day showed 96% of supply in one cluster; hours later the team still held 80%+; the presale breakdown (285 presale wallets receiving 17%) was published about a day after launch; 155 of the 285 sold, realising about $3.3M; market cap about $500M to about $25M. The page gives no collapse timestamp and no sell times, so seconds/minutes of warning cannot be derived. — [Bubblemaps, HAWK](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/hawk-anatomy-of-a-celebrity-rug/)
- **TRUMP-adjacent (Jan 2025)** [PRESS, SNIP]: wallet "6QSc2" was funded with over $1M SOL about four hours before Trump's announcement, bought over 5.9M TRUMP within 30 seconds, then split holdings across 10 wallets and began selling next day; by the following Monday it had sold $85M and held $75M; a $45M TRUMP sale to buy MELANIA moved TRUMP down nearly 50%. Decrypt could not confirm insider information. A single fresh wallet is one bubble, not a cluster, at minute 1; the split into 10 wallets is the later fragmentation Bubblemaps itself lists as a dump sign. — [Decrypt via search results](https://decrypt.co/302126/lucky-crypto-trader-over-100-million-trump-meme-coin)
- **Rugproof (Bubblemaps post 2025-07-28)** [FOR via PRESS]: creator sent SOL to 162 wallets that then bought 50% of supply at launch. The team said the token redistributes SOL to users; Bubblemaps said that could explain the pattern but noted the distributing address was a private wallet. Price outcome is not reported. — [Cointelegraph, 2025-07-29](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-rugproof-launchpad-alleged-rug-pull)
- **AVA AI (Pump.fun launch 2024-11-13; Bubblemaps post 2025-12-19)** [FOR via PRESS]: 23 wallets linked to the deployer, funded in a tight window via Bitget and Binance, sniped about 40% of genesis supply. The token's all-time high was on 2025-01-15 (two months after launch) at $0.33, and it is down over 96% from that high and over 79% from launch. The cluster was revealed about 13 months after launch using Time Travel, i.e. no real-time warning, and a minute-1 flag would have been followed by a two-month run-up before the decline. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-ava-ai-token-sniping-deployer-wallets-solana)
- **MYSTERY (article 2026-05-06)** [FOR via PRESS]: 90 newly funded wallets (funded by one wallet that withdrew 20 ETH from Binance) bought 90% of supply at launch; they sold about $100,000 and still held about 40%; market cap peaked at $7.5M on 28 April and was down about 75% to $1.9M at publication. Bubblemaps called it a "textbook scam." The seller held a large share for over a week while the price fell, i.e. the dump was incomplete and slow. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-flags-mystery-over-90-wallet-launch-sniping-cluster)
- **FOCAI (date not confirmed)** [PRESS, SNIP]: Lookonchain tracked at least 15 suspected insider wallets that bought over 60% of supply and sold for about $20M. — [Cointelegraph via search](https://cointelegraph.com/news/focai-memecoin-insider-trading-solana-profit)
- **LAPTOP (2026-09-09)** [FOR via PRESS]: Bubblemaps reported about 60% of top holders were fresh wallets funded within the prior 10 days, most on launch day, and more than 80% of 11,500+ buying wallets in the red; token fell more than 99% from peak within roughly an hour of a launch at around 08:00 ET. Founders hold 30% locked six months then vesting; the team blamed sniper bots and thin liquidity. **This appears to be a Base token, not Solana** (the coverage mentions Aerodrome pools and a Base explorer; my inference from that, not an explicit statement). It is relevant only as a "fresh-wallet share of top holders" example. The two news pages disagree on price figures. — [The Block](https://theblock.co/news/markets/2026-09-09-hunter-biden-laptop-airdrops-briefly-worth-over-1-million-each-before-99-crash-414034), [Parameter](https://parameter.io/laptop-memecoin-plummets-98-hours-after-launch-amid-bot-activity/)
- **CAR**: no source found. No cluster-signature write-up located despite a direct search.

### Inferences
- Warning time, where measurable, was measured in hours to about a day for celebrity/large-cap launches (LIBRA, HAWK) because the actors needed liquidity to build before exiting. That is not the regime GodsEye copies. Bitquery (dev sells within a minute on 65% of launches) and Pine (85% of sniper positions closed in five minutes) point to the small-cap regime having far shorter windows, but those two sources do not cover the same token set as the case studies.
- Case selection bias: all case studies are chosen because they collapsed. The two non-collapsing-for-weeks examples (AVA's two-month run, MYSTERY's slow drift with 40% unsold) are the only evidence about how a cluster signal can be right about the actor and wrong about the timing.
- The Bubblemaps LIBRA page, the HAWK page and the HackerNoon article give no precision data; I found no case where a cluster was flagged at launch, the token did not dump, and the flag was later retracted (aside from the Edel denial).

### Gaps
- No minute-by-minute bubble-map snapshots for any case (LIBRA's page states no minute-level views).
- CAR and any Hawk Tuah sell-time-in-minutes not found; no 2026 Solana case with a documented seconds-to-minutes warning interval found.
- TRUMP-adjacent details are search-snippet level.

## Q3. Which signals are strongest, and what thresholds have been published (with provenance)?

### Takeaway
Ranked by evidence quality: launch-transaction bundle share (best measured, but only as frequency and owner behaviour), fund-flow plus bundle merged share (MemeTrans, the only academic support), then funder-cluster and creator-linked supply (CCS'26 supports that funding clusters dominate creation, not that they predict loss). Raw top-N share and fresh-wallet share have the weakest support. All published cutoffs lack provenance.

### Cited Findings

**Published thresholds (all unvalidated; provenance noted)**

- Bitquery's measured distribution is the only data point against which these can be located: median dev bundle 10% of supply on bundled launches, >50% on 2% of them. **Signal-by-signal evidence**
- Launch-block bundle share (dev-linked): measured prevalence and behaviour (Bitquery; Luo et al. one quarter of projects, lower returns and shorter dump durations). Strongest evidence on both frequency and the owner's speed to sell; no price-outcome link published.
- Merged fund-flow + co-purchase + Jito bundles: only academic support (MemeTrans ablation); definitions untested for error. Fund-flow alone is the biggest slice (28.22% of supply).
- Connected-wallet share of supply (Bubblemaps cluster): forensic use only; no error rate.
- Same-funder share / funding clusters: supported structurally by CCS'26 (clusters concentrate creation 52.99% to 58.57% in the top 1%) and by AVA, Rugproof and MYSTERY (exchange-funded or creator-funded rings). The CCS'26 authors removed Arkham-labelled exchanges and stopped at three hops because coverage barely grew past two.
- Creator-linked holders: Pine's deployer-funded snipers (87% profitable), Bitquery's dev-bundle definition (fee payer = dev).
- Fresh-wallet share: only LAPTOP (Base) and AVA (zero prior history); the Dec 2025 AVA detection used amount, timing and no prior activity rather than a common upstream funder. No validated rate.
- Top-N concentration: weakest incremental value in MemeTrans (removing holding features costs 0.4 AUPRC points); bundle-adjusted top-10 differs sharply by risk group (24 vs 6 points).
- Holder count versus cluster count: Chen's rug tokens have a median of 9 holders; MemeTrans' filter dropping tokens with sale under 1 minute or under 100 holders removed 47.8% of tokens, 95% of them high-risk (earlier note). No source reports holder-count-per-cluster as a feature.
- Shared-CEX-withdrawal patterns: Bubblemaps AVA case (Bitget and Binance, tight window, similar amounts). Nansen treats the same CEX deposit address as high confidence (>90%) with no caveat on pooled deposit addresses (earlier note).

### Inferences
- A copier should treat bundle share and funder-cluster share as two objects (earlier note's point, reinforced by Bubblemaps, Solana Tracker, Conyr). Under Bitquery's definition roughly 1 in 5 launches already has dev wallets in block 0, so "bundle present" alone would reject a large share of candidates; magnitude and the owner's sell behaviour carry the information.
- Recommended starting posture (my synthesis, not a published result): log raw values under each definition (Bitquery-style launch-transaction bundle, MemeTrans-style merged, Bubblemaps-style connected-cluster) before choosing any cutoff; use published figures only as priors (10% launch-bundle, 20-30% cluster, 30% top-10 excluding pool/burn/exchange).
- Convergence of vendors at 10% (bundle) and 20% (cluster/bundler) is consistent with copying each other's heuristics; there is no independent confirmation.

### Gaps
- No threshold in any table above has a derivation, error rate or base-rate adjustment.
- Whether Bubblemaps' cluster share excludes CEX/LP/program accounts in the percentage calculation (earlier gap) remains unresolved; its blog says those bubbles are not insiders but not how the sum is built.
- No published data for creator-linked-holder share, shared-CEX-withdrawal share or fresh-wallet share as quantitative predictors.

## Q4. Evasion and blind spots

### Takeaway
Evasion is commoditised: bundler sellers advertise "clean Bubblemaps profile" outcomes, open-source bundlers and "disperse" tools exist, and forensic and vendor sources describe splitting, staggering and differently-routed funding. No source measures how much supply is hidden this way, and several blind spots (escrow/program accounts, sell-and-rebuy, Token-2022 vaults) have no evidence either way.

### Cited Findings

**Documented evasion**
- Bubblemaps' own list of evasion tactics (19 Mar 2026): star patterns, chain funding (A funds B funds C holds the tokens), mirrored trading, bundling, internal shuffling, dormancy then reactivation, deployer fan-out then joint dump, and "staying under alert thresholds by splitting holdings." [FOR/VEND] — [Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-spot-a-rug-pull-using-bubblemaps-before-it-happens/)
- Conyr (2026-06-12, updated 2026-08-26): dispersion across about forty wallets each under 1% (40 x 0.7% = 28% of float); staggered entries over minutes or hours with randomised sizes and different DEX routes; varied funding paths and pre-funded snipers; atomic Jito bundles; staged exits from many wallets in a few slots. It also warns single-hop tracing "produces garbage" when many wallets share an exchange funder, and relies on a 128-origin exchange registry. [VEND] — [Conyr](https://conyr.ai/blog/detecting-coordinated-bundles-on-solana)
- Luo et al.: gradual (non-launch-block) bundles are "substantially more difficult to detect accurately" and their pipeline falls back to candlestick visuals. [ACAD] — [Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- Chen et al.: multi-account wash trading is used to forge low top-holder concentration; scammers disperse tokens into multiple addresses to evade monitoring. [ACAD] — [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1)
- Pine Analytics: multi-hop funding of 5-7 hops excluded from its study for cost (earlier note). CCS'26: clustering stops at three hops because coverage barely grows after two; a leave-one-address-out test shows the largest remaining component keeps a median 80.4% of addresses, i.e. clusters are somewhat robust to removing a hub. [ACAD] — [Meme Coin Factories](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1)
- Bubblemaps AVA: CEX-funded wallets (Bitget, Binance) were still linked by narrow timing, similar SOL amounts and zero history, so CEX funding alone does not defeat Bubblemaps — but the detection came 13 months after launch. [FOR via PRESS] — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-ava-ai-token-sniping-deployer-wallets-solana)
- Bubblemaps: only the top 250 holders are loaded and the connection rule is any historical SOL transfer between wallets (earlier notes), so supply below the top-250 cut is invisible by construction.

**Blind spots and false-positive generators**
- LP and bonding-curve accounts as holders: Bubblemaps says CEX/DEX/LP bubbles are among the largest and are not insiders; CoinPaprika's top-10 rule explicitly excludes pool, burn and exchange wallets. [FOR, PRESS] — [Bubblemaps guide](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/), [CoinPaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/education/launchpad-red-flags/)
- Mayhem mode: creators opt in at launch; the feature mints an extra 1 billion tokens for an autonomous agent that trades in the first 24 hours with size and frequency caps and no protocol fees, and unused agent tokens are burned after 24 hours. Bitquery excluded the Mayhem agent from its census. The articles do not state the agent's share of supply or describe a vault, so the agent-vault concentration problem noted earlier is plausible but not documented here. [PRESS, VEND] — [The Block, 2025-11-18](https://www.theblock.co/post/379285/pump-funs-new-mayhem-mode-fails-boost-token-launches-revenue), [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)
- Bundle != owner: a launch service sending bundles for customers is indistinguishable from a dev's own bundle (Bitquery); legitimate Jito use produces same-slot sequential transactions from unrelated addresses (Conyr). [VEND]
- Exchange-funded or pre-existing rings can be missed if the detector discards CEX funders (AVA case; earlier note).
- A burned LP blocks only pool withdrawal, not creator selling or sniping (CoinPaprika). [PRESS]

### Inferences
- Evasion is cheap relative to detection. Splitting 28% of supply into forty sub-1% wallets defeats any single-wallet cap and only a timing or funding link catches it; staggering and independent CEX funding defeat both timing and funder links, leaving only behavioural similarity and later co-selling (my reasoning from Conyr and Bubblemaps). Since the cluster signal is only as good as the link rule, a clean map is weak evidence of absence while a dense cluster is moderately strong evidence of presence.
- The adaptation is already reflected in vendor products: Bubblemaps' Time Travel and the Sept 2026 "insider score" and pre-screened feed push detection earlier and further; the evasion sellers target their tags. No source compares them head to head.
- A defender can exploit what evasion cannot hide: the cluster still has to sell. Pine (85% of sniper positions closed within five minutes) and Bitquery (65% first dev sale within a minute) mean co-selling from earlier-flagged wallets may be the most reliable post-buy trigger (design idea, not tested).

### Gaps
- No source on cluster-evasion services other than the unopened listings above; none opened and verified.
- No evidence found on wallets that sell and re-buy to reset cluster maps, tokens whose supply sits in program/escrow accounts (apart from the Mayhem agent), or Token-2022 specifics affecting bubble-map calculation.
- No measurement of what share of supply is hidden from Bubblemaps in practice.
- Bubblemaps' own handling of top-250 truncation and pre-migration bonding-curve accounts is not documented in the pages read.

## Q5. Does a clustered holder base predict loss for a copier entering minutes after launch? Evidence on copy-trade or sniper-follower outcomes by cluster share

### Takeaway
No source conditions follower outcomes on cluster share. The nearest evidence says owners of bundles sell very early, copiers earn much less than the wallets they copy, and the survivors of the first minutes are mostly not the cluster. That makes a mid-cluster token look worse for a late copier on theory but is not measured.

### Cited Findings
- Luo et al. (arXiv 2601.08641, Jan 2026): smart-money trades averaged +14% in their data and estimated copier return was +3% per trade after realistic frictions; statistic-driven models gave negative copier returns and only the multi-agent system was positive; bundle flags (about one quarter of projects) are associated with lower returns and shorter dump durations (descriptive, not causal). Delay parameters were not visible in the text I received. [ACAD] — [Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- Bitquery: unlinked early buyers were net +$22.7M and 54% of sniper wallets were ahead; devs and bundles sell fast (first sale within a minute on 65% of launches); 88 sniper wallets lost over $10,000 and 8,239 lost under $100. Retail losses were not measured. [VEND] — [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)
- Pine Analytics: 85% of deployer-funded sniper positions closed within five minutes; snipes profitable 87% of the time. [VEND via secondary] — [CoinPaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/education/launchpad-red-flags/)
- LAPTOP (Base) and MYSTERY: 80% of 11,500+ buyers lost money (Bubblemaps, LAPTOP); the MYSTERY cluster sold only about $100,000 of a 90% position over about eight days while price fell about 75%. [FOR via PRESS] — [The Block](https://theblock.co/news/markets/2026-09-09-hunter-biden-laptop-airdrops-briefly-worth-over-1-million-each-before-99-crash-414034), [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-flags-mystery-over-90-wallet-launch-sniping-cluster)
- YZY (Aug 2025, earlier notes): 73.8% of traders lost money per Bubblemaps data, with 11 wallets taking nearly 30% of profits. [FOR]
- MemeTrans' label window starts at migration; any entry after migration into a token with a high merged-bundle share inherits that exposure, but the paper does not condition outcomes on the bundle level at entry. [ACAD] — [MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480)
- Marino et al.: conditioning on "successful traders" having bought gives only a modest uplift over the baseline graduation probability and remains below the buy-and-hold breakeven curve, described as a "double-edged signal." [ACAD] — [Marino et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)

### Inferences
- A copier entering about 60 seconds after a leader enters after the 0-5 block layer has bought; if the owner of that layer sells within a minute (Bitquery: 65% of launches) the copier is the exit liquidity in the window where the cluster signal is strongest. This is inference from two aggregate statistics, not a copier-specific measurement.
- The gap between leader and copier returns (14% vs 3% in Luo) suggests the cluster signal is more useful for avoiding the specific wallets that sold first than for forecasting token direction; one practical form is "is a pre-flagged cluster member still holding," which can be measured at entry, but no study tests it.
- A cluster signal has to beat the base rate: with 74-92% of fresh tokens dumping anyway, even a genuinely predictive cluster flag changes a bad base rate modestly. The value is likely in magnitude and speed of the drawdown, which GodsEye can measure only with its own paths.

### Gaps
- No study, dataset or dashboard measuring copier or follower outcomes by cluster/bundle share at entry, at any delay.
- Luo et al.'s copier delay parameters (appendix A.6) and the leave-out effect of filtering bundle-flagged tokens on copier return were not retrievable.
- No Dune dashboard of bundle share against outcome found despite search; the University of Sydney / SBFC 2025 paper (84% of 6,000 coins with a sniper in five blocks) that CoinPaprika cites could not be located or verified, so do not rely on it.
- Not retrievable: Bubblemaps insider-score documentation (invezz, CryptoRank and CryptoTimes pages returned 403), and the primary Bubblemaps posts for MELANIA/TRUMP.
