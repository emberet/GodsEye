# Memecoin wallet tracking: advanced signals, ML, off-chain sources, open-source projects, data products (Solana, as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: GodsEye already has a >=3 smart wallets / 1h consensus signal, a 0.5x-1.5x lead-score weight loop and a SQLite wallet DB. Those are not researched here. Research budget was ~35 tool calls; several sub-questions came back empty and are listed under Gaps. Vendor blog posts (MadeOnSol, Bitquery, Allium, DEXTools, Apify actors) are marketing and are labelled as such.

---

## 1. Consensus / cluster-buy ("smart money flow") signals: definitions, parameters, validation, hit-rates, decay

### Takeaway
No independent, published backtest of a Solana "N smart wallets bought within T" signal was found, so hit-rates and decay horizons for it are unknown in the public record. The best-documented building blocks are (a) how vendors define and threshold consensus (net flow, exit rate, minimum count, entry market cap), (b) strong adjacent evidence from equity insider "cluster buying" research that clustering across independent insiders is more informative, and (c) strong evidence from KOL/influencer studies that attention-driven signals decay fast and often reverse.

### Cited Findings
- MadeOnSol (vendor, sells the API) exposes a per-token "KOL consensus" endpoint returning `total_kol_buyers`, `total_kol_sellers`, `kol_exit_rate` (share of buyers who also sold), `net_flow_sol` (buys minus sells), `median_entry_mc_usd`, `first_touch_wallet`; its example filter is >=3 KOL buyers, positive net flow, exit rate <0.3; its "strong signal" heuristic is 5+ buyers, <20% exit rate, >10 SOL net flow; it says the 5-KOLs-in-an-hour window is an illustration, not a documented parameter, and that consensus is "backward-looking" (confirmation, not first entry) — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-kol-consensus-scout-leaderboard-token-discovery) (vendor; thresholds are heuristics, not validated).
- Same vendor claims S-tier "first-touch" KOL tokens reach 3+ follow-on KOL buyers within 4 hours ~50% of the time vs a 14% baseline (~3.7x) and a median 12-second lead before a second KOL buys, from a 38-day / 491k-trade backtest whose methodology was not presented — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-kol-consensus-scout-leaderboard-token-discovery) (unverified; note the outcome measured is "more KOLs follow", not price return).
- MadeOnSol's KOL leaderboard ranks wallets by 30-day realized PnL (sells minus buys, SOL) with a 50-trade minimum, and itself warns that copy-bots disguised as smart money are a common costly mistake and that a 30-day window may not persist — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/top-solana-kol-wallets-to-copy-trade) (vendor).
- GMGN's tags (as documented by third-party skill pages, not GMGN's own docs): `smart_degen` = algorithmically flagged wallets with a record of profitable trades; `renowned` = KOL/fund/public-figure wallets (described as social/marketing signal, "not necessarily alpha"); token-level tags `sniper`, `bundler`, `rat_trader` (insider/sneak trading); holder vs trader distinction (holder = currently holds; trader = ever transacted); smart-money flagged as stronger alpha than KOL — [GMGN skill docs mirror](https://skills.cat/skills/gmgnai/gmgn-skills/gmgn-track), [gmgn-token mirror](https://skillselion.com/skills/gmgnai/gmgn-skills/gmgn-token). Exact tagging criteria not published (secondary source).
- Nansen defines Smart Money as performance-based (realized PnL, win rate, trade history) rather than size-based; tiered "Smart Trader" labels use top-ranked PnL over 30D/90D/180D/2Y windows (listed as EVM; Solana applicability unconfirmed); Solana-specific behavioural labels include "Early Memecoin Trader"; Nansen publishes no forward hit-rate for these signals — [Nansen label guide](https://www.nansen.ai/guides/wallet-labels-emojis-what-do-they-mean), [Nansen Academy labels 101](https://academy.nansen.ai/articles/2149924-labels-and-watchlists-101). A third-party explainer notes insider wallets can mimic Smart Money buying — [eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14800361-nansen-smart-money-how-it-works) (unverified).
- Adjacent equity evidence for the "cluster" idea: corporate insiders cluster trades with close colleagues and clustered purchases are followed by abnormal returns >2% in the next month; clustering is stronger when information asymmetry is high — [Alldredge & Blank 2019, J. Financial Research (IDEAS)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfnres/v42y2019i2p331-360.html). Kang, Kim & Wang (2018) find cluster purchases more informative than non-cluster trades (cited in the same search result, not independently opened).
- Entity-resolution evidence relevant to "one operator counted as many wallets": MemeTrans groups accounts via three heuristics — co-signing the same transaction, being funded by the same address (excluding CEXs), and sharing a Jito bundle ID — then merges overlapping clusters; bundled accounts held 36.5% of supply at migration — [MemeTrans, arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480).
- Pump.fun context for entry bands: <2% of tokens graduate from the bonding curve (Q4 2024); graduation at ~$100k market cap cited; Pump.fun-created tokens were 40-67% of daily Solana DEX transactions — [Mancino et al., arXiv 2512.11850](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11850v2).
- Decay evidence from KOL tweets (36k tweets, 180 influencers, 1,600+ tokens, to Dec 2022): +1.83% mean 1-day return, then cumulative -2.24% at 10 days and -6.53% at 30 days; effect strongest for low-cap tokens, self-described experts, positive sentiment and explicit buy calls — [Merkley et al., Review of Accounting Studies 2024 (IDEAS)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v29y2024i3d10.1007_s11142-024-09838-4.html), [TechFlow summary](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/17848) (horizon table is from the TechFlow summary of the working paper; exact horizon figures not verified against the paper).
- Telegram pump groups: 412 organised pump events (Jun 2018 - Feb 2019) analysed; authors built a pre-pump coin-likelihood model; the arXiv v1 (220 events) and USENIX version report different strategy returns (80% over three weeks vs up to 60% over 2.5 months) — [Xu & Livshits, USENIX Security 19](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/xu-jiahua), [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10109v1). Centralised-exchange era, not Solana; shows the "who is early" problem.

### Inferences
- Parameter variants worth testing (none have published validation): minimum independent-cluster count rather than wallet count; net-flow and exit-rate gates (vendor-style); median-entry-market-cap bands; recency weighting of each wallet's buy relative to the first buy (late joiners are confirming, not leading); buy size as a multiple of that wallet's own median buy (conviction); optional count of "first-touch" quality.
- Cluster independence is the key gap versus GodsEye's >=3-wallets rule: MemeTrans-style funding-source / co-signer / Jito-bundle clustering gives a ready-made way to collapse operators, and the equity insider literature suggests information is carried by independent but coordinated actors, so one operator with 5 wallets should count as 1.
- Because KOL and consensus signals appear to be mostly "confirmation" and the KOL evidence shows a 1-day pop then reversal, the useful horizon for a consensus signal is probably minutes to a few hours, with the existing 1h/6h/24h measurement likely capturing mostly the reversal tail. Add 5/15/30-minute checkpoints and compare to a matched baseline (tokens with similar age, market cap and volume but no consensus).
- Eventual hit-rate claims should be measured against a matched control, not against "all tokens", because >98% of Pump.fun tokens never graduate and a base rate of "pumps at least once" is high.

### Gaps
- No independent backtest of N-wallet/T-window consensus on Solana memecoins; no published hit-rates or decay curves (only the unverified MadeOnSol numbers).
- No source for optimal N, T, or entry market-cap band; no source comparing weighting schemes.
- GMGN, Nansen, Birdeye do not publish forward performance of their smart-money tags.
- Equity cluster-buy analogy was not verified beyond abstracts; Kang-Kim-Wang paper not opened.

## 2. Machine learning for wallet and token outcomes

### Takeaway
The credible published ML work is token-level risk (rug / high-risk launch) from first-minutes trading, holder-concentration and bundle features, using gradient boosting / tabular models with temporal splits; results are modest (MCC ~0.3-0.4 in-domain, near zero cross-platform). I found no published GNN wallet-labeling, learning-to-rank, bandit/Thompson-sampling copy-allocation, or regime-detection work specific to Solana memecoin wallets; for skill-vs-luck and shrinkage the relevant evidence is from fund-performance and backtest-overfitting literature.

### Cited Findings
- "Catching the Rug" (Li et al., arXiv 2608.20271, 20 Aug 2026): ~6.4M Solana tokens, Nov 30 2024 - Jun 30 2025; label = TVL drawdown >99% OR idle >80% of lifetime, evaluated at 1 hour; features from only the first 5 minutes (tx counts, buy/sell ratios, unique buyers/sellers, SOL values, price change over first 3 blocks, price std, timing of first buy/sell, max/min price); rolling 3-month training window, forward time split, Optuna tuning; models RF, XGBoost, MLP, FT-Transformer, TabTransformer, AutoInt — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20271), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20271).
- Same paper's results: in-domain MCC ~0.25-0.36, F1(pos) ~0.75-0.78, AUCPRC ~0.74-0.80; cross-DEX transfer (Raydium->PumpFun etc.) drives MCC to ~0 or negative; trees (RF/XGBoost) more robust than neural/transformer models cross-domain; training on fused Raydium+PumpFun data gave XGBoost MCC 0.3947 on PumpFun vs 0.3562 PumpFun-only; authors state results are "not yet sufficient for real-world deployment"; they dropped tokens already rugged within the 5-minute feature window — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20271).
- Class balance in that paper's test set: PumpFun 43,835 rug vs 9,711 non-rug; Raydium 2,931,946 vs 1,893,539 — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20271).
- MemeTrans (Hu et al., Georgia Tech, arXiv 2602.13480): 40k+ Solana launches that migrated to a DEX, 122 features in groups (contextual 6, holding concentration 59, market activity 22, bundle statistics 35, plus time-series); label = high risk if min post-migration price within 20 min / migration price <0.3 or manually judged manipulated (37.5% of 9,471 reviewed were manipulated); chronological 7:3 split; best model MLP+LGBM AUPRC 0.5821; market-activity features most important in RF importance and ablation, holding+bundle features next; time-series Transformer worst (AUPRC 0.4841); top-100 selection simulation cut average loss from 60.71% (random) to 26.64% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480); code/data at [git-disl/MemeTrans](https://github.com/git-disl/MemeTrans) (4 stars, no license shown, last push 2026-05-21 via GitHub API).
- MemeTrans insider facts: 98.7% of "create" events co-occur with developer buys at the lowest curve tier; first-10/20 buyers hold 17/19 points more supply for high-risk tokens; bundling raises median top-10 holdings by 24 points for high-risk vs 6 for low-risk; filter removed 19,835 tokens (sale <1 min or <100 holders), 95% of them high-risk; only migrated tokens studied — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480).
- SolRPDS (CODASPY 2025): 62,895 suspicious pools / 33,746 tokens, 2021-2024, from 3.69B transactions; ~75% of inactive tokens lived <1 day; classifier on inactive vs active reached AUC 0.987 (RF) — but label is derived from the same liquidity-activity features, so high scores are circular — [arXiv 2504.07132](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07132), dataset [DeFiLabX/SolRPDS](https://github.com/DeFiLabX/SolRPDS) (8 stars, no license shown, last push 2025-10-02).
- Manipulation prevalence: across 34,988 meme coins on Ethereum/BSC/Solana/Base, most high-return (>100%) tokens (~82.6-82.8%; two sources disagree on the decimal) show wash-trading / liquidity-pool price inflation indicators — [arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01963) (abstract fetch said 82.8%; search snippet said 82.6%).
- Related prior art cited in Li et al.: wash-trading features on BSC meme tokens, Random Forest PR-AUC 0.9185 with mean 3.8 h warning (Cao et al.); RugScreener temporal GNN on ERC-20 (Wu et al.); TON rug detection with gradient boosting in 5 minutes (Yaremus et al.) — as described in [arXiv PDF 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20271) (not independently opened).
- GNN for wallets: no Solana smart-money GNN paper found. Closest: a GraphSAGE+GATv2 Solana fraud model card on Hugging Face (not peer-reviewed) and an Ethereum stablecoin-AML wallet classification paper with 68 engineered node features; Elliptic notes positive-unlabeled learning is typical because labels are incomplete — [CredLayer model card](https://huggingface.co/ritesh-das/credlayer-solana-fraud-gnn), [StableAML arXiv 2602.17842](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17842), [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3550/neural-network/neural-network-architectures-for-on-chain-aml-risk-classification-and-entity-attribution.html).
- Skill vs luck / shrinkage: Barras-Scaillet-Wermers apply a false-discovery-rate approach to 2,076 US equity funds (1975-2006) and find ~75% of funds have zero alpha net of costs, with skilled funds largely gone after 1996; a later JF paper argues the method has low power and misclassifies many truly skilled funds — [Barras et al. 2010 (IDEAS)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i1p179-216.html), [critique summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/360544).
- Selection bias / multiple testing: Deflated Sharpe Ratio adjusts for number of trials, cross-trial variance of Sharpe ratios, skewness and kurtosis; Minimum Backtest Length is its companion — [Bailey & Lopez de Prado, SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551).
- Bandits: no paper on Thompson sampling / EXP3 for copy-trading leader allocation was found; only generic bandit papers and a TradingView educational Beta-Thompson script — [search evidence: Agrawal & Goyal-type TS papers e.g. arXiv 1111.1797](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.1797), [TradingView script](https://cn.tradingview.com/script/WbBroPO3/).
- Pump.fun profitability base rates: share of profitable wallets (realized PnL only, netted per wallet across tokens, Dune `amount_usd`, no bot filtering) was below 50% in most months Apr 2024 - late 2025 (low 30.08% Jun 2025), then 56.83% Feb 2026, 70.00% Mar 2026, 73.28% Apr 2026, with ~65% of April 2026 wallets earning only $1-$500; authors call the 2026 shift a hypothesis — [CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback). Jan 2025 Dune cut: 55,296 of 13.55M addresses >$10k realized profit, 293 >$1M (disputed because post-graduation and unrealized PnL excluded) — [Cryptometer summary](https://www.cryptometer.io/news/pump-fun-data-shows-most-traders-havent-locked-major-gains/).

### Inferences
- Robust vs overfit: features that survive across both MemeTrans and Li et al. are early market-activity/trading-dynamics features (buyer/seller counts, buy-sell ratios, price path) plus concentration/bundle features; deep sequence models underperformed simple tabular models in both; cross-venue transfer collapses. Anything evaluated with random K-fold, or with a label computed from the features, should be treated as overfit/circular (SolRPDS AUC 0.987 is an example of the latter).
- Li et al.'s F1(pos) ~0.78 looks weak relative to the PumpFun test prevalence (43,835 / 53,546 = ~82% rug); a trivial predict-all-rug rule would give F1 ~0.90, and an AUCPRC of ~0.76 is below that prevalence. This is my arithmetic from their tables; it suggests MCC is the only informative number and the paper's apparent signal is small. Treat as caution, not a refutation (metrics may be computed differently than described).
- A defensible use for GodsEye: a token-level risk/"bundled supply" veto on consensus signals (MemeTrans features: top-10 holdings, bundle share, dev pre-buy) rather than a price predictor.
- For wallet scoring, empirical-Bayes shrinkage of win-rate / mean-return toward a population prior (beta-binomial for win rate, normal-normal for log returns, tuned on a held-out period) is the standard fix for small-sample wallets; the Barras et al. result implies most top-ranked wallets over any window are lucky, so lead-score weights should be shrunk hard and re-estimated out-of-sample. (Method inference; not from a memecoin source.)
- Thompson sampling with a Beta posterior per leader on "followed trade beat a matched baseline net of fees/slippage" is a natural fit for the 0.5x-1.5x weight loop, with discounting (exponential forgetting) for non-stationarity; EXP3 is the adversarial alternative. No memecoin-specific evidence exists, so treat as a design idea to validate in simulation.
- Regime detection: no source found; plausible low-cost proxies are the share-of-profitable-wallets series (CoinGecko shows large regime swings 30% -> 73%) and Pump.fun graduation rate/volume, used to scale position size, not to select wallets.

### Gaps
- No Solana-specific, peer-reviewed work on GNN wallet labeling, learning-to-rank wallets, copy-trade bandits, or regime detection was found.
- Feature-importance rankings beyond group level were not extractable (MemeTrans appendix not fully read).
- MemeTrans "56.1% reduction" appears in secondary summaries; the paper's own numbers (60.71% -> 26.64% loss) imply ~56% relative reduction by my arithmetic, i.e. consistent, but the paper states it as ~34 points.
- Li et al.'s token counts in Table II were garbled in extraction and do not clearly sum to 6.4M.
- Whether any wallet-quality score (PnL, win rate) persists out-of-sample on Solana memecoins: no public evidence found.

## 3. Off-chain / alternative signals

### Takeaway
Evidence that off-chain attention predicts memecoin returns is weak and mostly negative at horizons beyond a day: influencer tweets show a one-day pop then reversal, and many high-return memecoins show manipulation indicators. Social-analytics vendors (Kaito, Cookie.fun, LunarCrush, Dexscreener trending) publish no validated predictive tests that I could find.

### Cited Findings
- Crypto-influencer tweets: +1.83% mean 1-day, negative 10- and 30-day cumulative returns (-2.24%, -6.53%); a 30-day hold of small tokens bought on tweet day lost ~7.9% on average in the authors' estimate; stronger for large-follower self-described experts, positive sentiment, buy calls, low-cap tokens — [Merkley et al. 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v29y2024i3d10.1007_s11142-024-09838-4.html), [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/17848). Sample ends Dec 2022 and is pre-Solana-memecoin-boom (older material).
- ~82.6-82.8% of >100%-return meme coins show wash-trading or LPI manipulation indicators (cross-chain, 34,988 tokens), so social attention and price co-movement is confounded by coordinated activity — [arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01963).
- Telegram/Discord pump groups: ~100 organised Telegram channels averaging ~2 pumps/day and ~$7M/month artificial volume (2018 sample); Hamrick et al. covered Telegram and Discord with thousands of pumps — [Xu & Livshits](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10109v1), [Hamrick et al. (HUJI listing)](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/an-examination-of-the-cryptocurrency-pump-and-dump-ecosystem/). Pre-2020 and CEX-based.
- Kaito: described as information-discovery / narrative / share-of-voice tool; mindshare is used as an airdrop indicator; Cookie.fun: index/data layer tracking mindshare, engagement, on-chain activity; LunarCrush: Galaxy Score, AltRank. No backtest or independent performance analysis found — [DEXTools comparison](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/compare-crypto-tools-kaito-cookie-lunarcrush-de) (vendor tutorial), [4Pillars on Kaito](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/loud-is-a-kaito-testnet).
- Dexscreener trending score blends volume, liquidity, transactions, unique makers, holders, community engagement and trust signals; formula proprietary; boosts are paid promotion; no forward-return study found — [Dexscreener docs](https://docs.dexscreener.com/trending).
- Google Trends / Twitter sentiment: only a Universidade Nova de Lisboa master's thesis on Twitter-based Dogecoin forecasting (results not seen) and a Bitcoin-only paper linking Twitter emotions/Google Trends/YouTube search to returns; a 2023 Dogecoin logo-swap case shows co-movement, not prediction — [UNL thesis](https://run.unl.pt/handle/10362/150904), [UKM Bitcoin paper](https://www.ukm.my/jem/wp-json/wp/v2/article/8161), [Carma](https://carma.com/twitter-logo-change-and-its-impact-on-dogecoin/).
- MadeOnSol (vendor) argues that by the time a KOL tweets a Pump.fun token, the best entry is already gone — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/how-to-copy-trade-on-solana) (unmeasured assertion).

### Inferences
- Linking social mentions to wallet activity is best treated as a feature on top of the wallet signal (e.g., "consensus AND not yet heavily shilled" or "consensus preceding the first KOL mention"), because the tweet-day pop and later reversal suggest social attention marks late/exit liquidity.
- If testing alt-data, log first-mention timestamp vs first smart-wallet buy; the lead/lag ordering is the measurable quantity and can be done cheaply from the existing DB plus a mention feed.
- Mindshare indices are gameable (airdrop-incentivised, per sources), so they are likely noisier than on-chain flow.

### Gaps
- No evidence found on Farcaster or TikTok trend detection for Solana memecoins (not specifically searched beyond a general sentiment query).
- No Telegram/Discord "alpha group" study specific to Solana found.
- No independent test of Kaito/Cookie/LunarCrush/Birdeye social/Dexscreener trending predictive power.
- Narrative-detection (topic clustering) methods: nothing found.

## 4. Open-source projects worth learning from

### Takeaway
High-quality, actively maintained open source is concentrated in the infrastructure layer (Yellowstone gRPC, Carbon indexer, DEX parsers, streamers). Wallet-ranking/smart-money repos are small, young and unvalidated, and the popular "copy trading bot" repos are largely templates with inflated star counts; there is documented key-stealing malware in this niche, so audit before running anything.

### Cited Findings
(Stars / license / last push read from GitHub API on 2026-10-27 unless noted.)
- [rpcpool/yellowstone-grpc](https://github.com/rpcpool/yellowstone-grpc) — Triton's Dragon's Mouth Geyser gRPC streaming; Rust; 1,015 stars; AGPL-3.0; pushed 2026-10-26. The standard low-latency source for transaction/account streams; Quicknode also offers a Yellowstone-compatible service — [Quicknode guide](https://www.quicknode.com/guides/solana-development/defi/pump-fun-copy-trade).
- [sevenlabs-hq/carbon](https://github.com/sevenlabs-hq/carbon) — "indexing framework on Solana", Rust; 636 stars; MIT; pushed 2026-10-21. Pipeline/decoder architecture suitable for custom wallet-trade indexing.
- [0xfnzero/solana-streamer](https://github.com/0xfnzero/solana-streamer) — Rust DEX event streaming SDK over Yellowstone gRPC / Jito ShredStream / RPC parsing; 187 stars; MIT; pushed 2026-10-25. [0xfnzero/pumpfun-sdk](https://github.com/0xfnzero/pumpfun-sdk) — Pump.fun create/buy/sell + gRPC subscription; 83 stars; MIT.
- [Shyft-to/solana-defi](https://github.com/Shyft-to/solana-defi) — Yellowstone gRPC parsing examples for Pump, Meteora, Raydium; Rust; 354 stars; no license shown; pushed 2026-10-06. [lamports-dev/richat](https://github.com/lamports-dev/richat) — Geyser/Yellowstone stream fan-out; 150 stars.
- [cxcx-ai/solana-dex-parser](https://github.com/cxcx-ai/solana-dex-parser) — TypeScript parser for Jupiter, Raydium, Meteora, PumpFun, Moonshot swaps; 114 stars; MIT; last push 2025-10-02 (possibly stale vs new Pump AMM versions). Go port: DefaultPerson/solana-dex-parser-go (15 stars, MIT).
- Wallet analytics: [OkoyaUsman/solana-wallet-analyzer](https://github.com/OkoyaUsman/solana-wallet-analyzer) — Python; PnL, win rate, holding period filters with min-capital/win-rate thresholds; Birdeye prices; MIT (per search result, stars not checked). [Arboff/solana-pnl-tracker](https://github.com/Arboff/solana-pnl-tracker) — small MIT PnL tracker for own trades. [1f1n/Dragon](https://github.com/1f1n/Dragon) — scrapes top holders/traders for PnL and win rate on SOL/ETH/BSC, ~800 days old, likely stale. [DoradoDevs/solana-wallet-pnl-tracker](https://github.com/DoradoDevs/solana-wallet-pnl-tracker) — FIFO cost-basis PnL with DEX trade parsing; MIT; 1 star; pushed 2026-07-02. [DracoR22/handi-cat_wallet-tracker](https://github.com/DracoR22/handi-cat_wallet-tracker) — Telegram wallet tracker, TypeScript, 206 stars, last push 2026-02-17.
- Smart-money-labelled repos are tiny (0-2 stars): e.g. [forgottenpeak/soulwinners](https://github.com/forgottenpeak/soulwinners) (Python, MIT, Telegram alerts), [MessengerPigeonn/solana-smart-tracker](https://github.com/MessengerPigeonn/solana-smart-tracker), [nvimts/awesome-solana-smart-money](https://github.com/nvimts/awesome-solana-smart-money) (address list, MIT) — none appear to publish validation.
- Copy-trade bots by stars: coffellas-cto/Solana-Copy-Trading-Bot (412 stars, Rust, no license, last push 2026-04-17), mortdeus/solana-copy-trading-bot (63), justFiveDev/Solana-Copy-Trading-Bot (49, Rust, pushed 2026-10-25), imcrazysteven/Solana-Copy-Trading-Bot (43, TypeScript, MIT) — from GitHub search; descriptions are promotional; architecture is generally "watch target wallet -> mirror buy/sell with sizing". Quicknode's guide documents the same pattern: monitor target wallet via Yellowstone gRPC, get base64 transaction from a Pump.fun API, sign and send — [Quicknode](https://www.quicknode.com/guides/solana-development/defi/pump-fun-copy-trade).
- Security warning: SlowMist traced a July 2025 theft to a fake "solana-pumpfun-bot" repo with inflated stars/forks and an obfuscated Node.js payload exfiltrating wallet/key files; a later Rust "pumpfun-pumpswap-sniper-copy-trading-bot" hid exfiltration in a config file that read the private key from `.env`; a similar Polymarket copy-bot case was reported Dec 2025 — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/solana-trading-bot-github-malware-scam), [MEXC News on SlowMist report](https://www.mexc.com/tr-CT/news/malicious-robots-reappear-in-solana-ecosystem-private-key-leak-trap-hidden-in-configuration-files/59206), [Polymarket case](https://www.mexc.com/news/318651). Star counts are not a trust signal in this niche.
- Datasets/research code: [git-disl/MemeTrans](https://github.com/git-disl/MemeTrans) and [DeFiLabX/SolRPDS](https://github.com/DeFiLabX/SolRPDS) (see section 2). A MemeTrans follow-on named MELT (41k+ launches, 200M+ transactions, separating swaps, wash trades, transfers, mints) appears on SOTAVerified; relationship to MemeTrans unconfirmed — [SOTAVerified](https://sotaverified.org/papers/260213480).

## 5. Data products and honest back-testing

### Takeaway
Full-history Solana wallet trades are obtainable from Dune, Bitquery, Allium, and Helius (raw), but none ship a backtester; Birdeye is a weak fit for wallet-level history. An honest backtest requires point-in-time wallet selection, inclusion of dead/losing wallets, routed-trade de-duplication, realistic delay and slippage, and multiple-testing control. Flipside coverage could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- Dune `dex_solana.trades`: raw trade events per liquidity-pool segment; routed (multi-hop) trades appear as multiple rows; `trader_id` is the swap initiator, `amount_usd` is USD value at execution with unspecified method, `trade_source` marks direct vs aggregator; page says nothing on freshness, bots or accuracy — [Dune docs](https://docs.dune.com/data-catalog/curated/dex-trades/solana/solana-dex-trades). Another Dune page lists ~3 hour refresh for Solana trading and ~30 minutes for generic DEX data (conflicting) — [Dune Solana trading](https://docs.dune.com/data-catalog/curated/solana/trading), [overview](https://docs.dune.com/data-catalog/curated/dex-trades/overview).
- Bitquery: GraphQL Solana DEX trades with per-wallet history; bulk Parquet delivery to S3/Snowflake/BigQuery advertised; its copy-trading tutorial states Bitquery "supplies data, not a built-in backtester" and the example only polls one leader's recent trades (no execution, no latency figures) — [Bitquery copy-trading tutorial](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/usecases/copy-trading-bot/), [Bitquery Solana DEX API](https://bitquery.io/products/solana-dex-api) (vendor).
- Allium: Solana raw blocks/transactions/instructions/inner instructions plus decoded DEX trades, liquidity events, pools; claims history from genesis and delivery to Snowflake/BigQuery/Databricks/S3 — [Allium Solana](https://www.allium.so/ecosystems/solana) (vendor claim).
- Birdeye: token market data (prices, trades, OHLCV, wallet portfolios, token security) but not full instruction/transaction context — per comparison pages by competitors, so treat as biased — [Bitquery vs Birdeye](https://www.bitquery.io/alternatives/bitquery-vs-birdeye), [MadeOnSol data API comparison](https://madeonsol.com/compare-data-apis).
- Helius: full ledger from genesis, archival RPC (`getBlock`, `getTransaction`, `getSignaturesForAddress`), plus `getTransactionsForAddress` with time/slot filters, bidirectional sort and token-account history (`getSignaturesForAddress` excludes associated token accounts); `getTransfersByAddress` is Developer plan or higher; Enhanced Transactions API is legacy/maintenance mode, with Wallet API and Parsed Events (open beta, paid) as successors — [Helius historical data](https://www.helius.dev/docs/rpc/historical-data), [Enhanced Transactions overview](https://www.helius.dev/docs/enhanced-transactions/overview.md).
- Flipside: no documentation surfaced in two searches.
- Methodology evidence: Li et al. use forward rolling windows by token issuance time (3-month training window), guarantee >=5 positives per validation fold, and drop tokens that already rugged inside the feature window, explicitly rejecting random K-fold because of look-ahead and concept drift — [arXiv PDF 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20271). MemeTrans uses chronological splits and simulates purchase at migration price with loss averaged over 100 random sell times in the next hour — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480).
- CoinGecko's wallet-PnL series shows how metric definitions change conclusions: realized-only, netted across tokens, Dune `amount_usd` (may be missing/stale for illiquid memecoins), no bot filtering — [CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback). Dune January 2025 analysis was disputed for excluding post-graduation and unrealized PnL — [Cryptometer](https://www.cryptometer.io/news/pump-fun-data-shows-most-traders-havent-locked-major-gains/).
- Multiple-testing control: Deflated Sharpe Ratio and Minimum Backtest Length (Bailey & Lopez de Prado); FDR-style luck correction (Barras et al.) — sources in section 2.

### Inferences
- Honest backtest checklist for wallet-copy signals (synthesised, not from a single source):
  1. Point-in-time wallet scores: rank wallets using only trades before the signal timestamp; walk-forward (train window -> next window) like Li et al.
  2. Survivorship: keep wallets that went inactive, were drained, or whose scores later collapsed; include rugged/dead tokens with terminal price zero.
  3. Delay model: add observed latency (slot lag, your RPC/stream delay, Jito tip/landing) and fill at the price N seconds/slots after the leader's fill, using pool state not mid-price; stress at 1, 5, 15, 30, 60 s.
  4. Costs: DEX fee, priority fee/tip, slippage as a function of pool depth and your size; Pump.fun bonding curve price impact; exit liquidity (can the position actually be sold).
  5. Data hygiene: de-duplicate routed trade legs (Dune), filter wash/bot flow, check `amount_usd` gaps for illiquid tokens, check data freshness for live-vs-historical parity.
  6. Cluster independence: collapse wallets sharing funders/co-signers/bundles into one vote before counting.
  7. Matched control and horizon grid: compare consensus tokens to matched non-consensus tokens by age, market cap, volume; report 1/5/15/60 min and 6/24 h; report medians and the share of tokens that go to near-zero, not only means (heavy tails).
  8. Multiple testing: log every (N, T, band, weight) variant tried and report deflated or FDR-adjusted significance; hold out the most recent period untouched.
  9. Reproducibility of the signal in the live DB: record the signal as seen at the time (ledger of emitted signals) so the learning loop is evaluated on what was actually knowable.
- Cheapest historical backfill path for GodsEye is probably Helius `getTransactionsForAddress` per tracked wallet (plus its own live DB), with Dune/Bitquery used for cross-checks and population-level base rates (leader wallets are a tiny share of all trades).

### Gaps
- Flipside, Birdeye and Allium pricing, rate limits and schema details were not verified from primary docs; Dune freshness is contradictory across pages; Dune history start date not stated.
- No published numbers on realistic copy-trade latency or slippage on Solana; no empirical follower-vs-leader return study was found.
- Helius filter parameter names and plan limits for backfill not documented in the fetched page.
