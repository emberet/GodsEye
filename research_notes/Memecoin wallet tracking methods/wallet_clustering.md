# Wallet-entity resolution and insider/coordination detection for Solana memecoins

Scope note: research run 2026-10-27, about 25 tool calls. Most vendor methodology pages are marketing-grade and give no thresholds or algorithms. Where a vendor's internals could not be found, that is stated under Gaps rather than guessed. Anything labelled "Inference" is my own engineering reasoning (including general Solana mechanics I know but did not source) and is not backed by a cited page.

## 1. Which on-chain heuristics link wallets, and what are their false-positive risks?

### Takeaway
Sourced evidence supports six signals: first funder, direct SOL transfers between wallets, same-block or same-window co-buys, same-atomic-transaction (bundle) buys, tight buy-size similarity, and consolidation of tokens or SOL into one wallet. Every vendor treats shared funding as necessary-but-not-sufficient and warns it can be innocent (shared CEX withdrawal, friends, team allocations). Fingerprints such as compute units, priority-fee bands, Jito tip amounts and ATA create/close patterns are described only as weak signals. I found no source quantifying false-positive rates for any heuristic.

### Cited Findings
**Funding-based signals**
- Helius Wallet API `GET /v1/wallet/{wallet}/funded-by` returns the sender of the wallet's earliest incoming SOL transfer, with funder, funderName, funderType, amount, date, signature and slot. A known funder such as an exchange comes back labelled. A wallet that never received SOL returns 404. It is beta, needs a paid plan, and costs 100 credits per call — [Helius funded-by](https://docs.helius.xyz/docs/wallet-api/funded-by.md)
- Solscan Pro API `account/funded_by` is the same idea (returns funded_by, txhash, timestamp), batches up to 50 addresses per call, and Solscan itself pitches "funding tree" grouping for sybil detection and entity clustering. The doc says the endpoint gives an address, not a real-world owner, and does not explain multi-hop handling or exchange labelling — [Solscan KB](https://kb.solscan.io/solscan-api-trace-solana-wallet-funders)
- Nansen's related-wallets template treats "First Funder", shared signers and the same CEX deposit addresses as high confidence (>90%). It rates high interaction volume or coordinated timing as medium (60-90%) and indirect or behavioural similarity as low (<60%). The doc does not explain how the percentages are derived and tells the reader to "filter out false positives from common protocol interactions" without a method — [Nansen docs](https://docs.nansen.ai/guides/templates/complex-use-cases/use-case-3-identifying-related-wallets-at-scale)
- Pine Analytics' pump.fun study used a "one-hop funding filter": same-block buyers (bought in the block the token was created) that also had a direct SOL transfer to or from the deployer before launch. In the month before 2025-04-21 this gave 15,000+ tokens, 4,600+ sniper wallets, 10,400+ deployers, about 1.75% of pump.fun launch activity, and 87% of snipes profitable. The author says it misses multi-hop funding (5-7 hops cited) and that wallet links "do not establish who controls the wallets" — [Pine Analytics, Exit Liquidity Machines](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines) (April 2025, vendor-adjacent blog, not peer reviewed)
- Bubblemaps' connection rule: two wallets are linked if there was at least one historical transaction between them in the chain's gas token (SOL on Solana). Link direction is dotted for one-way and solid for two-way. Only the top 250 holders are loaded. It also has a "native token" mode showing the token's own transfers, and clusters can look different in each mode — [Bubblemaps: Find Deeper Connections](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/find-deeper-connections-between-wallets/)
- Bubblemaps caveat: a link shows a transaction happened, not why. It can be a presale, marketing payment, or one investor's wallets — same source.

**Timing and co-buy signals**
- Bubblemaps separates *bundle* (temporal: wallets that bought in the same block or within seconds at launch, not necessarily funding-linked) from *cluster* (structural: shared funding, token transfers between wallets, shared patterns across multiple tokens, persisting over days to months). It says organic bundles are rare and "usually suspicious", and calls a bundle holding 10%+ of supply a major red flag — [Bubblemaps: Bundle vs Cluster](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/whats-the-difference-between-bundle-cluster-2/)
- Mobula's heuristics for bundlers: several wallets buying in one atomic transaction, a common funding source, buys in the same block, tight buy-amount range, tokens later consolidated into one wallet. A cluster of 3+ wallets with these patterns is a bundler; 10+ is red. For snipers: buy within 0-3 blocks of creation, fresh wallet funded shortly before launch, high priority fees or MEV bundles. Mobula does not define "tight range" or how many signals trigger a tag — [Mobula](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers)
- Mobula's insider signals: direct transactions with the deployer, buying before public announcement, funding from dev or known insider wallets, timing matching team wallets. It notes dev-funded wallets can be a planned team allocation rather than misconduct — same source.
- A third-party Solana clustering guide lists: transaction-graph expansion over 3-10 hops with pruning by value, token or time and then community detection (results treated as hypotheses); similar instruction shapes in tight windows; interaction fingerprints (repeated program paths and swap parameters, normalised for popular default routes); similar compute units or priority-fee bands (flagged "weak alone"); peel/dust hops; ATA create/close patterns; repeated wallet co-occurrence in Jito bundles and similar tip amounts (positional heuristics "fragile"); launchpad early-buyer graphs in the first 10-30s after mint, split buys, shared metadata URIs, coordinated post-launch sells. It names false-positive sources: default-route popularity, timing overlap by organic crowds, bundle overlap from searcher competition, dust noise. Its stated principle: "Prefer false negatives over false public accusations" — [Solana clustering skill guide](https://openskillindex.com/skills/neversight-learn-skills-dev-solana-clustering-advanced) (secondary/community skill listing, not a primary or peer-reviewed source)
- Jito mechanics relevant to bundle fingerprints: a bundle holds at most 5 transactions, executes atomically, tips should go to a randomly chosen tip account (from `getTipAccounts`) to reduce contention, and the tip is usually in the last transaction of the bundle — [Jito low-latency docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/) and [QuickNode Jito bundles guide](https://www.quicknode.com/guides/solana-development/transactions/jito-bundles)
- Because tip accounts are chosen at random by well-behaved clients, the tip *account* identity carries little information; this is why the clustering guide says positional/tip heuristics are fragile (inference supported by the two sources above).

**False-positive sources named in sources**
- Bubblemaps: clusters form organically, e.g. friends who withdrew from the same exchange; CEX, DEX and LP wallets are among the largest bubbles and are "not insider positions"; some wallets "will always snipe a launch"; wallet age and history are "only slightly informative" — [Bubblemaps meme coin holder guide](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/) and [Bubble vs Cluster](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/whats-the-difference-between-bundle-cluster-2/)
- Elliptic's clustering write-ups: shared infrastructure such as exchanges causes false positives; heuristics are "useful but not absolute"; recommend confidence scoring and separating clusters with direct control evidence from those based only on behavioural similarity — [Elliptic, Address Clustering Algorithms](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3495/software-development/address-clustering-algorithms.html) and [Elliptic, clustering for Ponzi/HYIP](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3678/digital-asset/on-chain-clustering-heuristics-for-identifying-crypto-ponzi-and-hyip-schemes.html) (vendor material, Bitcoin-heavy)
- The Dec 2025 Bubblemaps $AVA case: 23 wallets including the deployer were funded from Bitget and Binance shortly before launch, in narrow time windows with similar SOL amounts, had no prior activity, and sniped at launch. Note the shared funder here was *exchanges*, so a funder-grouping rule that discards CEX-funded wallets would miss it; the signal was amount similarity + timing + zero history, not a common upstream address — [Blockchain.News summary of Bubblemaps post](https://blockchain.news/flashnews/23-wallets-from-bitget-binance-sniped-ava-on-solana-bubblemaps-on-chain-alert-for-traders) (secondary aggregator; the Bubblemaps X post was not read directly; the aggregator's price and insider commentary is unverified)
- A Solana bot study (ASE '26, arXiv 2607.28424) excluded transfers to bot-controlled addresses used for self-funding and transfers involving protocol state accounts such as Solscan-labelled pools when analysing funding relationships. I could only read the metadata, not the body — [arXiv 2607.28424](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28424)

### Inferences
- A "first funder with >=25 wallets = exchange" rule is a reasonable popularity filter, but the $AVA case shows exchange-funded insider rings exist. Distinguish "funder is an exchange hot wallet (do not link on funder alone)" from "these wallets are an exchange-funded ring" by adding secondary signals (amount, timestamp, token set).
- Bot-platform fee and routing wallets (Trojan, BonkBot, Photon etc.) and popular funders behave like exchanges for clustering purposes: huge fan-out, so any signal that goes through them should be down-weighted by inverse degree. I found no source that documents these fee-wallet addresses or how they trigger false positives (see Gaps).
- Not sourced but standard Solana mechanics worth using: (a) ATA rent (about 0.00204 SOL) returned on account close, and "close-authority" or rent destination equal to a common wallet, is an ownership-ish signal because only the token account owner or close authority can close and redirect rent; (b) a transaction's fee payer and co-signers are an exact (not probabilistic) link when a single keypair pays fees for several wallets' swaps; (c) SOL sweeps from many wallets to one destination after a sell. All three should be validated before relying on them, and none were confirmed by a source in this run.
- Exact signals (shared fee payer, shared signer, one wallet closing another's accounts, direct two-way transfers) deserve very different weights from probabilistic signals (timing, amount similarity, compute-unit fingerprints, tip similarity). The sources agree on tiering but not on numbers.

### Gaps
- No source gives measured false-positive or precision/recall figures for any single heuristic (shared funder, same-slot, fee fingerprints).
- No source on Trojan/BonkBot/Photon/BullX fee-wallet or relay-wallet behaviour in clustering. The only hits were fee percentages and a 2024 drainer incident, which are off-topic.
- No source on CEX deposit-address reuse as a Solana-specific signal other than Nansen's generic template (which applies it to "same CEX deposit addresses" with >90% confidence, without caveats about custodial pooled deposit addresses).
- No documented treatment of rent-reclaim, ATA-create patterns or compute-budget fingerprints beyond the community guide's one-line "weak" assessments.
- The ASE '26 Solana bots paper (fingerprints) could not be parsed; PDF text extraction was unavailable. Worth reading directly (DOI 10.1145/3832783.3837522) for compute-budget/Jito/program-ID fingerprints.

## 2. How do Bubblemaps, Arkham, Nansen, RugCheck, Solscan/SolanaFM, GMGN, Birdeye, Helius and TrenchTools-style products detect and present clusters?

### Takeaway
Bubblemaps is the only tool with a clearly documented, simple linking rule (any gas-token transfer between top-250 holders; same colour = likely same entity; separate bundle-timing view). Nansen documents a confidence-tiered related-wallet workflow. Helius and Solscan expose first-funder lookups as raw primitives. For GMGN, RugCheck, Arkham, Birdeye and SolanaFM I found no primary documentation of how tags or clusters are computed, only that the outputs exist.

### Cited Findings
- **Bubblemaps**: bubbles sized by holdings, coloured by connected group, lines for gas-token transfers; separate "bundle" badge/tab showing percentage of supply bought at launch; bands for connected-cluster share (<15% reasonably healthy, 15-30% elevated, >30% high, >50% extreme) and bundled launch supply (<5% low, 5-15% moderate, >15% serious, >30% avoid). The article states these are "not hard rules", and a 25% cluster could be a locked VC or team wallet. Data comes from explorers such as Solscan — [Bubblemaps holder guide](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/), [Find Deeper Connections](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/find-deeper-connections-between-wallets/)
- **Bubblemaps cross-chain case**: in the LIBRA/MELANIA investigation Bubblemaps tied a Solana sniper wallet to an Avalanche wallet linked to MELANIA's creator, then saw the same EVM address fund LIBRA's creator wallet via cross-chain swaps, and concluded one entity likely controlled both. Reports in November 2025 say two addresses from the original LIBRA cluster reactivated after nine months and swapped $60M USDC for SOL. These are secondary reports; I did not read the primary Bubblemaps posts — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/341238/bubblemaps-links-single-entity-to-libra-and-melania-memecoins) and search summary of [Bubblemaps LIBRA Playbook](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/the-libra-playbook-how-one-cluster-drained-87-million-in-a-single-hour/)
- **Nansen**: public guide says it combines clustering algorithms, behavioural models (abnormally high win rates, unusual timing) and a human research team that hand-maintains the most valuable labels. Nansen CLI chains labels, related wallets and counterparties with high/medium/low confidence rules; supports Solana — [Nansen use case 3](https://docs.nansen.ai/guides/templates/complex-use-cases/use-case-3-identifying-related-wallets-at-scale) and search-result summary of Nansen's wallet-clustering skill listing (secondary)
- **Arkham**: described by third-party reviews as AI-assisted entity clustering from transaction patterns, timing and funding sources; one review says heuristic clustering sometimes misattributes wallets. Arkham's own Solana rules were not found — [Toolchase listing](https://toolchase.com/tool/arkham/) (secondary review). Pine Analytics used Arkham Intel for wallet-flow visualisation — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)
- **RugCheck**: has an "insider network" feature and CLI/skill command `insiders <mint>` returning a connected-wallet graph; Solana Tracker's API exposes insider wallets, snipers, bundlers and dev holdings inside a token risk object with a 1-10 score. Signals and weights not documented — [Termo RugCheck skill](https://termo.ai/skills/rugcheck), [MadeOnSol listing](https://madeonsol.com/tools/rugcheck), [Solana Tracker](https://www.solanatracker.io/blog/rugchecks) (all secondary)
- **GMGN**: token pages show insider, sniper and bundler tags; holder/trader lists can be filtered by tags smart_degen, renowned, sniper, bundler. One explainer says bundled buys are identified as several wallets buying in a single block and that GMGN labels some checks as third-party reference information. No official definition or thresholds found — [Data Wallet GMGN explainer](https://www.datawallet.com/es/cripto/gmgn-explained) and [GMGN skill listing](https://skillselion.com/skills/gmgnai/gmgn-skills/gmgn-token) (both secondary)
- **Helius**: Wallet API has `funded-by`, `identity` and `batch-identity` endpoints (beta, paid). Funder label and category come from the identity system, so exchange funders are named — [Helius funded-by](https://docs.helius.xyz/docs/wallet-api/funded-by.md)
- **Solscan**: dedicated funded_by endpoint, 50 addresses per request; pitched for sybil and entity clustering — [Solscan KB](https://kb.solscan.io/solscan-api-trace-solana-wallet-funders)
- **Aggregated vendor outputs** (Solana Tracker, Mobula) present clusters as counts and percentage-of-supply with traffic-light thresholds: 1-2 snipers in top 20 yellow; multiple snipers >20% combined red; small bundler cluster <5% yellow; 10+ wallet networks red; bundlers 20%+ with snipers high-risk; dev holding <3% green. Mobula states these are heuristics with no validation — [Mobula](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers)
- **Elliptic** (compliance vendor) describes its approach to launches as looking at how early buyers were funded (exchange deposit clusters, mixers, bridges), whether fresh wallets share an upstream source, buys in tight windows before promotion, and fast splitting of proceeds. Vendor marketing prose, generic across chains — [Elliptic memecoin insider page](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-2717/finance-game/on-chain-insider-trading-detection-for-meme-coin-and-token-launch-events.html) (search result summary; the fetch of a sibling URL returned 404)

### Inferences
- The visible market convention is two separate objects: a *bundle/launch-window set* (time-defined, cheap, ephemeral) and a *cluster/entity* (graph-defined, persistent). An agent should store both and not conflate them; Bubblemaps' own docs make the same distinction.
- Percentage-of-supply thresholds are product-risk heuristics for token buyers. They are not directly applicable to a copy-trader's question ("do these two *tracked* wallets share an operator?") but the underlying link rules are reusable.
- Third-party "skill" listings (skillselion, openskillindex, termo.ai) are community summaries and may be inaccurate; treat as pointers only.

### Gaps
- "TrenchTools-style products": no authoritative source found. Search results did not surface any product documentation under that name.
- No primary documentation for GMGN, RugCheck, Arkham, Birdeye or SolanaFM cluster/insider logic.
- Not found: whether Bubblemaps filters CEX/LP/program-owned accounts when computing cluster share (its guide says CEX/LP bubbles are not insiders but is silent on the percentage calculation).

## 3. What does the academic and industry literature say?

### Takeaway
There is little peer-reviewed Solana-specific work on wallet clustering. The one directly relevant 2026 preprint (sniper cohorts on pump.fun) has withdrawn its own numerical results, so only its method outline is usable. The rest is Bitcoin-era clustering (Meiklejohn et al.), vendor material, and non-Solana pump-and-dump GNN work.

### Cited Findings
- arXiv 2607.02795 (July 2026, single author, preprint) "Coordinated Sniper Cohorts on Pump.fun": 1,578,333 buyer observations from 166,098 launches over about 13.4 days (June 2026); extracts first-buyer window per launch; finds persistent cohorts across launches via union-find on co-occurrence graphs; reports 1,012 cohorts of 2-12 wallets (2,965 addresses). Frames it using Meiklejohn et al. (2013) wallet clustering. **The paper's correction notice withdraws all numerical results** because buyer records included sells and missed many buys. Its placebo test also produced larger lifts than the real estimate in 100/100 seeds, so its causal estimate was biased. Co-occurrence thresholds are not stated in the summary I could read. Use only as an architectural idea, not as evidence — [arXiv abs 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)
- ASE '26 / arXiv 2607.28424 "Demystifying Solana Bots: From GitHub Blueprints to On-Chain Fingerprints": relevant for bot taxonomy and on-chain fingerprints; PDF body unreadable here — [arXiv 2607.28424](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28424)
- PumpWatcher (IEEE TIFS 2025): GNN + contrastive learning for DeFi pump-and-dump, tested on Uniswap data from December 2022 with 92.3% balanced accuracy. Not Solana — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10212382)
- Spatio-temporal GNN for crypto fraud (arXiv 2604.24590, April 2026): token-level graphs from hourly market data, events from about 2017-2021; not wallet-level — [arXiv 2604.24590](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24590)
- Elliptic's guidance on address clustering: heuristics are not absolute; use confidence scoring; clusters with direct control evidence differ from behavioural-similarity-only clusters — [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3495/software-development/address-clustering-algorithms.html)
- Sybil-filtering practice for Solana airdrops (low-rigor, partly vendor): flags wallets that fund each other, transact in sequence or share a SOL source; Jito's airdrop used sybil filtering and distributed 80M tokens to 9,852 addresses; reports of one entity controlling 14,000 wallets in the aPriori airdrop and 900 identical wallets funded a day before IRYS launch — [MadeOnSol airdrop farming](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-airdrop-farming-strategies), [Streamflow sybil](https://streamflow.finance/blog/sybil-resistant-airdrop) (Streamflow sells sybil tooling)

### Inferences
- Solana lacks Bitcoin's multi-input common-ownership heuristic in the same form: a transaction can have multiple signers, but only the fee payer/signers reveal control, and many "multi-signer" transactions are user plus relayer/bot. So the analogue is "same signer set / same fee payer", not "inputs spent together". This is my reasoning, unsourced.
- Union-find over a launch co-occurrence graph (as in the sniper-cohort preprint) is a cheap persistent-cohort method; its validity is currently unestablished given the paper's withdrawn results.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper found on GNN wallet labelling specifically for Solana memecoins; no benchmark dataset with ground-truth operator labels.
- Did not retrieve the Meiklejohn et al. paper itself, or any Solana SSRN/IEEE paper on pump.fun bundling.
- No law-enforcement or Chainalysis/TRM Solana-specific pieces were retrieved; the ZachXBT-style sources suggested were not surfaced.

## 4. How do operators evade detection, and how is rotation tracked?

### Takeaway
Documented evasion in sources: multi-hop funding (5-7 hops cited), CEX-funded fresh wallets with varied amounts and timing, split purchases, cross-chain swaps/bridges before exchange deposit, and funding directly from the deployer. Evidence on *tracking* rotation (following a leader to a new wallet) is thin: the only concrete practice is first-funder and fresh-wallet-funded-by-tracked-wallet logic, which GodsEye already has.

### Cited Findings
- Pine Analytics: multi-hop funding of 5-7 hops through intermediary wallets before the sniper buys; it was excluded from the study for computational cost — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)
- Airdrop farmers fund each wallet from a CEX with varied amounts and timing specifically to avoid a common funding fingerprint — [Wormhole sybil filtering breakdown](https://insightfulinfo.substack.com/p/breaking-down-the-wormhole-airdrop) (Wormhole-era, not Solana-specific, secondary)
- Elliptic: actors use cross-chain routes to fragment evidence (swap, bridge, swap, then deposit to exchange), and templated fresh wallets funded similarly with fast splitting of proceeds are an indicator — [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3648/rain-in-a-dry-land/on-chain-monitoring-for-meme-coin-launches-rug-pulls-and-pump-and-dump-manipulation-risks.html) (vendor, generic)
- DEXTools reports some platforms advertise ways to split purchases to obscure bundling — [DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/news/bundled-supply-memecoin-risk-2026) (via search snippet; fetch returned 404)
- Bubblemaps' real-time signs of a coming dump: a stable cluster fragmenting into smaller wallets, rapid top-20 churn, and rising wallet connections; and "cross-chain reuse" of deployer clusters. Cross-chain linkage was key in LIBRA/MELANIA — [Bubblemaps holder guide](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/), [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/341238/bubblemaps-links-single-entity-to-libra-and-melania-memecoins)
- The $AVA case (Dec 2025): despite CEX funding and fresh wallets, Bubblemaps linked 23 wallets by narrow funding time windows, similar SOL amounts and zero prior history — [Blockchain.News](https://blockchain.news/flashnews/23-wallets-from-bitget-binance-sniped-ava-on-solana-bubblemaps-on-chain-alert-for-traders)
- Dormant-then-reactivate behaviour: LIBRA cluster addresses reactivated after nine months and swapped $60M USDC to SOL — [search-result summary of Bubblemaps coverage](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/the-libra-playbook-how-one-cluster-drained-87-million-in-a-single-hour/)
- A 2026 copy-trading guide survey notes that not every "smart money" wallet is smart; some are bots, wash traders or lucky gamblers. It did not describe rotation tracking — [MadeOnSol copy-trading](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-bots) (affiliate-style blog)

### Inferences
- Rotation tracking for a copy-trader reduces to three observable events on a tracked wallet T: (1) T sends SOL (or tokens) to a fresh wallet N, (2) T's behaviour moves to N, (3) T is swept/closed. A stronger version of event (1) than "any fresh wallet T funds" is: N's first inbound SOL comes from T *and* N's first swap occurs within a short time and uses the same token(s) or same size band as T's recent trades. This is my reasoning; no source tested it.
- Sweeps back (N returns residual SOL to T, or both sweep to one collector) are a strong confirmation link and also mark the end of a rotation epoch.
- Evasion by CEX funding with varied amounts is only defeated by secondary signals (arrival time, amount ratios, shared token set, post-trade consolidation), not by funder identity.
- Privacy tooling (mixers/privacy protocols on Solana) was not researched in any source I could retrieve; chain-breaking is then essentially undetectable by graph methods.

### Gaps
- No source documents tracking a leader's wallet rotation in copy-trading or forensic practice; no empirical rotation-rate data (how often insiders rotate wallets, average wallet lifespan).
- No Solana-specific mixer/privacy evasion coverage (found only Elliptic's generic bridge/swap remark).
- No ZachXBT, Chainalysis or TRM Solana-memecoin write-ups surfaced.

## 5. Practical recommendations for an agent that stores first funder and groups by funder

### Takeaway
Next most valuable signal: a *co-trading* edge from "same token, near-identical timing, near-identical first-buy slot" between tracked or candidate wallets, computed per token and accumulated over multiple tokens, combined with a direct SOL transfer edge between wallets (wallet A to wallet B, not just shared parent). Score clusters with tiered, evidence-count-based confidence and inverse-degree weights on shared parents. Everything below beyond the cited points is my design inference.

### Cited Findings
- Sources converge on tiered confidence: Nansen (>90% first funder/shared signer/same CEX deposit address; 60-90% volume or timing; <60% behavioural) — [Nansen](https://docs.nansen.ai/guides/templates/complex-use-cases/use-case-3-identifying-related-wallets-at-scale); Elliptic (confidence scoring, "possible association" vs definitive control) — [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3495/software-development/address-clustering-algorithms.html); the Solana clustering guide (0-100 score or tiers, seed from high-confidence heuristic clusters only, propagate under strict gates, normalise popular routes, version thresholds, show evidence tables, precision over recall) — [openskillindex guide](https://openskillindex.com/skills/neversight-learn-skills-dev-solana-clustering-advanced)
- Bubblemaps says repetition across multiple tokens is part of a cluster definition (patterns across multiple tokens, persistence over days to months), whereas same-block buying in a single launch is only a "bundle" — [Bubble vs Cluster](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/whats-the-difference-between-bundle-cluster-2/)
- Pine Analytics' direct-transfer filter (SOL transfer to or from the deployer before launch) is a cheap, high-precision subset; multi-hop is the main recall loss — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)
- The sniper-cohort preprint's approach (persistent co-occurrence across launches via union-find) is the closest published analogue, with the caveat that its results are withdrawn — [arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)
- Helius/Solscan give first-funder lookups cheaply (50-address batches on Solscan; 100 credits per call on Helius) so funding-depth expansion (funder of the funder) is API-feasible — [Solscan KB](https://kb.solscan.io/solscan-api-trace-solana-wallet-funders), [Helius](https://docs.helius.xyz/docs/wallet-api/funded-by.md)

### Inferences
Ranked suggestions (my reasoning, none benchmarked):
1. **Direct wallet-to-wallet SOL edges (either direction), not only first funder.** Cheap since you already read the first transaction; extend to "any SOL transfer between two tracked/candidate wallets in the last N days". Two-way transfers and sweeps back to a common wallet are the strongest cheap upgrade.
2. **Funding-chain depth with degree-based stop.** Walk the funder chain up to ~3 hops; stop at any node whose fan-out is above the popularity threshold (your 25+ rule) or whose label is exchange/bot/bridge/program. Weight each hop by 1/log(fan-out of the shared ancestor) and decay by depth.
3. **Co-buy edges across multiple tokens.** For each token, take the slot and ordinal of each wallet's first buy; add an edge weight when two wallets buy within the same slot or a couple of slots, multiplied by token rarity (1 / number of wallets that bought that token in that window). Require >=2-3 distinct tokens before the edge counts as more than a bundle. This follows the cluster-vs-bundle distinction in Bubblemaps.
4. **Exact-control signals when available:** same fee payer, shared co-signer, a wallet closing another's token accounts (rent destination), common destination of SOL sweeps. Treat as "confirmed" tier, subject to excluding known relayer/bot fee payers via a high-degree filter.
5. **Funding-fingerprint similarity for CEX-funded wallets:** same CEX hot wallet is uninformative, but similar withdrawal timestamp window (minutes), amount ratio, and zero prior history are informative (the $AVA pattern).
6. **Weak fingerprints last:** compute-unit limit/price bands, Jito tip amount similarity, program path sets. Use only as tie-breakers (+small weight), never to create an edge alone.

Scoring sketch:
- Edge types with fixed base weights: confirmed-control (fee payer, rent close, two-way sweep) ~0.9; direct transfer ~0.6; shared non-popular first funder ~0.5 (scaled by 1/log(degree)); multi-token co-buy ~0.4 per rarity-weighted distinct token, saturating; single-launch same-slot ~0.15; weak fingerprints ~0.05.
- Combine edges noisy-OR style: P(link) = 1 - prod(1 - w_i), capped; require at least one non-timing edge for a "same operator" label.
- Cluster confidence = minimum-spanning or average pairwise link strength, not the max, to prevent chaining (A-B strong, B-C strong, A-C unrelated). Require a clique-ish density or two independent paths before merging components.
- Label tiers: Confirmed (exact control), Probable (>=2 independent signal families), Possible (single family). Only "Confirmed" and "Probable" should drive position sizing or copy-weighting; "Possible" should only be displayed. This mirrors Nansen/Elliptic tiering and the "false negatives over false accusations" guidance.
- Keep evidence rows per edge with timestamps so a cluster can be re-scored when a popularity list or exchange label changes (versioned thresholds, as the clustering guide recommends).
- For copy-trading specifically: a tracked wallet's cluster-mates add information when they buy the same token after the leader (confirmation) or before (the leader is following an insider). Rotation rule suggestion: when T funds a fresh wallet N, carry over T's trust score at a discount and promote N to full tracking only after N's first trades overlap T's token set or N sweeps to/from T.
- Back-test before trusting: ground-truth is scarce, so use known cases (Bubblemaps-published clusters such as $AVA, LIBRA, MELANIA) as sanity checks, and measure precision as the share of high-score clusters that also co-trade out of sample.

### Gaps
- No published benchmark or labelled dataset for tuning edge weights or thresholds; the weights above are placeholders.
- No source on optimal time windows for co-buy (the community guide suggests 10-30s after mint, "tuned per protocol").
- No source on measuring false positives from bot-platform wallets (Trojan, BonkBot, Photon); the agent will need to build its own list from high-fan-out addresses.
