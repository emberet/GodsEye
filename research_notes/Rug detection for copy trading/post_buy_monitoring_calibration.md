# Post-buy rug monitoring/exit and rug-screen threshold calibration for a Solana memecoin copy-trader (as of 2026-10-27)

Scope and method notes. About 25 tool calls (the researcher brief suggested ~15; the topic has six sub-questions). All pages read on 2026-10-27 unless a "search snippet only" flag is shown. I did not repeat what `signals_ml_opensource.md` and `realtime_infrastructure.md` already establish (Li et al. "Catching the Rug", MemeTrans basics, SolRPDS basics, latency chain, Yellowstone filters/pricing, Jito hop); where I rely on them I say "see prior notes". The recurring finding is negative: **no published study measures how many seconds before a price collapse each on-chain precursor appears, nor false-positive rates of post-buy monitors, nor realistic recovery rates after an LP pull.** Those numbers have to come from GodsEye's own shadow logging. Things marked "(inference)" are my reasoning or arithmetic, not sourced. Things marked "(general knowledge, unverified)" were not checked against a primary source in this pass.

## Q1. What to watch on a held position, how early each signal appears, false-positive rates, data source and cost

### Takeaway
Most labeled Solana "rugs" are not single-transaction LP pulls: in the one hand-labeled Solana benchmark (117 tokens) 89 were pump-and-dump (holders/wallets exiting through swaps), 26 liquidity withdrawal and 2 freeze-authority abuse, and lifecycles are short (median about 17 minutes from creation to first profit-taking). An LP removal is atomic, so its own transaction gives no lead time; lead time exists only in precursors (creator/insider selling, LP tokens held by a creator, freeze authority retained, reserve drains) and the best observable that catches both soft and hard rugs is the pool's vault balances, not the price feed. No source quantifies lead times or false-positive rates, so those columns below are explicitly unknown.

### Cited Findings
- SolRugDetector (arXiv 2603.24625) built a 117-token confirmed-rug set from 68 community incident reports (Chainabuse, SlowMist, CertiK) with three-researcher consensus labeling, plus 65 legitimate tokens: 2 freeze-authority abuse, 26 liquidity withdrawal, 89 pump-and-dump. Applied to 100,063 tokens on Orca, Raydium and Meteora (first half of 2025) it flagged 76,469 (76.42%): 60,402 pump-and-dump, 15,606 liquidity withdrawal, 461 freeze-authority abuse; at least USD 151 M of quantifiable losses. Only tokens that successfully created liquidity pools were included (pump.fun tokens that never migrated are outside) — [arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1)
- Same paper, rule definitions: freeze-authority abuse = non-null freeze authority plus a `FreezeAccount` instruction whose logs show a frozen account; liquidity manipulation = a creator-related address with positive liquidity profit and a post-removal transaction rate below τ_active (text says 5 tx/hour, notation list says 55; 0.5-5 tx/hour reportedly gave no change); pump-and-dump = holders and pool token balance stay below start values with a holder decline of at least τ_down = 0.73 within 24 h of creation. τ_down was tuned on the same labeled set, so performance is probably optimistic — [arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1)
- Same paper, timing (lifecycle = creation to first confirmed profit-taking transaction, in days): all rugs median 0.0116 (about 17 min), p25 0.0024 (about 3.5 min), p75 0.0576 (about 83 min); pump-and-dump median 0.0221 (about 32 min), p75 0.0489 (about 70 min); liquidity manipulation median 0.0385 (about 55 min), p75 0.966 (about 23 h); freeze-authority abuse median 0.483 (about 11.6 h), p75 4.69 days. The paper's text claims p75 "generally does not exceed 5 hours", which its own Table VI contradicts for two types. After execution the flagged tokens average 0.2 tx/hour versus 3,462 for legitimate tokens ("cliff-like"). The paper does not report time from liquidity addition to withdrawal or speed of price collapse, and does not quantify authority changes or creator transfers before withdrawal — [arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1)
- Same paper, tool comparison on its labeled set (precision / recall): RugChecker (rugcheck.xyz) 76.92% / 94.02%; Solsniffer 67.45% / 97.44%; Solanatracker 98.96% / 81.89%; "Solana Rug Checker" 67.74% / 35.90%; SolRugDetector 100% / 93.16%. In the wild, 1 of 382 flagged tokens was a false positive (0.26%) but 9 of 100 sampled non-flagged tokens were missed (9%). Precision here depends on the set's base rate (117 rugs vs 65 legit), so it does not transfer to a stream of live copy candidates — [arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1)
- SolRPDS (CODASPY 2025) detects "rug-like" pools by liquidity-action instruction names: `addLiquidity`, `withdraw`, `removeLiquidity`, `removeLiquiditySingleSide`, `removeAllLiquidity`; a token is "Inactive" if its latest swap came after a liquidity removal and nobody traded afterwards to the cutoff. Roughly 75% of inactive tokens lived under one day (mint to last interaction). Inactive pools averaged about 13 removals (many only one) versus over 80 for active pools; the paper says removal was "all at once or gradually" and gives no lead-time data — [SolRPDS paper (PDF, text extracted locally)](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07132)
- Cernera et al. (USENIX Security 2023, Ethereum/BSC, not Solana): about 59% of tokens are "1-day tokens"; their 1-day rug-pull pattern is a pool with exactly one Mint and one Burn event where one address burns at least 99% of the minted LP tokens (99% rather than 100% for rounding); 81.2% of BSC and 86.3% of Ethereum 1-day-token pools matched; one worked example shows liquidity pulled about two hours after launch. EVM-only; the point for Solana is that a pull is a single event by an LP holder — [Cernera et al., PDF text extracted locally](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08202)
- PumpSwap pools: `withdraw` burns the caller's own LP tokens for base and quote; the pool creator receives initial LP tokens at creation; the doc defines no pool-level admin that can withdraw funds; `lp_supply` excludes tokens burned directly (so a direct LP burn does not show up in `lp_supply`); effective quote reserves = quote vault balance + `virtual_quote_reserves` (a signed i128, 0 on all pools at fetch time, can be negative from 30 Sep); creator/protocol fees stay in the pool until swept — [pump-public-docs PUMP_SWAP_README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/main/docs/PUMP_SWAP_README.md), [pump-public-docs index](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs)
- Migrated pump.fun pools reportedly burn the LP tokens at graduation, which is why LP-pull risk on migrated pump.fun tokens is low (secondary sources only; no official pump.fun doc found). Separately, pump.fun's BOOST change deploys about 20% of migration liquidity into automatic buys-and-burns in the first five minutes after migration instead of leaving it as locked LP — [The Block via search snippet](https://theblock.co/news/defi/2026-07-29-pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-jumps-boost-changes-launch-incentives-409815), [Seoul Data Labs / skills page via search snippet](https://skills.cat/skills/sanctifiedops/solana-skills/pump-fun-mechanics)
- Raydium: LaunchLab-graduated tokens have mint authority burned and LP in Burn & Earn by default; unlocked LP means the team can pull liquidity at any time; a live mint authority allows unlimited minting; top-10 holders at about 80% of supply is called out as a one-sale-crashes-it risk — [Raydium trust and safety](https://docs.raydium.io/getting-started/trust-and-safety.md)
- Meteora DAMM v2: liquidity is a position controlled by whoever holds the position NFT; positions can carry vesting locks or permanent locks that restrict only withdrawal (fees still accrue); locks follow the NFT; pool price range fixed at creation. Did not find DLMM lock semantics — [Meteora DAMM v2 docs via search snippet](https://docs.meteora.ag/overview/products/damm-v2/liquidity-locking.md) (the .md page itself returned 404 when fetched directly)
- Yellowstone/Dragon's Mouth account subscriptions: filter by `account` or `owner`, with `memcmp`/`token_account_state` filters; `accounts_data_slice` returns only part of the account data (example: 40 bytes instead of 165); updates carry a slot, and Dragon's Mouth streams multiple account updates within a slot (plain RPC gives one per slot); default commitment is processed; the page does not say whether an initial snapshot is sent on subscribe, gives no pricing, and does not clearly say whether transaction updates carry logs/status (the deshred stream explicitly does not) — [Triton gRPC subscriptions docs](https://docs.triton.one/rpc-pool/grpc-subscriptions). Pricing and latency of streams: see prior note `realtime_infrastructure.md` (Triton USD 0.08/GB metered; Alchemy USD 75/TB).
- Freeze authority is described as the main Solana honeypot mechanism (a frozen token account cannot sell or transfer while others keep buying); Token-2022 extensions (permanent delegate, transfer hook, transfer fee, pause authority) can each block or tax exits; some scanners read these flags straight off the mint, others offer a simulated sell as a complement. These are vendor/blog pages (InsightX docs, a dev.to write-up), not primary Solana docs — [InsightX scanner indicators](https://docs.insightx.network/reference/scanner-solana-indicators), [dev.to freeze-authority write-up](https://dev.to/mrvlyouknowwho/freeze-authority-is-the-solana-honeypot-how-to-check-any-spl-token-in-10-seconds-free-no-wallet-15h4) (search snippets only)
- Jupiter Price API v3: price is the token's "last swapped price"; it updates "with swap activity rather than on a fixed interval"; tokens without a reliable price are omitted (not returned as null), typically because the token has not traded in 7 days or heuristics flag it; there is no per-mint reason; keyless limit 0.5 rps, free 1 rps, up to 50 ids per request; the docs do not mention rugged, unsellable or honeypot tokens — [Jupiter Price v3 docs](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/price/v3)
- Jito: `sendTransaction` through Jito always sets `skip_preflight` true (no RPC simulation); sells can be sent with `bundleOnly=true` for revert protection — [Jito low-latency send docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)

### Inferences
Signal table (lead time and false-positive columns are "unknown" unless a source gave a number; "where" = cheapest stream that sees it):

| Signal | What you actually see | Lead time before collapse | Source / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| LP removal (Raydium `withdraw`/`removeLiquidity`, PumpSwap `withdraw`, Meteora remove-liquidity) | A tx by an LP holder; vault balances fall in the same slot | **None as an event** (atomic); detection is about one slot (200 ms) plus your latency after it lands. Unknown how often it is preceded by other signals | Transaction stream with `account_include` = pool address (or vaults), or account stream on the two vaults. Cheap per pool |
| Vault reserve shock (quote vault drops by X% in N slots without a matching swap; or reserve ratio jumps) | Direct measure of exit depth, covers both hard pulls and heavy dumping | Same slot as the event; but sustained drain (soft rug) shows over many swaps | `accounts` filter on the pool's two token vaults with a data slice to the amount field (token-account layout puts `amount` at byte offset 64, length 8; general knowledge, unverified) so each update is tiny. My arithmetic: even 100 updates/s of about 200 bytes is about 1.7 GB/day, about USD 0.14/day at USD 0.08/GB, so a held-position-only subscription is negligible |
| Creator/dev selling (pump.fun creator wallet balance in the mint falls) | Swap by the creator wallet, visible in transactions touching creator | Real lead only for gradual dumps; zero for a one-shot dump | Add creator wallet to `account_include` (you already track wallets) or read the creator field from the create event; cheap |
| Insider/bundle wallet sells (known cluster from buy-time screen) | Group of wallets selling | Potentially the earliest hard-to-fake precursor for pump-and-dump (the dominant label type) | Needs the cluster list at buy time (screen already computes it) and a transaction filter on those addresses; cost grows with list size, use the cuckoo filter if large (prior note) |
| Freeze/authority event | `FreezeAccount` hits your token account, or `SetAuthority`/extension update on the mint | A freeze is per-account and instantaneous; once frozen you cannot exit at all | Include **your own token account** and the mint in `account_include` and watch for `FreezeAccount`/`SetAuthority`/`Pause`-style instructions (inference: such a tx lists the token account as an account key, so the filter should catch it). The preventive control is the buy-time check (freeze authority null, no permanent delegate/transfer hook), not a post-buy reaction |
| Route disappears / price omitted on Jupiter | Mint absent from Price v3 response | **Late and unreliable**: omitted only after 7 days of no trades or heuristic flag; the last swapped price lingers after an LP pull | REST poll; do not use as an early signal |
| Price drop | Last-swap price | After a pure proportional LP removal the constant-product price ratio y/x does not change (inference, below), so the price feed can look normal while exit depth is gone | Already available but must be paired with depth |
| Sell failure / simulate-sell failing | Failed sell attempt or failed `simulateTransaction` of a sell | Late for hard rugs, but the only direct "can I exit?" probe | RPC simulation per held position, cheap; run on a short cadence for positions with any other flag. (A `simulate_sell` MCP tool exists in the search results, confirming the pattern but not evidence of efficacy) |

- Because 76% of the labeled Solana rugs are pump-and-dump (holders leaving through swaps) rather than LP pulls, a monitor that only watches LP removal would miss most of them; insider/creator selling and reserve/holder-decline rules matter more. Counterpoint: SolRugDetector's 100,063-token population is 60,402 pump-and-dump by its own broad τ_down = 0.73 rule, so the "pump-and-dump" bucket is partly a definitional artifact (any token whose holder count falls 73% in 24 h).
- Migrated pump.fun tokens (the bulk of this bot's tape per the internal audit: 340 of 478 priced entries under 5 minutes old, mostly PumpSwap) should have low classic LP-pull risk if LP is burned at migration, so their dominant failure is insider/bundle/creator dumping and thin-pool collapse. Verify the LP-burn claim against the PumpSwap `migrate` instruction/IDL before relying on it; I only found secondary sources.
- Given the lifecycle quartiles (p25 about 3.5 min, median about 17 min), a 60-second poll is slow relative to the window in which most rugs resolve, and a price-only poll is blind to depth loss. Event-driven checks on the already-open gRPC stream (vaults, creator, cluster wallets, own token account) cost almost nothing next to the stream already running.
- Latency floor from prior notes (about 400 ms to 1.5 s behind the leader's processed event for a copy; for a sell you start from detection of the precursor, similar chain) means a sell cannot beat an LP removal that is already in the stream; it can beat the *second wave* of sellers.

### Gaps
- No source gives lead time (seconds/minutes) of any precursor ahead of the price collapse on Solana; no false-positive rates for creator-sell, LP-removal, vault-ratio or authority-change triggers. These must be measured in shadow mode.
- Official pump.fun statement that migrated-pool LP is burned (and whether the creator keeps any LP or fee claims that could be abused) was not found; PumpSwap `migrate`/`withdraw` per-instruction docs and IDL were not opened.
- Meteora DLMM lock semantics and Raydium CPMM/CLMM LP-holder mechanics were not verified from primary docs.
- Whether `FreezeAccount`/`SetAuthority` transactions are delivered by a token-account `account_include` filter (all providers) was not tested; Triton docs do not state initial-snapshot behavior for account subscriptions.
- Token-account byte layout offset (64) is general knowledge, not verified in this pass.

## Q2. Exit options once an LP pull is in progress or done; unsellable state; how other bots handle it

### Takeaway
Once liquidity is gone, the maximum recoverable value is the residual quote reserve, and a higher slippage setting cannot create liquidity; it only lets the transaction clear at a worse price when depth remains. Front-running with priority fees or a Jito bundle can only help when you have detected a *precursor* (the pull itself is atomic), because Jito's auction ranks by tip among bundles touching the same accounts. Public bot code mostly handles the "can't sell" case by giving up on selling past a loss threshold; I found no source on real recovery rates and no bot that formally writes positions off.

### Cited Findings
- Jito: a bundle holds up to 5 transactions executed sequentially and atomically in one slot, all-or-nothing; needs a tip to one of 8 tip accounts (minimum 1,000 lamports, "may not be enough during high demand"); parallel auctions run on 50 ms ticks; bundles that touch the same accounts with conflicting locks compete in the same auction, ranked by tip and compute efficiency; for `sendTransaction` Jito suggests roughly 70% priority fee / 30% tip; default rate limit is 1 request/s per IP per region; tips do not prioritize when the leader is not a Jito-Solana validator; put the tip in the same transaction as core logic so you do not pay it if that transaction fails; bundles can land in uncled blocks and be rebroadcast outside the bundle, losing atomicity; `jitodontfront` accounts reduce sandwiching but are not a guarantee; the page has no sell-specific guidance — [Jito low-latency send docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)
- Jito's own ~50 ms extra Block Engine hop and the 200 ms slot time: see prior note `realtime_infrastructure.md` section 5.
- Open-source Solana sniper bot `fdundjer/solana-sniper-bot` exposes `STOP_LOSS`, `TAKE_PROFIT`, `TRAILING_STOP_LOSS`, `PRICE_CHECK_INTERVAL` (ms), `PRICE_CHECK_DURATION` (auto-sells when it expires), `MAX_SELL_RETRIES`, `SELL_SLIPPAGE`, and a rug-protection setting `SKIP_SELLING_IF_LOST_MORE_THAN`: past that loss the bot stops trying to sell ("useful if you find yourself in a situation when rugpull happen, and you failed to sell"), i.e. it holds the bag. Buy-side filters: `CHECK_IF_BURNED`, `CHECK_IF_MINT_IS_RENOUNCED`, `CHECK_IF_FREEZABLE`, `MIN_POOL_SIZE`/`MAX_POOL_SIZE`, and `CONSECUTIVE_FILTER_MATCHES` (its docs note burn-based rugs may not show in other filters right away) — [fdundjer/solana-sniper-bot README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fdundjer/solana-sniper-bot/master/README.md)
- Another open-source sniper (pump.fun bitman09 repo) polls unrealized SOL value until take-profit, stop-loss or timeout, with a `SIMULATION_MODE`; a Gitea-hosted sniper's docs warn there is no guarantee a token can be sold at a profit or at all; SolSniperX advertises liquidity-lock, mint-authority and dev-wallet tracking plus automatic take-profit/stop-loss. None surfaced explicit failed-sell or write-off handling — [search snippets: awesome.ecosyste.ms](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fbitman09%2Fpumpfun-sniper-bot), [Gitea SOL sniper](https://gitea.com/NateTrades/solana-sniper-bot), [SolSniperX](https://codeberg.org/Dhaher-Labs/SolSniperX)
- Custodial Telegram bots (Banana Gun, Trojan, Maestro): review sites credit Banana Gun with "anti-rug protection" and a pre-trade honeypot simulator ("Banana Simulator", a buy-side check), one site quoting an "85% success rate" with no methodology; none document what triggers an anti-rug sell. Treat as marketing — [DEXTools comparison via search](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/maestro-vs-trojan-vs-banana-gun-fr), [coin360 review via search](https://coin360.com/review/banana-gun)
- Jito `sendTransaction` skips RPC preflight; the doc says to simulate first. For the stale-price problem: Jupiter Price v3 omits unreliable/untraded tokens for no stated reason and updates only on swap activity — [Jupiter Price v3](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/price/v3); [Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)

### Inferences
- **What the pool math says (my arithmetic, constant product, fee ignored):** selling dx tokens into reserves (x tokens, y quote) returns y·s/(1+s) with s = dx/x. A proportional LP removal leaves the ratio y/x unchanged, so the last-swap price does not move, but the remaining quote y′ is a hard cap on proceeds. Example: a position worth 1 SOL in a pool that had 100 SOL; after 95% of LP is removed (5 SOL quote left), s = 0.2 and proceeds = 5 × 0.2/1.2 ≈ 0.83 SOL; after 99.9% removed (0.1 SOL left), proceeds < 0.1 SOL. So the recovery question is "what fraction of the pool is left relative to my size?", and this is computable from vault balances in real time. A pull of 100% of LP typically leaves nothing to trade against (and many programs will not let reserves hit zero), so recovery is about zero. Realistic *rates* of recovery are unknown (no source).
- **Why higher slippage does not rescue a sell:** (1) proceeds are capped by residual reserves; (2) with a min-out derived from a stale quote, the swap fails when reserves change between quote and landing, but widening to 50-90% only helps if depth remains and then widens sandwich/MEV exposure; (3) if the instruction is disabled, the pool is empty, the account is frozen, or a Token-2022 hook/permanent delegate blocks transfer, no slippage setting matters; (4) each failed attempt still pays fees (tips are avoidable if placed inside the same transaction per Jito). Fixed 300 bps means every sell after any serious depth change fails; the better design is to compute min-out from *current vault reserves at send time* and accept a floor tied to the residual-depth formula, not a fixed bps.
- **Front-run feasibility:** Jito conflicts are resolved by tip within a 50 ms auction among bundles locking the same pool accounts, so a high-tip sell bundle can beat a *pending* removal only if you had submitted it before the removal landed. The removal reaches you only after it executed (processed stream) except via shred/deshred feeds that lead by single-digit to low-hundreds of ms (prior note), which is below the 200 ms slot and below your own send latency. Practical front-running therefore targets multi-step rugs: creator transfers LP tokens to a fresh wallet, creator begins selling, insider cluster starts selling, vault drains in steps. For those, a sell sent with a priority fee/tip at the 75th-99th percentile (Jito tip-floor endpoint; prior note) may land ahead of the next sellers. No source shows success rates.
- **Partial exit:** since proceeds are concave in size (y·s/(1+s)), splitting a sell into several smaller sends does not beat one sell against a static pool (same total impact), but sells submitted across successive slots can capture refills from buyers and avoid a single revert; the practical benefit of "partial" is risk control (take 50% at first soft trigger, rest at hard trigger), not better pricing. (reasoning, no source)
- **Stale price / unsellable state: yes, mark and write off automatically, but in stages.** Current behavior (no price means skip forever; failed sells retried; never `rugged`) leaves capital accounting, risk limits and PnL wrong. Because Jupiter's price is last-swap and omitted only after 7 days of no trades, a dead token can keep its pre-rug price in the feed for hours. Suggested state machine (design, not sourced): `active` -> `degraded` (price stale > N minutes with expected trading activity, or any hard/soft trigger) -> `unsellable` (K consecutive failed sells or simulated sell returns less than a dust floor, e.g. under 5% of cost, or residual quote reserve below position value) -> `written_off` (mark at residual-depth value, free the exposure slot, stop retries and fees). Keep a cheap re-check (hourly, then daily for 7 days) so a false write-off can be reversed. The open-source bots surveyed either stop selling (`SKIP_SELLING_IF_LOST_MORE_THAN`) or keep retrying; neither is an accounting write-off.
- Tokens with Token-2022 freeze/permanent-delegate/transfer-hook features should be mostly eliminated at buy time (CHECK_IF_FREEZABLE-style gate) because the post-buy options are nil.

### Gaps
- No empirical recovery rates (share of rugged positions where any sell succeeded, and at what fraction of cost) from papers, post-mortems or bot builders; no data on sell-failure rates for rugged Solana pools.
- No bot with a documented, code-visible write-off or `rugged` state found (only snippets of 4 repos; no repo's source was read beyond the fdundjer README).
- No measurement of Jito/priority-fee exit success during an active pull; Jito docs have no sell guidance.
- Whether Solana has any pre-execution visibility of a pending LP-removal (public mempool) was not verified; I believe there is no public mempool (general knowledge, unverified).
- Banana Gun/Trojan/Maestro anti-rug mechanics are undocumented in what I found.

## Q3. Kill-switch designs: evidence on what saves money versus false exits

### Takeaway
There is no published evidence on kill-switch efficacy for Solana memecoin bots; the only quantitative anchors are the speed of failure in the base data (about 60% of migrated tokens trade below 20% of migration price within 20 minutes; rug lifecycle median about 17 minutes) and that vendor/bot claims are unvalidated. Design the kill-switch as tiered rules whose parameters are fit on GodsEye's own shadow tape, using the existing replay harness to score each trigger by money saved minus upside forgone.

### Cited Findings
- MemeTrans label definition and base rate: within 20 minutes of migration, 60.26% of tokens fall below 20% of the migration price and 12.70% between 20% and 40% (about 73% below 40%); only 5.20% stay at or above migration price; 84.13% of 41,470 launches are high-risk; in the 21,635-token experimental subset 74.18% are; a simulated top-100 selection with an MLP cut average loss from 60.71% (random) to 26.64% (sell time sampled randomly within the next hour). The 20-minute window is a trade-off between catching in-progress sell-offs and natural decay — [MemeTrans, arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480)
- Same paper, filters: dropping tokens with sale under 1 minute or under 100 holders removed 19,835 tokens (47.8%), 95% of which were high-risk (no precision/recall for the rule alone) — [MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480)
- Li et al. (see prior note) label a rug as TVL drawdown over 99% or idle over 80% of lifetime at 1 hour and report MCC only about 0.25-0.40 in-domain from first-5-minute features, "not yet sufficient for real-world deployment"; Cao et al. wash-trading RF on BSC reports mean 3.8 h warning (as cited in Li et al.; not opened) — via prior note `signals_ml_opensource.md`.
- Existing open-source rule set (fdundjer): stop loss, take profit, trailing stop, max duration auto-sell, retries, and a stop-selling-after-big-loss cutoff, plus `CONSECUTIVE_FILTER_MATCHES` to confirm buy-side filters over several checks — [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fdundjer/solana-sniper-bot/master/README.md)
- Raydium's guidance flags top-10 holders near 80% as crash-prone and unlocked LP as pull-able at any time — [Raydium trust and safety](https://docs.raydium.io/getting-started/trust-and-safety.md)

### Inferences
Proposed tier structure (design to test, not validated anywhere):
1. **Hard triggers (sell everything immediately, max priority/tip, no cooldown):** quote-vault drop of at least X% in at most N slots not explained by buys/sells in that window (an LP removal), freeze/authority-change event on the mint or your account, simulated sell failing twice, creator/insider-cluster selling above a share of their balance. Start X at 50% as a hypothesis; tune on shadow data.
2. **Soft triggers (sell half, tighten stop):** reserve-implied price down 20-25% in under 30-60 s, buy:sell volume ratio flip, leader sells (already implemented), residual exit depth (formula in Q2) falling below 3x position size.
3. **Housekeeping:** the existing stop-loss/TP/48 h max hold; move the 60 s poll to event-driven checks on the held-position vaults; unsellable/write-off logic from Q2.
- Scoring each trigger offline with the replay harness: for every shadow-logged candidate with a real vault-reserve path (Q5), record (a) value saved = exit value at trigger minus value at the existing stop/hold exit, (b) value forgone = later peak/exit value minus exit value if the trigger fired on a token that survived. Keep a trigger only if mean(saved on true rugs) × P(rug|fire) exceeds mean(forgone on survivors) × P(survivor|fire) plus costs (fees, tip, spread). Because 73-84% of fresh migrated tokens are high-risk per MemeTrans, a trigger with even moderate precision on this universe may be net positive, while on a pre-filtered universe (after the buy-time screen) the base rate is lower and false exits cost more.
- Multi-signal confirmation reduces false exits for soft triggers (the `CONSECUTIVE_FILTER_MATCHES` pattern), but hard triggers should not wait for confirmation because the lead time may be a single slot.
- The 35% stop-loss only helps if the exit fills and the price feed shows the loss (Q1/Q2); the audit's finding that 16.5% of would-be copies fell 50% within 60 minutes on observed prices (a lower bound) says the stop-loss will be exercised often.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or post-mortem evidence found comparing kill-switch rule sets; no bot builder write-up with before/after PnL; Banana Gun's "85%" claim is unsourced marketing.
- Cao et al.'s 3.8 h mean warning is on BSC wash-trading and was not opened.
- No numbers for how fast a soft-rug reserve drain progresses on Solana (minutes vs seconds).

## Q4. Calibrating thresholds (top-10 50%, bundle 25%, cluster 15%, insider 20%, locked LP 90%, RugCheck 75): what is known and how to validate

### Takeaway
None of the six thresholds has a published validated cutoff. The studies report group-level effect sizes (bundles add about 24 points to top-10 share in high-risk tokens versus 6 in low-risk; holding+bundle features add a few AUPRC points), tool-level precision/recall on small labeled sets, and base rates so high (74-84% of fresh migrated tokens are "high-risk") that a threshold must be judged by expected value on the copy-candidate population, not by accuracy. Treat the current numbers as priors and fit cutoffs on GodsEye's own shadow-labeled data with time-ordered validation.

### Cited Findings
- MemeTrans: bundled accounts hold 36.50% of supply at migration and are 28.13% of holders; adding bundles raises top-10 holder share by about 24 points for high-risk tokens versus 6 for low-risk (medians 24 / 9 / 6 for high / medium / low); first 10 and 20 buyers of high-risk tokens hold 17 and 19 points more supply than for low-risk tokens; in 98.7% of create events the developer buys in the creation transaction. Not reported: risk rate by bundle-share or top-10 bucket, or any cutoff — [MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480)
- MemeTrans ablation (AUPRC, RF/MLP setting): full 0.5729; remove contextual 0.5567; remove holding 0.5693; remove market activity 0.5369; remove bundle 0.5451; remove holding+bundle 0.5268. Random-forest importance highest for market-activity then bundle features. Best model MLP+LGBM AUPRC 0.5821; label-1 precision 0.8459, recall 0.8325; top-100 pick precision 0.80 vs 0.254 random. Only migrated tokens (under 2% of memecoins) over a single three-month window around the $TRUMP launch; bundle detection is heuristic (co-purchase, fund flow, Jito IDs) — [MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480)
- Label definition is a post-event price rule, not a feature rule: high risk = minimum price in the 20 minutes after migration over migration price below 0.3, or manually judged manipulated (37.5% of the 9,471 reviewed tokens with ratio at least 0.5 were manipulated) — [MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480)
- Tool performance on SolRugDetector's labeled set: RugChecker (rugcheck.xyz) 76.92% precision / 94.02% recall; Solanatracker 98.96% / 81.89%; Solsniffer 67.45% / 97.44%. The RugCheck 75 score cutoff used by GodsEye is not evaluated anywhere I found, and the direction/scale of the score (and whether "75" is normalised) was not verified from RugCheck docs — [arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1)
- SolRPDS' classifier results (AUC up to 0.987) are circular because the "inactive" label comes from the same liquidity-activity attributes used as features (training 2022, test 2021 in that paper); see prior note — [SolRPDS paper](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07132)
- Li et al. (prior note): test sets 82% rug on PumpFun; MCC 0.25-0.36; rolling forward windows, no random K-fold; cross-venue transfer collapses to MCC about 0 — [arXiv 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20271)
- Datasets available to reuse: SolRPDS (62,895 suspicious pools, 22,195 inactive tokens, 2021-Nov 2024; CSV/JSON; label = inactivity after liquidity removal) — [DeFiLabX/SolRPDS](https://github.com/DeFiLabX/SolRPDS); MemeTrans features and labels — [git-disl/MemeTrans](https://github.com/git-disl/MemeTrans); SolRugDetector's 117-token confirmed set — described in [arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1) (repository/data link not confirmed in this pass). Effect of each on repo license/stars: see prior note.
- Multiple-testing and overfitting controls (Deflated Sharpe, Minimum Backtest Length; Bailey and Lopez de Prado) — via prior note `signals_ml_opensource.md`.

### Inferences
- Datasets cannot validate GodsEye's specific thresholds because (a) none provide bundle/insider/cluster shares computed with GodsEye's own definitions at the time of a copy, (b) SolRPDS has no holder or bundle features and a circular label, (c) MemeTrans is limited to migrated tokens in a single window, (d) the 117-token set is rugs plus a small legit set with no live base rate. Use them for sanity checks of direction of effect and for ablation ideas, not for cutoffs.
- Recommended procedure (design): log every candidate's raw feature values (top-10 share, bundle share, cluster share, insider share, LP-lock %, RugCheck score and sub-risks, liquidity, token age, venue) before applying thresholds; label outcomes with the Q6 rules; split chronologically (train earlier weeks, test later weeks); for each feature report precision-recall by quantile bucket (deciles of share) rather than one cutoff; choose cutoffs by expected value: block if P(rug|x) × L > (1 − P(rug|x)) × G, where L is average loss on a rug given your sizing/exit model and G is average net gain on a non-rug; use the break-even P* = G / (L + G). With heavy-tailed memecoin returns, G is dominated by rare large winners, so estimate G with medians and trimmed means as well as means.
- Tuning six thresholds jointly is a multiple-testing trap at 100-1,000 candidates/week; prefer a monotone risk score (logistic/GBM with few features, or a simple additive points system) with a single cutoff, regularised, with cutoffs selected by cross-validated EV on time-ordered folds, and report uncertainty (bootstrap intervals).
- Expect "locked LP" semantics to differ by venue: PumpSwap migrated LP burn is automatic (secondary sources), Raydium LaunchLab uses Burn & Earn by default (primary), Meteora uses position locks (primary) — so a single "locked LP ≥ 90%" rule should be evaluated per venue rather than pooled, and may be uninformative for venues where lock is universal.
- Candidate-level base rate (74-84% "high risk" at fresh migration) means the screen should be thought of as selecting the top-ranked 10-30% of candidates (cf. MemeTrans top-100 simulation) rather than removing only the obviously bad ones.

### Gaps
- No published optimal cutoffs or ROC points for bundle share, top-10 share, creator holdings, LP-lock status, liquidity or token age on Solana.
- RugCheck's current score semantics and API not verified; whether the screen's "insider 20%" and "cluster 15%" use definitions comparable to MemeTrans bundles (they likely do not).
- SolRugDetector dataset/code availability not confirmed.
- Token-age and liquidity predictive value: only qualitative (MemeTrans filter on sale under 1 minute / under 100 holders; Raydium guidance); no effect sizes found.

## Q5. Measuring the false-positive cost of a screen when blocked tokens are never bought

### Takeaway
You cannot recover blocked-token outcomes from the screen's verdict; you have to observe them. The clean approach is shadow logging of every candidate with a reserve-based price path (vault account subscription) so outcomes are observed regardless of whether tracked wallets trade; the robust complement is a small randomized exploration buy on a fraction of blocked tokens (the only fully reliable counterfactual per the reject-inference literature). At 100-1,000 candidates/week, estimating a blocked-group rug rate to a useful precision takes weeks; estimating a mean-return difference takes months at the low end.

### Cited Findings
- Reject-inference (credit scoring) literature: Hand and Henley (1993) argue standard reject-inference methods rest on very tenuous assumptions and that reliable inference is impossible; the only robust approach is to accept a sample of rejected applications and observe outcomes. The bound-and-collapse Bayesian approach treats rejection as missing-not-at-random and yields bounds rather than point estimates. A 2026 preprint (Scarone and Baeza-Yates, not peer reviewed as far as the search showed) argues standard metrics are misleading under selection bias and proposes controlled exploration (deliberately approving a fraction of rejected applicants) — [search summary of Hand & Henley and bound-and-collapse (HAL)](https://hal-hec.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00655036v1), [Edinburgh CRC survey](https://cer.business-school.ed.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/55/2017/02/An-Empirical-Survey-of-Reject-Inference-Techniques-Lee-Gregory-and-Radostina-Etimova.pdf), [2026 preprint listing](https://chatpaper.com/paper/300778)
- Doubly robust off-policy evaluation (Dudik, Langford and Li 2011) combines a reward model and a logging-policy (propensity) model; accurate if either is good; shrinkage variants (Su et al., ICML 2020) trade bias and variance by shrinking importance weights — [Microsoft Research listing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=580345), [ICML 2020 poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2020/poster/53400)
- Internal data (from `internal_audit.md`, already established): the stored tape is tracked wallets' swaps only; in 93.5% of entries the tape is silent within 60 minutes of entry, so collapses followed by silence are invisible; selection requires a stored swap within 2 minutes, dropping 45% of mints; zero filled trades and zero rug_checks rows to date; the agent already uses Jupiter/Birdeye/GeckoTerminal for `crash_checks`.
- Price source behavior: Jupiter Price v3 is last-swap based and omits untraded tokens; so candle/price pulls from aggregators also stop updating when trading stops — [Jupiter Price v3](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/price/v3). For Birdeye/GeckoTerminal quotas see prior note (GeckoTerminal 30 calls/min; Birdeye tiers).

### Inferences
- **Shadow logging design:** for every candidate that reaches the screen (blocked or passed), store the raw feature vector and verdict at decision time (append-only, not the 6 h latest-verdict upsert noted in the audit), then record the outcome path. Outcome sources, in order of reliability: (1) subscribe to the pool's two vault accounts on the existing gRPC stream for N hours (reserve ratio gives price and exit depth directly, sees every swap and any LP removal, no tracked-wallet dependency; cost estimated as negligible in Q1); (2) aggregator candles sampled at fixed marks (1, 5, 15, 30, 60 min, 6 h, 24 h) as a cross-check and for tokens where the pool cannot be subscribed; (3) a final "can I still sell, and for how much" simulated sell at each mark, which turns "silent" into an explicit outcome. Record "no data" as its own outcome, never as "no drop".
- **Counterfactual PnL for blocked tokens:** feed the logged entry timing and reserve path into the existing copy-fill replay harness (constant-product model) at the observed copy delay, apply the planned exit rules (stop/TP/kill-switch), and compute net PnL after fees and slippage, including exit depth at each time. Report both "rug rate in blocked vs passed" and "mean/median PnL blocked vs passed", with intervals.
- **Exploration as ground truth:** every blocked token gets an inclusion probability e (for example 3-5%, tiny size, capped total exposure) of a real or paper buy. Weight those explored outcomes by 1/e (inverse propensity weighting) and use the shadow-replayed outcomes as the model-based component of a doubly robust estimate. Paper-only exploration tests the screen's classification but not real fill/exit risk; small real buys test both and are the only way to learn recovery rates (Q2). Trade-off: exploration costs money exactly on the tokens the screen says are bad.
- **Survivorship handling:** include tokens that were never priced (no swap in 2 minutes) as explicit outcomes (likely unsellable), include tokens whose pools disappeared, and censor correctly (a token still alive at 24 h is censored, not safe). Condition comparisons on equal windows after the same decision timestamp. Separate leaders' sell-outs from true outcomes (leaders exiting is an informative signal but also a biased one).
- **Sample sizes (my arithmetic, 95% confidence, normal approximation):** for a proportion with half-width d, n = 1.96² p(1−p)/d²: p = 0.5, d = 0.05 → 384; p = 0.2, d = 0.05 → 246; p = 0.1, d = 0.03 → 384. Zero events in n → upper 95% bound about 3/n (100 observations → 3%). For a mean-return difference Δ between blocked and passed groups with an assumed return SD σ = 1.0 (100% of entry; heavy-tailed, unverified), n per arm ≈ 2 × (1.96 + 0.84)² σ²/Δ² ≈ 15.7σ²/Δ²: Δ = 0.20 → 392 per arm; Δ = 0.10 → 1,568 per arm. Weeks needed for the blocked arm if the screen blocks 40% of candidates:

| Candidates/week | Blocked/week | Weeks to 246 | to 384 | to 1,568 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 40 | 6.2 | 9.6 | 39 |
| 300 | 120 | 2.1 | 3.2 | 13 |
| 1,000 | 400 | 0.6 | 1.0 | 3.9 |

  The passed arm (60%) fills faster. Splitting by venue, token-age band or per-threshold (six knobs) multiplies the requirement: practical sample for a single go/no-go on the whole screen is 4-10 weeks at 100/week or 1-3 weeks at 1,000/week; per-threshold tuning realistically needs the upper range and pooled monotone scoring. Increasing the candidate count (shadow-log all leader buys incl. those skipped for lateness or zero balance — the audit shows 741 skipped buys already, and 877 distinct mints in 10 days from copy-weighted leaders) is the cheapest way to shorten the wait.
- Time dependence: memecoin regimes shift (see prior note on CoinGecko wallet-profit swings), so use rolling windows and treat a sample older than about 4-8 weeks with caution.

### Gaps
- No memecoin-specific paper applying IPW/doubly robust or exploration to a rug filter; the reject-inference and off-policy sources are from credit scoring and contextual bandits.
- σ of per-trade returns for this bot unknown; the sample-size table assumes σ = 1.0.
- Exact bandwidth/cost of long-lived vault subscriptions and provider caps on `account` list size and concurrent subscriptions not verified (prior note: caps are provider policy).
- Whether vault updates reliably reflect LP removals on every venue (Raydium v4, CPMM, CLMM, DLMM use different account structures; CLMM/DLMM liquidity is bin/tick-based so vault balance alone overstates executable depth) was not verified.

## Q6. Labeling "rugged" automatically: what papers use and what to adopt

### Takeaway
Published labels split into three families: (a) liquidity-event rules (LP burn at least 99% in one event, Cernera; any removal followed by inactivity, SolRPDS), (b) price/liquidity-collapse rules over a window (price ratio below 0.3 within 20 minutes, MemeTrans; TVL drawdown over 99% or idle over 80% of lifetime, Li et al.), and (c) holder/activity-decline rules (holders down at least 73% within 24 h plus creator profit, SolRugDetector). They label different things, with manual validation only in SolRugDetector (0.26% FP in flagged, 9% FN in unflagged) and MemeTrans (manual review of the ambiguous slice), so use a layered label and keep each component as its own column.

### Cited Findings
- Cernera et al.: pool has exactly one Mint and one Burn event and one address burns at least 99% of minted LP tokens (rounding allowance), for tokens with lifetime under one day — [Cernera et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08202)
- SolRPDS: Inactive = latest swap occurred after a liquidity removal, with no further user interaction until the cutoff (1 Nov 2024); no percentage threshold; the authors call inactivity "a signal for suspicious behavior and not" proof (text cut off in extraction); lifetime = first mint to last activity — [SolRPDS paper](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07132)
- MemeTrans: high risk = minimum price within 20 minutes after migration / migration price below 0.3 (or manually manipulated); low risk = ratio at least 0.7 and not manipulated; medium otherwise; manual review of the ratio at least 0.5 slice on GMGN price-volume trajectories (recurring sell-buy cycles from pre-programmed bots) — [MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480)
- Li et al. (prior note): TVL drawdown over 99% or idle over 80% of lifetime, evaluated at 1 hour; tokens already rugged within the 5-minute feature window dropped — [arXiv 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20271)
- SolRugDetector: three typed rules listed in Q1, preliminary activity filter (transaction rate under τ_active in the last 24 h over the latest 1,000 signatures), holder decline at least 0.73 for pump-and-dump; flagged FP 1/382 (0.26%, 95% upper bound about 1.45%), unflagged FN 9/100 — [arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1)
- Webacy and other glossaries split "hard rug" (sudden complete liquidity removal) from "soft rug" (gradual insider selling) — [Webacy glossary via search](https://docs.webacy.com/glossary/rug-pulls)

### Inferences
Suggested layered label for GodsEye (design; fields to store separately so definitions can be changed later):
1. **Hard rug (event):** quote reserves of the pool fall by at least 90% from their running maximum (or LP supply burned/removed at least 99% by one holder) within a short window (for example 10 minutes), observed on the vault stream.
2. **Collapse (price, relative to your entry):** reserve-implied price or executable sell value falls to at most 30% of entry within 20 minutes (MemeTrans-style) or at most 10% within 60 minutes; also store the fraction at 1, 5, 15, 60 min, 6 h.
3. **Dead (activity):** no swaps in N hours after a collapse (SolRPDS/SolRugDetector style); N around 1-6 h is a hypothesis, test sensitivity with 0.5-5 tx/hour as SolRugDetector reports.
4. **Unexitable (the label that matters for this bot):** a simulated sell of your actual position size returns under 10% of cost at any mark. This is bot-specific and captures freeze/honeypot as well as drained liquidity.
5. **Manual audit slice:** sample 50-100 labeled and unlabeled cases for human review (as SolRugDetector's 382-flag and 100-unflagged samples and MemeTrans' ambiguous-slice review) to estimate FP/FN of the rules; label noise bounds any threshold validation.
- Windows trade off: a short window (MemeTrans 20 min) misses slow soft rugs; a long window (24 h) mixes in natural decay. Report both and choose by the bot's actual hold-time distribution (seconds to hours).
- Do not mix "price down 70%" with "rug". Most memecoins decline; a drop label measures risk to the position, a liquidity-event label measures malicious pulls. The screen should be optimised against the position-loss label (collapse/unexitable); the liquidity-event label is for diagnosing which precursors matter.
- Keep the tape-silence censoring explicit: with reserve data, "no swap" is observable; without it, label as unknown, not survived.

### Gaps
- No label has been validated against independent ground truth at scale except SolRugDetector's small manual samples; none validated for the copy-trader's holding horizons.
- Exact SolRPDS rule for "suspicious" pools beyond inactivity and the end of the sentence quoted above (PDF text extraction cut off) not read; Li et al. details only via prior note.
- The τ_active inconsistency (5 vs 55 tx/hour) in SolRugDetector is unresolved.
- No source defines "rugged" for CLMM/DLMM concentrated-liquidity pools where one-sided liquidity can sit outside the active range.
