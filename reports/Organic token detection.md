# Separate organic demand from smart-money churn

**Bottom line.** For GodsEye, "organic" should mean a token whose recent flow comes from several independent wallets: not funded by the same source, not bots or followers of one another, not recycling the same size in round trips. Those wallets must take net positions at prices they did not set themselves, in a pool that can still be exited. It should not mean a high Jupiter score or "has a DexScreener page", because Jupiter defines organic negatively (wallets not on a bot or copy-platform list count as organic) and its formula, weights and label cutoffs are unpublished ([Jupiter blog](https://developers.jup.ag/blog/what-is-organic-score)). The owner's complaint has two causes that compound; it is not a token missing from an index. First, **in a live sample of 100 high-volume Meteora DBC tokens, DexScreener indexed all 100**, but none of the 99 DBC pairs carries a `liquidity` field, and the 64 tokens with a post-migration DAMM v2 pair show a **median liquidity of $0.98 and an FDV of exactly 1 for 32 of them**, so the page reads as dead because the pair is drained or the liquidity field is absent ([DexScreener API](https://api.dexscreener.com/latest/dex/tokens/HiTtfNdK1rmZtXFv9wquxBCqBbVWzkqgHtqtSDj4iKoY); researchers' measurement of 2026-10-27; an outright missing token was not reproduced). Second, **GodsEye's smart-flow path never checks a market** (no liquidity, volume, trader count or pool) and the feed ranks by net 24h USD, so one smart wallet buying hundreds of times tops the list: in the simulated feed at 2026-10-25T19:09Z, BIRB ranked first on 381 flows from one buyer and one seller (net +$24,319) ([smart-money path note, section 4](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/research_notes/Organic%20token%20detection/godseye_smart_flow_path.md)). Third, **68 of 192 recent signals contain wallets sharing a funder**, with one signal of 10 wallets all from one funder clearing the $500 floor at $540. The fix is to measure before gating: add a stream-wide per-mint tally of every swap (free, available at once), log it, then rank and gate on independent buyers; use Jupiter's token API only as a log-only second opinion; and widen venue coverage to Meteora DBC deliberately, because today a DBC-only trade never reaches the stream and these tokens look thinner than they are. No published wash-trading detector reports an error rate, so every threshold below is the researchers' proposal to calibrate on GodsEye's own data. Insider-pattern wallets stay copyable, since this changes how a token's flow is credited and ranked, not which wallets are copied, and the public site stays read-only and redacted. This report informs the design; nothing in it has been implemented.

## Organic means independent, exit-able demand, and no vendor measures that directly

GodsEye needs three separate answers that vendors tend to blur. The first is whether a market exists: a pool with enough liquidity that a buyer can sell. The second is whether the activity is manufactured: volume recycled by a few wallets or bought by a farm. The third is whether the smart-wallet confirmation is independent: whether three smart buyers are three opinions or one operator and two followers. A token can pass any one and fail another, and the owner's complaint touches all three.

Jupiter's Organic Score is the only industry system that scores this at scale, and its design limits what it can prove. It is a 0-100 composite of organic volume, holders, traders and buyers, normalised against the rest of the ecosystem, and classification is per wallet: wallets tied to known sniping, copy-trading or automated-execution platforms are non-organic, and everything else defaults to organic ([Jupiter docs](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/tokens/organic-score); [Jupiter blog](https://developers.jup.ag/blog/what-is-organic-score)). Two consequences follow from that design, and both are inferences from Jupiter's published description rather than tested findings. A farm built from fresh plain wallets funded with SOL dust would plausibly count as organic, and wallets reaching a token through copy-trading platforms count as non-organic, so a token bought mainly by smart wallets' followers will be under-credited. Jupiter's own worked numbers show how small the "organic" slice is even for healthy tokens: SOL scores 98.9 with 0.5% of buy volume organic, TRUMP 85.3 with 0.7%, and ASPEN 45.1 with $1,034 of $1.61M and 11 organic buyers among 3,034 traders ([Jupiter blog](https://developers.jup.ag/blog/what-is-organic-score)). No independent accuracy study of the score exists. The one open-source agent examined shows it for display only and gates on the `audit.isSus` flag instead, without stating why ([Meridian PR #107](https://github.com/fciaf420/meridian/pull/107)).

The independent literature gives measurement ideas but not validated thresholds. Every Solana wash-trading study reports counts under conservative rules, and none reports precision or recall; the cleanest statements of error are "lower bound" and "false positives grow with window length" ([Meme Coin Factories](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1); [Mongardini and Mei](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v1); [Wiedenmann thesis](https://www.cs.cit.tum.de/fileadmin/w00cfj/sebis/thesis/250610_Wiedenmann_Thesis.pdf)). The window parameter, not the concept, drives the headline: on pump.fun a strict same-transaction round trip touches 8.26% of coins and 1.32% of volume, while a loose 5-second matched round trip touches 44% of coins ([Meme Coin Factories](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1)). The same study found wash trading strongly tied to graduation (0.90% of coins graduate overall, 9.64% of coins with 500 or more wash transactions), so volume and even migration are gameable. The tolerances that recur across papers are 1% (Victor and Weintraud, Wiedenmann), 1.25% plus 15,000 lamports (Meme Coin Factories) and 2% (Mongardini and Mei); Chainalysis, on EVM chains, requires at least 3 matched trades per address ([Victor and Weintraud](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.07001); [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-market-manipulation-wash-trading-pump-and-dump-2025/)). Every threshold the tables below mark as a proposal is the researchers' own and unvalidated.

### Signals GodsEye can compute from its own stream, free and at once

The stream already sees every swap by every wallet, but GodsEye keeps only per-wallet counters and one per-mint record (last price, last-seen, first-seen), so none of these exist today ([smart-money.ts](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/smart/smart-money.ts), lines 91-98 and 124-151). "Real swap" below means after removing known protocol or agent flow, atomic MEV and the launch bundle. All ratio signals need a minimum denominator (the notes propose at least 30 swaps) and should output "insufficient evidence" below it rather than a pass.

| Signal | Definition | Provenance | Starting threshold | Failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct makers and effective traders | Unique signer wallets in window; effective traders = 1 / sum of squared volume shares | Concept standard; effective-buyer idea is the researchers' | No published cutoff. Proposal: judge makers against the age-bucket distribution of GodsEye's own stream | Counts signers, not owners, so a router or relay can merge or split people; sybils defeat it |
| Swaps per maker | Swaps / unique signers; share of swaps from wallets with 10+ swaps | Bitquery case: 174 trades per wallet on a pure-wash token ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/blog/solana-volume-numbers-are-a-lie)); Solidus uses trades-to-traders of 2 or more on mature ETH pools ([Solidus](https://soliduslabs.com/reports/crypto-wash-trading)) | Proposal: flag when over 50% of swaps come from wallets with 10+ swaps in a token under 1 hour old, or any wallet over about 50 swaps in the first hour | Real scalpers, market makers and arbitrage bots also trade often; one-trade-per-wallet farms evade it |
| Concentration | Top-1 and top-3 share of gross (buy plus sell) volume, excluding pool and program accounts | Qualitative only (Wiedenmann: two accounts near 5,000 SOL each, rest at baseline) | Proposal: top-3 above about 50% on a token with over 100 swaps is suspicious; top-1 above 30% needs explanation | A creator, sniper or legitimate market maker also concentrates volume |
| Round-trip share and repeat-cycle wallets | Same wallet buys then sells within a window at near-equal token amount; share = 2 x matched volume / gross volume; count wallets with 3 or more matched cycles | Tolerances 1%, 1.25% plus fees, 2% and the 3-cycle minimum are sourced (above); windows of 1 s, 5 s, 3 min, 1 h come from Meme Coin Factories and Wiedenmann | Proposal: treat as hard evidence only when repeated 3 or more times, run windows of 5 s, 3 min and 60 min side by side | A lone round trip is a normal scalper or MEV pattern; multi-pool routes hide cycles; Wiedenmann concedes failed-MEV false positives |
| Gross/net churn | Gross volume / absolute net volume, and gross volume / peak curve or pool reserve | Mongardini and Mei: 99% of a day's volume from makers who both bought and sold (hard-evidence tier) | Proposal: gross/net above about 10 in the first hour while over 50% of gross comes from wallets with 3 or more cycles | Early curves legitimately turn over tiny reserves; two-sided real trading balances out |
| Near-identical sizes | Per wallet, coefficient of variation of swap size over 10 or more swaps; modal-size share | Bitquery: "tight $13 increments" | Proposal: coefficient of variation below about 0.05 or modal-size share above 50% | Fixed-size copy bots and DCA also repeat sizes; operators can jitter |
| Timing regularity | Coefficient of variation of inter-swap intervals; share of intervals within 10% of the mode; synchronised bursts across wallets | Wiedenmann: wash intervals cluster near 100 s | Proposal: coefficient of variation below about 0.3, or over 60% of intervals within 10% of the mode, over 10 or more swaps | Bots can randomise; sub-slot intervals point to MEV rather than wash |
| Co-funded and fresh-funded share | Share of gross volume from wallets sharing a first funder (or funder-of-funder), or first funded within N hours of first swap | Bitquery: one funder matched 30 of 30 flagged wallets (200 bots at 0.5 SOL in 52 s); two of five suspects were funded by unique 0.002 SOL senders and escaped it; Chainalysis: a controller funding 5 or more addresses | Proposal: N of 1, 6 and 24 hours, logged raw; 5 or more wallets from one funder trading the same token is high confidence | Dust-from-unique-sender farms are invisible; exchange hot wallets over-merge unless excluded; GodsEye knows the funder for only 39% of signal wallets |
| Known-bot and protocol share | Share of gross volume from wallets on a bot list, plus protocol flow such as pump.fun agent trades | Jupiter treats such wallets as non-organic; a replication package holds seed Trojan and MEV addresses ([arXiv 2607.28424](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28424)), not opened by the researchers | No threshold; log only | Lists lag new tooling; any list under-counts |
| Non-smart, non-bot, non-follower buyer share | Share of gross buy volume from wallets that are none of: smart-listed, bot-platform, flagged cluster, follower | Researchers' proposal only | Proposal: near zero after the first minutes means the demand is a closed loop | Depends on the quality of the follower and cluster labels |
| Persistent farm-operator flag | Wallets with 3 or more matched cycles, or top-3 volume share, in 5 or more tokens that each died within a day | Persistence across tokens is the strongest recurring pattern (Wiedenmann, Mongardini and Mei, Chen et al., Meme Coin Factories) | Proposal: 7- and 30-day lookbacks | Needs stored history and a definition of "died"; no public wallet list to seed it |

The notes recommend combining signals in tiers rather than OR-ing them, because single signals are noisy and running a dozen over thousands of tokens produces spurious flags at any fixed cutoff. Tier 1 is repeated near-exact round trips by funder-linked wallets; Tier 2 is high gross/net plus high concentration among unlinked wallets; Tier 3 is a single soft signal, which should not discount a token. Cutoffs are best set as percentiles within an age bucket (0-5 min, 5-30 min, 30 min-6 h, 6-24 h) from GodsEye's own stream, since no published age-conditioned distribution exists. Cong et al.'s size-distribution tests (Benford, rounding) need hundreds of swaps and break on round-number users, so they belong only at token level on mature tokens ([NBER w30783](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30783)).

### Signals from providers, and what they cost

Provider data adds what the stream cannot see (pool liquidity, holders, creator history, the market outside GodsEye's tracked venues) at the price of rate limits and unmeasured freshness.

| Signal | What it is | Provenance | Starting threshold | Limits and freshness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jupiter `organicScore` | 0-100 relative composite | Vendor; formula unpublished; no independent accuracy study | None sourced. Proposal: log it, never gate on it alone; score 0 with thousands of trades is a strong negative | Jupiter says fresh tokens have volatile scores; whether it is populated in a token's first minutes is unmeasured |
| Jupiter `stats5m/1h/6h/24h`: `numTraders`, `numOrganicBuyers`, `numNetBuyers`, `buyOrganicVolume` | Counts over windows; organic buyers are organic wallets whose buys exceeded sells | Vendor definitions from the blog; sub-fields not defined on the reference page | None sourced. Proposal: prefer these counts to the label | `numOrganicBuyers` is a net-buyer count, so it undercounts early one-way buyers; `numTraders` includes bots |
| Jupiter `audit` (`isSus`, `devMints`, authorities, top holders) and `holderCount` | Safety and creator-history fields | `isSus` seen in live responses but undocumented; `devMints` is the dev wallet's token count | None sourced. Proposal: use `devMints` as a farm tell | In the DBC sample, 42 of 100 were `isSus` and 42 devs had 20 or more mints; vendor field, researcher's tally |
| Jupiter `liquidity`, `graduatedPool`, `graduatedAt`, `launchpad` | Market-existence and lifecycle fields | Vendor | Proposal: post-migration liquidity of a few dollars means no exit | Single source for graduation lookups off-chain |
| GeckoTerminal `buyers`/`sellers` (m5 to h24), `reserve_in_usd`, `volume_usd` | Per-pool unique-wallet counts and reserves | Vendor; wallet attribution undefined ([CoinGecko docs](https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/trending-pools-list.md)) | None | Migrated DBC pools report zero reserve yet keep that day's volume; the 60 s/30 s cache claim is unconfirmed |
| DexScreener `txns`, `volume`, `liquidity` | Buy/sell counts, no makers field in the schema read | Vendor ([API reference](https://docs.dexscreener.com/api/reference)) | None | No `liquidity` object on DBC pairs; the token endpoint returns about 30 pairs per call and silently drops tokens beyond that |
| Solana Tracker and Birdeye unique buyers/wallets | Per-window unique counts | Field names and plan gating unverified | None | Not tested |
| Volume-to-liquidity and holder count | Ratio and count | MadeOnSol rules of thumb (above 20:1 suspicious, 1:1 to 10:1 organic, under 100 holders on a high-volume token a warning), unsourced vendor heuristics ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-volume-bots-explained)) | Treat as priors, not thresholds | Hot thin tokens legitimately exceed 20:1 |

Jupiter's keyless tier on `api.jup.ag` allows 0.5 requests per second (30 per minute), a free key 1 per second, Developer 10, Launch 50 and Pro 150; all share one bucket across swap, price and token calls, and one search call accepts up to 100 mints ([rate limits](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/portal/rate-limits); [Token Search v2](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/tokens/v2/search)). The old `lite-api.jup.ag` host is described as being phased out, and the docs disagree on whether a key is needed ([migration guide](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/portal/migration.md)). That budget is ample for a watchlist of signal candidates and display rows, but not for per-swap lookups. In the 100-token DBC sample, 90 carried the "low" label and 10 "medium", and the median `numOrganicBuyers` was 0, so on churn-heavy DBC launches the label barely discriminates. The raw counts do: EDGE shows 2,374 buys and 2,475 sells from only 130 traders (about 37 trades per trader, the researchers' arithmetic) with an organic score of 0 ([Jupiter Tokens v2](https://lite-api.jup.ag/tokens/v2/search?query=HiTtfNdK1rmZtXFv9wquxBCqBbVWzkqgHtqtSDj4iKoY)). The Jupiter freshness question matters most for GodsEye and is unmeasured: leaders buy a median 34 seconds after the pool appears ([Bubble maps for rug checks](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/reports/Bubble%20maps%20for%20rug%20checks.md)), so no provider reading will exist at entry, which is another reason these signals belong in feed credit and signal creation, not an entry gate.

## Meteora DBC tokens look empty because they graduate in minutes and the new pool is drained

Meteora's Dynamic Bonding Curve is one Anchor program, `dbcij3LWUppWqq96dh6gJWwBifmcGfLSB5D4DuSMaqN`, where a launchpad "partner" creates a config and each token gets a virtual pool trading on a piecewise curve until quote reserves reach the config's `migration_quote_threshold` ([DBC README](https://github.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-bonding-curve)). A keeper then migrates it into a Meteora DAMM v2 pool (program `cpamdpZCGKUy5JxQXB4dcpGPiikHawvSWAd6mEn1sGG`); Meteora's keeper auto-migrates only configs whose threshold equals listed values such as 10 SOL or 750 USDC, and other thresholds need a manual migration ([DBC developer guide](https://docs.meteora.ag/developer-guides/dbc.md)). Two details matter for a builder. Migration emits no DBC event (the source carries a TODO), so the stream-side graduation signal is the `migration_damm_v2` instruction itself, while `EvtCurveComplete` marks "curve full, awaiting migration" ([migration source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-bonding-curve/main/programs/dynamic-bonding-curve/src/instructions/migration/dynamic_amm_v2/migrate_damm_v2_initialize_pool.rs); [events docs](https://docs.meteora.ag/developer-guides/dbc/program/events.md)). And because the threshold is partner-configured and cheap at 10 SOL, graduation says almost nothing about demand: in the sample, **52 of 100 graduated with a median of 5.6 minutes from first pool to graduation (range 1.2 to 44.7)**. That sample is the top 100 pools by 24h volume on GeckoTerminal from roughly 2026-10-26 03:00 to 23:00 UTC, so it is biased toward high-volume, very new pools and is not representative of all DBC tokens ([GeckoTerminal DBC pools](https://api.geckoterminal.com/api/v2/networks/solana/dexes/meteora-dbc/pools?page=1)).

What the sample shows after graduation is the pattern the owner is seeing. The median graduated-pool liquidity was **about $0.49 with a median 24h price change of -100%**, against about $111 median liquidity for the 48 not yet graduated; 70 distinct partner configs sat behind 68 distinct dev wallets, with median `devMints` of 13 and 28 devs at 100 or more ([Jupiter Tokens v2 batch](https://lite-api.jup.ag/tokens/v2/search?query=HiTtfNdK1rmZtXFv9wquxBCqBbVWzkqgHtqtSDj4iKoY); researchers' tally). The EDGE case is typical: pool created 19:48:31Z, graduated 20:00:21Z, dev wallet with 369 prior mints, DAMM v2 liquidity $0.62, and GeckoTerminal showing 2,213 buys and 2,305 sells from only 112 buyers and 110 sellers ([GeckoTerminal](https://api.geckoterminal.com/api/v2/networks/solana/dexes/meteora-dbc/pools?page=1)). The researchers read this as automated launch farming plus volume churn, possibly to harvest creator and partner fees or to bait copy-trading and screener feeds; that is their interpretation, and Meteora publishes no statement on DBC abuse. The mechanism of the drain (creators or partners claiming or removing LP) is also unverified on chain. For scale, an August 2025 Jupiter-data snapshot put DBC at 35.3% of Solana token issuance against pump.fun's 49.3%, but that is a year old and secondhand ([MEXC summary](https://www.mexc.com/news/80561)).

So why does DexScreener look empty? In the sample every token had a pair. All 99 DBC pairs (`dexId: meteoradbc`) lack the `liquidity` object entirely, though 96 of 99 show non-zero 24h volume and FDV; the 64 migrated tokens show a second `meteora` pair labelled `DYN2` with median liquidity $0.98, 50 of 64 under $10 and 32 of 64 with FDV exactly 1 ([DexScreener tokens API](https://api.dexscreener.com/latest/dex/tokens/HiTtfNdK1rmZtXFv9wquxBCqBbVWzkqgHtqtSDj4iKoY)). The likeliest causes of "no data" are therefore a page resolving to the drained DAMM v2 pair, or a UI or filter that needs a liquidity figure DBC pairs never supply; the third candidate, an indexer lag on a brand-new pool, was not reproduced. DexScreener's website behaviour was never observed, only its API. One measurement trap deserves a warning: querying 30 mints at once silently dropped 13 tokens that reappeared when queried 5 at a time. The post-hoc DexScreener snapshot in GodsEye's own analysis used batches of up to 30 mints and found 765 of 1,296 window mints (59%) with no pairs, so that figure is probably overstated and must be re-run before anyone relies on it, although a second endpoint also returned nothing for at least the Marvin token ([smart-money path note, section 4](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/research_notes/Organic%20token%20detection/godseye_smart_flow_path.md)).

### What GodsEye's venue detection misses

GodsEye's venue table maps only the DLMM (`LBUZ...`) and Dynamic AMM v1 (`Eo7W...`) programs to "meteora", and has neither DBC nor DAMM v2 ([programs.ts](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/chain/programs.ts), lines 7-17). Three consequences follow, each established from the code in the notes. The gRPC subscription filters on `accountInclude` for those nine programs, so a transaction that touches only DBC or only DAMM v2 is never delivered ([stream.ts](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/stream/stream.ts), lines 125-139). A DBC swap routed through Jupiter does arrive, but `detectVenue` checks top-level programs first, finds Jupiter and labels it `jupiter` (or `jupiter (via bot)`), hiding the DBC pool; one that reaches the parser with no listed program anywhere would return null ([parse.ts](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/chain/parse.ts), lines 97-110). And `smart_flows` carries no venue column, so the venue is recoverable only by joining to `wallet_swaps`. The database confirms the stream hides rather than flags the unknown: **0 of 33,827 window flows have a null venue**, while 8.9% of tape swaps do, mostly from RPC history backfills. A DBC token therefore appears in GodsEye only through its Jupiter-routed slice, and a stream-side "thin token" test would over-flag DBC tokens: 52 of 118 Jupiter-dominant mints (44%) have three or fewer tape wallets. No row identifies a swap as DBC, so the owner's specific tokens are unconfirmed; the researchers predict the Jupiter label and name JUFF (`vNJrbTyZ...`) as the first signature to decode.

The detection recipe itself is cheap. Match the DBC program id in top-level and inner instructions; recognise swaps by the first eight data bytes (`swap` f8c69e91e17587c8, `swap2` 414b3f4ceb5b5b88, hex converted from the published IDL); read curve progress from `EvtSwap2`, which carries `quote_reserve_amount` and `migration_threshold` without any RPC call; and read pool state from account offsets that the researchers computed from the IDL and that one third-party pull request corroborates for the config and creator fields ([IDL](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-bonding-curve-sdk/main/packages/dynamic-bonding-curve/src/idl/dynamic-bonding-curve/idl.json); [opentrench PR 19](https://github.com/frogeth/opentrench/pull/19)). The offsets were not read from a live account, and no real stream transaction containing a Jupiter route with a DBC hop was decoded, so inner-instruction ordering is unconfirmed.

### Subscribing to DBC trades accuracy against wallet-promotion noise

Adding DBC and DAMM v2 to the subscription would make the per-mint tally accurate for these tokens and make venue-level rules possible, such as "a pre-graduation DBC token needs more distinct makers". The cost is a larger stream and, more importantly, a flood of the exact wallets that look active: a farm wallet with hundreds of round trips per day is the kind of address that fills a wallet book. The notes do not measure either the added load (about 5.6 swaps per second average today, at least 17 wallets per second at peak, both derived) or how many farm wallets would reach promotion; they do show that a bot gate exists on the smart set, using hold time, transaction rate and same-block round trips ([smart-money path note, sections 2 and 5](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/research_notes/Organic%20token%20detection/godseye_smart_flow_path.md)). The sensible design, a suggestion rather than a finding, is to subscribe in log-only mode first, send DBC swaps to the per-mint tally, and keep them out of wallet promotion until a count of how many DBC wallets would pass the existing gates is in hand. A cheaper alternative that avoids the trade-off is to read graduation, liquidity and `devMints` from Jupiter's token API at signal time. Copy execution has its own hazards on this venue: anti-sniper base fees can run as high as 99% decaying over the first slots (Meteora's docs give the 99% ceiling; the decay numbers are third-party and unverified), so a copy buy seconds after creation can fill at a very different effective price, and Jupiter's `priceImpactPct` reported 0.93% for 0.1 SOL into EDGE's $0.62-liquidity pool while the implied price was roughly 1,400 times below the DBC price ([fees docs](https://docs.meteora.ag/core-products/dbc/fees.md); [Jupiter quote](https://lite-api.jup.ag/swap/v1/quote?inputMint=So11111111111111111111111111111111111111112&outputMint=HiTtfNdK1rmZtXFv9wquxBCqBbVWzkqgHtqtSDj4iKoY&amount=100000000&slippageBps=100)). The impact field is therefore not a reliable guard on drained pools; a liquidity floor is.

## Three smart wallets are one opinion if they share a funder

The signal rule asks for 3 distinct wallets with positive net USD inside 60 minutes and at least $500 combined, and it never asks whether those wallets are related ([config.ts](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/config.ts), lines 62-66). The database shows they often are. Across the 192 signals in the 2026-10-22 to 10-25 window, the funder is known for only 441 of 1,134 signal-wallet rows (39%), yet **68 signals have at least two wallets sharing a known funder and 30 have at least half their wallets sharing one**; signal 759 (WAKARU) had 10 wallets with the same funder and $540 total, about $54 each. Because 61% of funders are unknown, these are lower bounds. Signal 724 shows the opposite failure: three wallets ($19, $551, $208) on a token whose entire tape was 7 swaps by 7 wallets and 8.7 SOL ([smart-money path note, section 2](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/research_notes/Organic%20token%20detection/godseye_smart_flow_path.md)). A related defect sits in the return measurement: prices are the last swap price seen from any wallet, so on a thin token the "return" is whatever the next trade pays, which can be a smart wallet's own follow-up, and these returns feed the follow-bar learner and the buys-tops drop rule.

Funder identity is only one way wallets fail independence, and it misses the cheapest farms. The independent evidence on the other failure, followers, is the Luo et al. study of about 6,000 meme coin projects (WWW '26): a sniper's early buy attracts copiers who replicate it, manipulators profit from copy traders as exit liquidity, and smart-money trades averaged +14% while the estimated copier return was +3% per trade after frictions ([Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)). Marino et al. likewise found that conditioning on successful traders buying yields only modest uplift and stays below breakeven ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14860)). No study measured herding among smart wallets or follower-lag distributions, so the procedure the notes propose is a design: build a graph over the wallets that bought a token, with edges for exact links (shared fee payer, direct transfers), shared funder or funder-of-funder, shared bot or relay path, and a follower relation (wallet B buys the same token within roughly 30-60 seconds of wallet A at similar size, and has done so to A on at least 3 other tokens). Collapse each connected component to one vote and report independent smart voters and effective buyers (inverse HHI of component-level volume). A follower is not wrong money, but counting it doubles the leader's vote, and a leader followed by many bots creates demand that vanishes when the leader sells. Smart-wallet counts and organic-buyer counts should stay in separate fields, since the first is heavily selected and correlated and the second is the evidence of broad demand.

### How much evidence is enough: binomial bounds

No source publishes a minimum sample for judging a token's flow, and the published detectors work over days and hundreds of transactions. The numbers below are the researchers' own arithmetic, not a sourced result. If none of n sampled buyers is linked to an operator, the one-sided 95% upper bound on the operator-controlled share is 1 − 0.05^(1/n):

| Independent buyers (n) | 3 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 30 | 50 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 95% upper bound on controlled share | 63% | 45% | 26% | 18% | 14% | 9.5% | 5.8% | 3.0% |

The n = 3 column, which is today's signal minimum, is the researchers' extension of the same formula. For an observed flagged share, Wilson 95% intervals are wide: 2 flagged of 10 buyers spans 5.7% to 51%, 3 of 30 spans 3.5% to 25.6%, and 10 of 30 spans 19.2% to 51.2%, so below about 30 independent buyers "5% manipulated" cannot be told from "40% manipulated". Two caveats cut against reading these as comfortable. These are best-case bounds, because they assume buyers can be classified independently and linking recall is poor (dust-funded farms are invisible, and GodsEye knows only 39% of funders); and smart buyers are a selected, correlated sample, not random draws. The researchers' proposal is therefore that a token counts as having independent demand once it has at least about 30 distinct buyers and 15 distinct non-flagged, non-bot, non-follower buyers who still hold, observed over 15-30 minutes and at least two separate bursts, to rule out a single bundle. That is far above the current 3-wallet rule and takes longer than GodsEye's entry window, so it belongs on feed credit, not on entry. Keep scores monotone and saturating in independent-buyer count, as Jupiter does, show the sample size next to every ratio, and report "insufficient evidence" instead of a pass when the denominator is small.

## The feed records everything, signals on three addresses, and ranks by net dollars

A swap becomes a `smart_flows` row if its wallet is in the active smart set (1,296 wallets) and its mint is not on the major or wrapped list; nothing checks liquidity, volume, trader count, venue, token age or pool existence ([smart-money.ts](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/smart/smart-money.ts), lines 124-181). There is no minimum USD on a flow row: in the window, 183 flows were under $1 and 1,942 under $10. The non-memecoin check uses the cached symbol, so a mint with no symbol passes (96 of 1,296 had none), and `7c52LfFG...pump` is the example: no symbol, two buys of $102 and $18,327, three tracked wallets, and rank two in the simulated feed at net +$18,429. External APIs only name tokens (Shyft, then GeckoTerminal as a fallback); RugCheck, Birdeye and Jupiter are not used in `smart/` or `stream/`. Token "age" on a signal is the time since this process first saw a priced swap, so it resets on restart (one signal shows 3e-5 minutes). The simulated feed query sorts by `buy_usd_24h - sell_usd_24h` and takes 40 rows; the Home page then shows the top 5 flows and 3 signals, each linked to DexScreener, with only symbol, buyer and seller counts and net USD ([dashboard-data.ts](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/shared/src/dashboard-data.ts), lines 356-413; [page.tsx](/Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/dashboard/app/page.tsx), lines 37-39 and 111-150). Where a filter fits depends on which damage the owner wants to stop, and the stages need different treatment.

| Stage | Today | Proposed treatment (design suggestion) |
|---|---|---|
| Flow recording (`smart_flows`) | Records any smart swap above 0.0001 SOL | Keep recording everything; annotate with the per-mint tally and the organic verdict at write time, so history can be re-scored as thresholds change |
| Signal creation (`smart_signals`) | 3 wallets, $500, 60 min, 6 h cooldown | Count funder-distinct components, not addresses; mark or suppress signals whose token fails the tally; log first, enforce after calibration |
| Display ranking (Home, `LIMIT 40`) | Net 24h USD | Filter or rank in SQL before the `LIMIT` so the 40 slots are not wasted; rank by independent buyers or effective buyers rather than net dollars; show a "thin" badge and the sample size |
| Learning inputs (follow-bar learner, buys-tops rule) | All flows | Exclude non-organic flows so thin-token returns stop training the thresholds |

For the tally itself, the notes sketch a per-mint structure (swaps, buys, sells, volume, first and last seen, a bounded set of distinct makers capped near 32, and a top-1/top-3 tracker) hooked either in `SmartMoney.onSwap` between the price update and the smart-wallet check, or in the `stream.ts` loop beside the live book, persisted as new columns or a `token_stats` table. Memory is estimated at 200-400 bytes per mint, 60-120 MB for 300,000 mints; the researchers did not benchmark it, and the distinct-mint count per day was never measured. Existing patterns support a pure, exported verdict function with unit tests in the style of `detectSignal`, plus one end-to-end test that feeds swaps from a non-smart wallet.

## Recommendations are ranked by evidence, and most thresholds still need local calibration

The ranking weighs how well the evidence supports the change against cost. "Evidence-backed" means the problem or gap is established from code, database or sourced measurement; "design suggestion" means the remedy or its threshold is the researchers' proposal. Nothing in the table is implemented.

| Rank | Change | Type and evidence strength | Cost and effort | How to validate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add a stream-wide per-mint tally (distinct makers, swaps per maker, top-1/top-3 share, buys/sells, volume, first/last seen), log-only first | Gap evidence-backed (strong: no per-mint counters exist); metric choice design suggestion | Medium; memory dominated by the maker set | Run a week of logging, compare distributions by age bucket, and hand-label 200-300 tokens to score each signal |
| 2 | Re-rank and filter the Home feed on independent buyers or effective buyers before `LIMIT 40`; badge thin rows | Evidence-backed (strong: BIRB, BMT, and the 3.7%-of-dollars cost); cutoff a design suggestion | Hours; a tape-based stopgap needs no new data | Re-run the simulated feed on stored flows; list which rows drop and check they are single-wallet churn |
| 3 | Count signals by funder-distinct components, not addresses; backfill first funder for signal wallets | Evidence-backed (strong: 68 of 192, signal 759); unknown-funder handling a design choice | Small to medium; funder backfill needs RPC | Compare later returns of independent vs shared-funder signals once returns exist (many 6 h returns are missing) |

## Conclusion

The owner asked for "organic", but the notes show the question splits into market existence, manufactured activity and independent confirmation, and GodsEye is currently blind on all three for a reason that is structural rather than a bad threshold: it records every smart swap without any view of the other wallets trading the same mint. The tally is the missing instrument, and nearly every other fix (ranking, signal independence, learner hygiene, a Jupiter cross-check) depends on it. The most useful non-obvious result is that two defects compound: the stream cannot see DBC-only trades, so a naive "thin token" test would penalise exactly the tokens it cannot observe, which means venue coverage must be settled before thin labels become gates, and an empty-looking DexScreener page is more often a drained pair than a missing token, which makes liquidity at signal time a better question than presence in an index.

The evidence also changes what "smart" can claim. Jupiter's definition counts wallets reaching a token through copy platforms as non-organic, Luo et al.'s copier-return gap shows followers dilute the edge, and GodsEye's own data show a third of its recent signals include co-funded wallets. A smart-money signal is therefore best read as a small number of opinions, and the research supports reporting that number openly and keeping insiders copyable while crediting only independent demand. What remains unsettled is calibration: no detector in the literature has a published error rate, so the first value GodsEye can add is its own labelled set, measured before any gate is switched on.
