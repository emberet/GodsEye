# Copy-Fill Replay Beats Smarter Wallet Rankings

The most valuable upgrade for GodsEye is not a better way to find "smart" wallets but a way to measure what a follower with GodsEye's own latency would actually have earned from each wallet. The public evidence says that wallet identification is the easy half: one preprint found that statistics-only models could pick out skilled memecoin wallets accurately and still produced **negative copier returns**, with smart wallets averaging **14% per coin versus about 3% for copiers** even when the model assumed zero delay ([arXiv 2601.08641, WWW'26](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641)). An unattributed two-week experiment reported that entering **two minutes** after large wallet clusters lost about **41% per token** across 93 tokens ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/investing/35409)). Every commercial tool ranks wallets on backward-looking realized PnL and none publishes a validated methodology, so GodsEye's current 30-day realized-PnL gate sits at the industry standard, which is a low bar. The upgrades with the best ratio of evidence to effort are (1) replaying each leader trade at a swept delay with a pool-state fill model, including for insider-pattern wallets, which stay in the copy set as you decided; (2) shrinkage-based wallet scoring with a walk-forward persistence test; (3) collapsing wallets that share an operator into one vote in the 3-wallet consensus rule; and (4) a token-structure risk layer on top of consensus. Infrastructure is a lower priority because 200 ms slots shrink provider differences to a rounding error next to your own execution path. Public evidence is thin throughout: no independent study measures out-of-sample profit for tracked Solana wallets or commercial copy-bots, and the roadmap at the end labels which items rest on sources and which on inference.

## Almost nothing here is independently validated

The research behind this report found a consistent pattern: the claims that matter most for a copy-trader are the least tested. Peer-reviewed or preprint evidence exists for token-level risk (rug rates, bundling, manipulation), for one copier-versus-leader gap, and for wallet persistence on a different venue. Vendor blogs, search-snippet summaries and one unattributed news-reported experiment carry the rest. Three source problems recur and should be kept in mind. First, **nearly every latency benchmark is published by a vendor selling a competing product** (RPC Fast, Shyft, OrbitFlare, Shreder, Alchemy); no independent provider ranking was found. Second, several leaderboard and "consensus" numbers come from MadeOnSol, which sells a competing API, and its backtest methodology is not shown. Third, the one academic paper on coordinated sniper cohorts, arXiv 2607.02795, **withdrew all of its numerical results on 5 October 2026** because its buyer records included sells and missed many buys ([arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)). Only its method (union-find over a launch co-occurrence graph) is reusable, and its method is itself unvalidated. Where a figure below comes only from a search-engine summary of a page that could not be fetched, the text says so. Statements marked "inference" are engineering reasoning from the sources, not findings.

## Vendors rank by backward realized PnL and no one has shown it persists

Nearly every product ranks wallets primarily on realized PnL over 30- or 90-day windows with trade-count and win-rate floors on top. The most transparent public schema is Solana Tracker's leaderboard API, whose defaults are 90 days, minimum **20 trades**, at least 3 active days, arbitrage wallets excluded, and an optional `maxSingleTokenPct` cap that removes "one-hit wonders" by limiting how much PnL may come from one token ([Solana Tracker leaderboards](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/pnl-v2/leaderboards.md)). Nansen discloses criteria categories (win rate, ROI, realized profit, token diversity, performance through downturns) but no numbers, and uses several windows (30D/90D/180D/all-time) as a form of persistence screening ([Nansen Academy](https://academy.nansen.ai/articles/2132837-smart-money-101)). GMGN's `smart_degen`, `renowned`, `sniper`, `bundler` and `rat_trader` tags are documented only through secondary mirrors, and the statistical test behind "smart" is unpublished ([gmgn-cli README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/gmgn-cli@1.5.2/Readme.md)). No documentation was found for Cielo, Axiom, Photon, BullX or kolscan.io's own ranking logic. The practical reading is that vendor tags are a candidate-discovery feed, not validated skill labels. GodsEye's gate (30-day realized USD PnL, a win-rate bar, 10 closed trades, no single trade above 50% of profit) matches this convention, but its trade floor is half of Solana Tracker's default and it has no token-diversity requirement.

Whether such rankings predict anything forward is the central unknown. The only rigorous persistence evidence is from a different market: a single-author, not peer-reviewed preprint on **Hyperliquid** perpetuals found a rank correlation of **0.52** between adjacent ten-day windows across 147,113 wallets, and live activity of top-ranked wallets improved out-of-sample prediction of one-second returns ([arXiv 2608.04373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04373)). That is a one-second horizon on a perp DEX and says nothing about copying under delay or about memecoins; it is best treated as an upper bound. No study of month-to-month persistence of Solana memecoin wallet PnL, and none testing whether `smart_degen` or Nansen tags predict forward returns, was found. The population context argues for caution. Across Apr 2024 to late 2025 the monthly share of profitable Pump.fun and PumpSwap wallets sat mostly between 30% and 50%, hit **30.1% in June 2025**, then climbed to **73.3% in April 2026**, yet about 65% of April's wallets earned only $1 to $500 and just 5.4% earned over $1,000 ([CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback)). That series is realized-only, nets buys and sells across tokens, does not filter bots or wash trading, and the authors only hypothesise the cause of the 2026 shift. Active wallets fell from 5.2M in May 2025 to 1.8M in December 2025, which is consistent with losers leaving, though that reading is inference. The relevant base rate for a systematic follower is therefore the pre-2026 range, not 73%.

Several PnL pitfalls inflate apparent skill, and vendors document few of them. Nansen and Bitquery use average-cost basis; Solana Tracker treats SOL and stablecoins as quote assets and, in its default "strict" mode, excludes positions where recorded sells exceed recorded buys, which screens out tokens that arrived by transfer or airdrop ([Solana Tracker PnL v2](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/pnl-v2/overview.md); [Nansen PnL guide](https://www.nansen.ai/post/understanding-nansens-pnl-tracking-a-guide-for-crypto-traders)). No vendor was found documenting treatment of fees, priority tips, failed transactions or dead bags. Wash trading is a live contamination risk: a Bitquery case study traced fake volume on one token to three wallets, one of which seeded about 200 bots with 0.5 SOL each to wash-trade over $500K in 12 hours ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/blog/solana-volume-numbers-are-a-lie)), and a cross-chain study of 34,988 tokens found that **82.6% to 82.8% of tokens that gained more than 100%** showed manipulation indicators ([arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01963)). A wallet that bought those "winners" may have been inside the scheme. Inference from these sources: a realized-only ranking rewards wallets that sell winners and leave losers in unsold bags, PnL should be computed in SOL as well as USD with fees and tips in the basis, transferred-in tokens should carry zero or flagged basis, and metrics should be per token position rather than per swap.

On the statistics, the sources supply no wallet-specific method, so the following is inference from standard practice. A 70% win rate over 10 closed trades has a 95% confidence interval of roughly 35% to 93%, so a fixed win-rate bar at that sample size mostly selects luck. Beta-binomial shrinkage toward the population rate, or a lower-confidence-bound score, would rank a 12-trade 100% wallet below an 80-trade 62% wallet. The fund-performance literature suggests why this matters: a false-discovery-rate study of 2,076 US equity funds found about 75% had zero net alpha ([Barras et al. 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i1p179-216.html)), although a later critique argues the method has low power. A practitioner source argues for profit factor and expectancy over win rate, with an example where a 75% win-rate wallet has roughly zero expectancy and a 45% win-rate wallet earns about +62% per trade ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/profit-factor-percentile-rank-kol-wallet-selection-solana)); the article is promotional and shows no data that high-profit-factor wallets keep outperforming. GodsEye holds one advantage no vendor has: its own trade history lets it run the walk-forward test (score in window T, evaluate in T+1) that the Hyperliquid study used.

## Funder grouping is a good start, but funding alone is neither necessary nor sufficient

Sources agree on a signal hierarchy for linking wallets and warn that shared funding can be innocent. Helius and Solscan both expose first-funder lookups as primitives (Solscan batches 50 addresses per call; Helius charges 100 credits per call and returns 404 for wallets that never received SOL) ([Helius funded-by](https://docs.helius.xyz/docs/wallet-api/funded-by.md); [Solscan KB](https://kb.solscan.io/solscan-api-trace-solana-wallet-funders)). Nansen's related-wallet template rates first funder, shared signers and shared CEX deposit addresses as high confidence (above 90%), volume or timing coordination as medium, and behavioural similarity as low, without explaining how the percentages are derived ([Nansen docs](https://docs.nansen.ai/guides/templates/complex-use-cases/use-case-3-identifying-related-wallets-at-scale)). Bubblemaps links two wallets on any historical gas-token transfer between them, and separates a **bundle** (wallets buying in the same block or within seconds at launch, temporal and ephemeral) from a **cluster** (shared funding, token transfers, patterns repeating across multiple tokens over days to months) ([Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/whats-the-difference-between-bundle-cluster-2/)). Mobula's bundler heuristic requires 3 or more wallets with signals such as one atomic transaction, a common funder, same-block buys, near-identical buy amounts and later consolidation, but gives no numeric definitions and calls its thresholds heuristics ([Mobula](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers)). No source reports measured false-positive or precision/recall figures for any single heuristic.

Funder grouping has two documented blind spots. Evasion is cheap: Pine Analytics noted multi-hop funding of 5 to 7 hops, which its one-hop deployer filter missed, so that filter covered only about 1.75% of pump.fun launches ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines); a 2025 vendor-adjacent blog, not peer-reviewed). And the shared funder can be an exchange: in the December 2025 $AVA case Bubblemaps linked 23 wallets funded from Bitget and Binance through narrow time windows, similar SOL amounts and zero prior history ([Blockchain.News summary](https://blockchain.news/flashnews/23-wallets-from-bitget-binance-sniped-ava-on-solana-bubblemaps-on-chain-alert-for-traders); an aggregator, the original post was not read). A rule that discards high-fan-out funders as "exchanges" would miss that ring. Inference: GodsEye's rule that a first funder with 25 or more wallets is an exchange is a sound popularity filter, but it should be supplemented by amount similarity, arrival-time windows and zero prior history for exchange-funded wallets, and bot-platform fee wallets should be down-weighted by inverse degree (no source documents their behaviour in clustering).

The most useful design guidance is to separate exact signals from probabilistic ones and to tier confidence. Elliptic recommends confidence scoring and separating clusters with direct control evidence from behavioural-similarity-only clusters, and a Solana clustering guide advises preferring false negatives over false public accusations ([Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3495/software-development/address-clustering-algorithms.html); [community guide](https://openskillindex.com/skills/neversight-learn-skills-dev-solana-clustering-advanced), a secondary source). The proposed edge set below is inference, with weights that are placeholders because no labelled benchmark exists. Exact-control edges: the same fee payer or co-signer across wallets, one wallet closing another's token accounts, or SOL sweeps to a common destination. Strong edges: direct SOL transfers in either direction, not just a shared parent. Medium edges: a shared non-popular funder walked up to about 3 hops and weighted by 1 over log fan-out, plus co-buys across at least 2 or 3 distinct tokens weighted by token rarity. Weak edges: compute-unit and Jito tip fingerprints, which the sources call fragile because well-behaved clients pick tip accounts at random. Combine edges noisy-OR style, require one non-timing edge before calling two wallets "same operator", and score clusters by average pairwise link rather than the maximum to avoid chaining. MemeTrans used three of these signals (co-signing, same non-CEX funder, shared Jito bundle ID) to merge accounts, which is the nearest published recipe ([MemeTrans, arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)). Because GodsEye's site and API are public, read-only and redacted, cluster evidence rows should stay internal; "possible" links should never drive public labels.

Insider detection is similarly heuristic, and the sources support keeping insider-pattern wallets in the copy set as you have chosen while changing how they are measured. Mobula defines snipers as buyers within roughly 0 to 3 blocks of creation, often fresh wallets with high priority fees, and insiders as wallets with direct transactions with the deployer or dev funding. Pine's deployer-funded snipers exited fast: over 55% fully exited within a minute, about 85% within five, and 87% of their snipes were profitable (per snipe, on a self-defined subset, so selection-biased) ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)). The consequence is not that such wallets are worthless; it is that their leader-side PnL is not their follower-side PnL. Edge-type and followability are separate axes: GodsEye's tiers mix timing (insider), statistics (lucky, proven) and automation (bot), and splitting them would let a wallet be both insider-pattern and statistically proven. KOL labels should add a conflict-of-interest risk flag rather than quality credit, since a petition alleges KOLs game the Kolscan leaderboard (an unverified advocacy claim) ([Change.org](https://www.change.org/p/ban-kolscan-io)) and ZachXBT alleged in February 2026 that an Axiom employee used internal wallet-linking tools to front-run KOLs (allegations, not adjudicated) ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/zachxbt-investigation-accuses-axiom-employee-141228617.html)). Rotation tracking, following a leader to a fresh wallet, has no documented practice or false-positive data in any source. The inference-only design is to watch a tracked wallet's non-swap transactions for a large SOL outflow to a never-seen counterparty, add that counterparty to the stream on probation with a discounted trust score, and promote it only if its first trades overlap the leader's token set or it sweeps to or from the leader.

## At 200 ms slots, the stream is fast enough and execution is the bottleneck

GodsEye already runs a Yellowstone-style gRPC stream, and the infrastructure research mostly confirms the design rather than recommending replacement. Mainnet slot time fell to **200 ms on October 9, 2026** ([Solana](https://solana.com/upgrades/reduced-slot-times)), and the Alpenglow consensus switch was scheduled for October 21, after which `confirmed` and `finalized` become the same state with a finality target of about 150 ms described as an expectation rather than a constant ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/anza-sets-alpenglow-mainnet-date-solanas-consensus-switch-is-scheduled-for-october-21-at-about-1450-utc)). No post-activation confirmation or measured latency was found, so verify activation and re-measure before relying on it. The same guidance says Agave 4.3 tags Geyser events with a `bank_id` because a slot can carry more than one candidate block, so consumers should buffer by slot and `bank_id` and dedupe on signature or blockhash rather than slot alone.

Provider choice should follow price, completeness and failover rather than millisecond leads. Within one provider, a vendor benchmark on PumpSwap showed p99 delivery only 6.8 to 23.6 ms behind the fastest stream ([Alchemy](https://www.alchemy.com/blog/solana-grpc-benchmark-methodology)). Shred-derived feeds were reported to lead gRPC by single-digit milliseconds at the median in same-region vendor tests ([Shyft](https://docs.shyft.to/solana-shredstreaming/rabbitstream-vs-jito-shredstream-benchmarks)), against claims of 120 ms average advantage over default nodes from another vendor ([RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/shredstream-vs-geyser-vs-standard-rpc)); the 20-fold disagreement likely reflects different baselines. Jito ShredStream carried a shutdown notice for September 5, 2026, and whether it actually shut down was not confirmed. Shred feeds also carry no logs or balances, so a balance-delta parser cannot run on them. The cost model matters more than latency: Triton charges **$0.08/GB** for streaming, Helius' mainnet gRPC starts at the $499 Business tier, and Shyft is flat per connection ([Triton](https://triton.one/pricing); [Helius plans](https://www.helius.dev/docs/billing/plans.md); [Shyft](https://shyft.to/solana-rpc-grpc-pricing)). A narrow `account_include` filter on tracked wallets is cheap on metered plans, while a program-wide firehose can reach terabytes monthly. Triton recommends a compressed cuckoo-filter account list once tracking roughly 100 or more accounts, with about 1% false positives to re-verify locally, and Helius says the filter handles hundreds of thousands of accounts; each new subscribe request replaces the previous one, so keep a local copy of the full config. For gap recovery, `from_slot` replay works within a retained window (about 48 hours on LaserStream), with duplicates at slot boundaries ([Triton docs](https://docs.triton.one/rpc-pool/grpc-subscriptions); [Helius LaserStream](https://www.helius.dev/docs/laserstream/grpc.md)). Running two providers and taking the first-arriving, deduped signature is an inference, not a sourced practice.

Parsing is where silent errors live. Pump.fun's documentation says its create and trade instructions moved to v2/v3 layouts and that parsers must handle both legacy and new formats; events gained holder-reward fields, and `virtual_quote_reserves` is a signed value that can be negative from September 30 ([pump-public-docs](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs)). Parser-library release notes describe PumpSwap decoders that had to handle both log and inner-instruction paths and had quote-mint handling bugs ([sol-parser-sdk](https://docs.rs/crate/sol-parser-sdk/0.6.1)). The research's inference, unverified against Yellowstone's primary docs, is to resolve account keys as static keys plus loaded writable plus loaded readonly addresses before indexing token balances, compute deltas per owner and mint, add back fees, tips and ATA rent, and ignore failed transactions. A hybrid design is the robust one: balance deltas for economic truth, instruction or event decoding only to identify the venue and pull creator and reserve context. The end-to-end budget from a leader's `processed` event to a landed copy is an inference of roughly 400 ms to 1.5 s, dominated by quoting, landing path and leader rotation; the largest controllable losses are aggregator quote round-trips (local pool math for Pump.fun and PumpSwap removes one), acting on a later commitment than needed, single-endpoint submission and cold connections. Jito's own figure for the Block Engine hop is about 50 ms ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-announces-bam-direct-bundle-ingest-routing-solana-bundles-past-the-block-engines-50ms-hop)). A copy can never share an atomic bundle with the leader's swap, so it lands later. No source measures real leader-to-copy fill gaps on Solana.

## Copying is a thin-edge trade where delay flips the sign

The evidence for copy-trade profitability is the weakest and most consequential part of this research. No independent, peer-reviewed study measures out-of-sample profit for publicly tracked Solana wallets or for commercial copy-bots (Trojan, Maestro, BonkBot, GMGN, Axiom, Photon, Cielo); searches returned only volume figures and security incidents. The two usable quantitative anchors both have limits. The WWW'26 paper, trained on 6,000 pump.fun coins with chronological splits, found smart wallets earned 14% per coin against an estimated 3% for copiers, but its appendix models only bonding-curve slippage, zero delay and equal size, and the notes could not confirm whether the 3% nets Pump.fun's 1% fee, so it is an optimistic bound ([arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641)). The TokenPost report of a late-August 2026 experiment is richer but the researcher is unnamed, exit rules and costs for the two-minute test are not stated, and its sub-tests are not comparable: entering two minutes after large clusters (450 to 2,000 wallets) lost about **41% per token** (median outcome a 95% decline), following clusters with positive records lost about 11%, a single-wallet backtest lost about 20% per trade, and a neural net that raised the doubling share from about 10% to 26% still lost about 11% per pick. Only 16 tightly connected clusters gained about 10% per token, a sample too small to lean on ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/investing/35409)).

The environment that makes decay plausible is well documented. Rug-class outcomes are the norm: 84.13% of 41,470 migrated pump.fun tokens were labelled high-risk by MemeTrans' own price/manipulation definition, with bundled accounts holding 36.5% of supply ([arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)), and a study of 100,063 new Solana pools flagged about 76% as rug pulls with median rug lifecycles under an hour ([Chen et al., arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24625)). Definitions differ (a price-drop threshold, a behaviour pipeline, and an undisclosed method for Solidus' roughly 99% figure), so the 76% to 99% range is a definitional spread, not a confidence interval. Over half of pump.fun tokens were sniped in the creation block in Pine's early-2025 sample, which is older data. Visible wallets also leak: the Axiom allegation shows that any single tracking provider can be an alpha-decay vector. Whether public listing on Kolscan or GMGN causes measurable decay has not been studied; no pre/post-listing event study exists. The honest position is that decay is likely and unquantified, and that the 1-to-10-second horizon is probably where the exploitable information lives (a Hyperliquid analogy, not memecoin evidence).

For GodsEye the implication is a measurement gap more than a strategy verdict. If the learning loop scores outcomes at 1-hour, 6-hour and 24-hour horizons, it largely measures token drift, not copier execution quality, because the first minutes dominate. That is inference from the sources; GodsEye's internals were not researched. The design the notes propose, which is inference with no published reference implementation, is to replay each leader buy at delays of 1, 2, 5, 15, 30, 60 and 120 seconds using pool state at the delayed time, price the fill with fees and tip, replay the leader's exit with the same delay, and report per leader the copier-minus-leader return, entry drift versus the leader's fill, expectancy, PnL share from the top one and three trades, and time to first sell versus your lag. Pool-state history at that resolution has to come from your own stream. For insider-pattern wallets, which you are keeping, this replay is what tells you the right size and exit rule for each rather than whether to copy; the sources do not support blocking them, only discounting their leader-side stats. Defenses with published numbers are few: the MemeTrans token-risk model cut average loss for its top-100 selection from 60.71% to 26.64% in a simulation that buys at migration ([arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)), and the WWW'26 ablations showed wallet-history and coin-context features contributing most.

## Consensus signals lack public validation, and clustering is the missing ingredient

No independent backtest of an "N smart wallets bought within T" signal on Solana exists in the public record, so hit rates and decay for GodsEye's 3-wallets-in-1-hour rule are unknown outside its own data. MadeOnSol exposes a "KOL consensus" endpoint with net flow, exit rate (share of buyers who also sold) and median entry market cap, with an example filter of at least 3 buyers, positive net flow and exit rate below 0.3, and claims a 3.7x lift (about 50% versus 14%) in follow-on KOL buying within four hours from a 38-day backtest whose method was not shown ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-kol-consensus-scout-leaderboard-token-discovery)). That outcome is "more KOLs buy", not price return, the vendor calls consensus backward-looking, and its thresholds are unvalidated; treat these as gate ideas, not benchmarks. The nearest adjacent evidence is mixed. Clustered insider purchases in equities are followed by abnormal returns above 2% in the following month ([Alldredge and Blank 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfnres/v42y2019i2p331-360.html), abstract-level), which supports independence across coordinated actors, while crypto influencer tweets show a +1.83% one-day pop followed by cumulative returns of -2.24% at 10 days and -6.53% at 30 days, on a sample ending December 2022 ([Merkley et al. 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v29y2024i3d10.1007_s11142-024-09838-4.html); horizon figures via a [TechFlow summary](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/17848)). Attention-driven signals look like confirmation near the top.

The sharpest inference for GodsEye is that five wallets from one operator should count as one vote. The consensus rule counts wallets, but the equity cluster literature and MemeTrans' entity merging point to counting independent clusters. Beyond that, the testable variants are: net-flow and exit-rate gates; recency of each wallet's buy relative to the first (late joiners confirm, they do not lead); buy size relative to the wallet's own median; and a token-structure veto using top-10 concentration, bundled supply and dev pre-buy, plus mint authority, freeze authority and Token-2022 extensions, which scanners treat as honeypot-class flags ([InsightX docs](https://docs.insightx.network/reference/scanner-solana-indicators), vendor). Bubblemaps' bands (bundled supply above 15% serious) are described by the source as "not hard rules" ([Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/)). Because many insider-pattern wallets buy bundled or deployer-adjacent tokens, any such veto should start in shadow mode and, for insider-tier copies, adjust size and exit behaviour instead of vetoing. Evaluate every variant against a matched control (tokens of similar age, market cap and volume without consensus), not against all tokens, since fewer than 2% of Pump.fun tokens graduate and "pumps at least once" has a high base rate ([Mancino et al., arXiv 2512.11850](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11850v2)). Add 5-, 15- and 30-minute checkpoints, log each emitted signal as seen at the time, and record every (N, T, band, weight) variant tried so multiple testing can be corrected for, using the Deflated Sharpe Ratio of [Bailey and Lopez de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551).

On ML, the credible published work is token-level, and it is modest. "Catching the Rug" trained on first-5-minute features from about 6.4M Solana tokens with a rolling forward split and got in-domain MCC of only about 0.25 to 0.36, with cross-venue transfer collapsing to near zero; the authors say it is not sufficient for real-world deployment, and trees were more robust than neural models ([arXiv 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20271)). The research's own arithmetic suggests its pump.fun test set was about 82% rug-labelled, so a predict-all-rug rule would score F1 near 0.90, a caution that only MCC is informative (the metrics may be computed differently than described). MemeTrans reached AUPRC of 0.58 and found market-activity features most important; its code is public ([git-disl/MemeTrans](https://github.com/git-disl/MemeTrans)). SolRPDS' AUC of 0.987 is circular because its label derives from the same liquidity-activity features ([arXiv 2504.07132](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07132)). Time-ordered splits are the standard to copy. No Solana-specific work was found on wallet-labelling graph networks, learning-to-rank, bandit allocation to copy targets, or regime detection. Two designs are therefore pure inference: Thompson sampling with exponential forgetting on a Beta posterior per leader for "copied trade beat a matched baseline net of fees", which fits the existing 0.5x to 1.5x weight loop, and using the profitable-wallet-share series or graduation rate to scale position size rather than to choose wallets. Off-chain signals (Kaito, Cookie, LunarCrush, Dexscreener trending) have no independent predictive test and are gameable; a cheap experiment is logging first-mention time against first smart-wallet buy.

Open-source resources are strongest at the plumbing layer. Useful, maintained projects include the Yellowstone server (1,015 stars, AGPL-3.0, pushed Oct 26, 2026), the Carbon indexing framework (MIT), and the solana-streamer SDK (MIT), with a TypeScript DEX parser whose last push was in October 2025 and may lag Pump.fun's new layouts ([yellowstone-grpc](https://github.com/rpcpool/yellowstone-grpc); [carbon](https://github.com/sevenlabs-hq/carbon); [solana-streamer](https://github.com/0xfnzero/solana-streamer)). Wallet-ranking repos are tiny and unvalidated, and no open-source Solana wallet-clustering library was found. Treat copy-bot repos as untrusted: SlowMist traced a July 2025 theft to a fake pump.fun bot repository with inflated stars, and later cases hid key exfiltration in config files ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/solana-trading-bot-github-malware-scam)). Borrow ideas, not code, and never expose the trading key to code you have not read.

## Prioritized improvements for GodsEye

The ordering below weighs how directly each item addresses the central finding (copy edge decays within seconds and is untested), how strong the evidence is, and the effort. "Sourced" means a cited finding supports the problem; "inference" means the specific design is reasoning from those findings. None of these blocks insider-pattern wallets; items 1, 4 and 5 are about sizing, exits, weighting and measurement for them.

| # | Improvement | Why | Evidence strength | Effort |

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. "Track wallets better" splits into finding wallets, which the industry already does adequately and unverifiably, and converting a visible trade into a follower's profit, which almost nobody measures. The strongest numbers in the corpus, a 14%-to-3% gap with zero delay and a reported sign flip at two minutes, are not proof that copying fails; they are the reason to build the replay harness that tells you, wallet by wallet, which leaders retain any edge at GodsEye's real latency. Insider-pattern wallets fit that framework without being excluded: their value to a follower is an empirical question about sizing and exits, answerable from the same replay.

Two further insights carry forward. The distance between "which wallets" and "which trades are copyable" is also the distance between a leaderboard and an auditable ledger; keeping a point-in-time ledger of emitted signals, forward-evaluated against matched controls, is what turns the 3-wallet rule and the learning loop from tuned heuristics into tested ones. And the open questions that decide the strategy are all answerable with data GodsEye already collects or can collect cheaply: decay by delay, persistence of its own tiers, and precision of its cluster links. Until those are run, the correct posture toward every vendor tag, consensus threshold and latency claim in this literature is to treat it as a hypothesis.
