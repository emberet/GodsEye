# Read pools from chain, not vendors

**Bottom line.** GodsEye understands swaps, not pools. It reads one wallet's balance changes, labels nine program ids, and never fetches a pool, vault, LP mint, position or program account; every depth, price-impact and LP-lock number comes from Jupiter, GeckoTerminal or RugCheck, or is back-solved from its own thin tape of tracked wallets' trades ([audit](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/research_notes/Pool%20and%20contract%20intelligence/godseye_current_understanding.md), [stream.ts](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/stream/stream.ts)). It should know, per token, which pools exist, how much each can pay out for a 10% and a 30% price drop, who can withdraw that liquidity, and which mint and program rules change what a trade really delivers. Seven gaps change numbers or decisions. First, the venue list is blind to Meteora DAMM v2, the Dynamic Bonding Curve (DBC) and Raydium LaunchLab: **255,623 of 2,856,656 stored swaps (8.95%) have no venue, 39 of 41 sampled null-venue transactions called DAMM v2, one such mint (`michi`) showed 21 wallets each making about 180 swaps for 7,512 SOL gross and roughly zero net, and 63 of the 68 wallets with mostly null-venue swaps are in the `insider` tier**. Second, Token-2022 is the majority program (**83% to 97% of sampled mints**) yet transfer fees (**11 of 300 swap-weighted mints, 6 of 300 random ones, 100 to 300 bps**) are ignored by the rug screen, the parser and the replay model. Third, the token-account rent constant is stale (2,039,280 lamports against a live 1,488,440 to 1,513,840), mispricing every first buy and full exit by about 0.0005 SOL. Fourth, non-Jito tips are priced into swaps (12 of 12 sampled mixed-quote swaps). Fifth, tokenized stocks count as memecoins (**23 mints, 18,844 swaps, 701 wallets, 46,391 SOL**). Sixth, the 90% LP-lock rule misfires on concentrated-liquidity pools (RugCheck shows about 0% locked on every Orca and DLMM market of PUMP, which holds $29.6M). Seventh, there is no on-chain pool state anywhere. The build order that follows from the evidence is: persist mint facts and fix the parser first (cheap, measured), then add a program-and-discriminator decoder with an unknown-program canary, then read pool state for the four venues where memecoins live and derive one exit-capacity number, then replace the single LP rule with per-venue checks. Insider-pattern wallets stay copyable throughout; every fix below is token-level or swap-level, not a wallet verdict, and the public site stays read-only and redacted. This report is research and design; it makes no claim that any change exists, because the owner's assistant is implementing parts separately.

## How pools work on Solana, for a bot: one formula, twelve venues

Evidence in this report carries four grades. Measurements are the researchers' own: queries on the local database (`data/copycow.db`, 2026-10-16 to 2026-10-26) and small samples from public RPC, Jupiter and GeckoTerminal on 2026-10-27, each a point in time. Source facts come from program source, IDLs and official SDKs read in this research. Vendor claims come from a protocol's own documentation (Raydium, Orca, Meteora, pump.fun, Jupiter, Helius) and are labelled as such. Computed items are the researchers' arithmetic: **every byte offset below was summed from IDL or struct field sizes and was not checked against a live account**, and so were the depth numbers. Treat computed offsets as hypotheses with a one-account test (see the last section).

Almost every readable venue reduces to constant product on some virtual reserve pair per active price range, which means one closed form prices exit depth everywhere and each venue differs only in where its reserves hide and who can pull them. Pump.fun's curve is "based on Uniswap V2" on synthetic reserves ([pump README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/main/docs/PUMP_PROGRAM_README.md)); Raydium CPMM computes `out = floor(in * out_vault / (in_vault + in))` after fees ([cp-swap source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raydium-io/raydium-cp-swap/master/programs/cp-swap/src/curve/constant_product.rs)); Orca, Raydium CLMM, DAMM v2 and DBC use Q64.64 square-root prices and liquidity `L`, where a quote amount is `L*(sqrt(P_hi) - sqrt(P_lo))` ([DBC curve source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MeteoraAg/dynamic-bonding-curve/main/programs/dynamic-bonding-curve/src/curve.rs), [DAMM v2 formulas](https://docs.meteora.ag/core-products/damm-v2/formulas.md)). Meteora DLMM is the exception: bins hold fixed prices and exchange at constant sum, so depth is a sum of token amounts rather than a curve ([DLMM formulas](https://docs.meteora.ag/core-products/dlmm/formulas.md)). The proprietary AMMs are a different category and cannot be read from accounts at all.

The proprietary venues matter less for memecoins than headlines suggest. The disagreement itself shows how volatile prop-AMM routing is. Market-share figures in secondary reports conflict by date and were not confirmed. The usable rule is to size against a Jupiter quote ladder and treat the answer as non-binding, because the quote depends on the operator's current curve.

### One exit-capacity number

A bot needs a single comparable figure: the net SOL received when selling just enough tokens to move the pool's marginal price down by `X`. For constant product with base reserve `B`, quote reserve `Q` (the effective or virtual quote where a venue has one) and a sale of `T` base tokens, the researchers derived `T = B*(1/sqrt(1-X) - 1)` and `out = Q*(1 - sqrt(1-X))` before fees; at that size the end-spot move and the average execution shortfall are the same number. Inside a concentrated-liquidity range `Q_virtual = L*sqrt(P)` and `B_virtual = L/sqrt(P)`; beyond the range edge, walk to the next range with its own `L`; for DLMM, consume bins and apply the variable fee. Then cap proceeds at the real extractable quote, which is the step that stops a virtual reserve from overstating depth. The algorithm is a bisection on the venue's own simulated sell. These are the researchers' derivations, checked by them against a brute-force constant-product simulation, not a published standard.

| Spot-price decline X | Tokens sold as share of base reserve (T/B) | Quote out as share of Q, pre-fee (average shortfall) |
|---|---|---|
| 5% | 0.026 | 2.53% |
| 10% | 0.054 | 5.13% |
| 20% | 0.118 | 10.56% |
| 30% | 0.195 | 16.33% |
| 50% | 0.414 | 29.29% |
| 75% | 1.000 | 50.00% |

Table: researchers' arithmetic. A pump curve with 60 real SOL (virtual 90 SOL, 357.67M virtual tokens) yields, by the same arithmetic, 19.35M tokens for 4.62 SOL at -10%, 69.8M tokens for 14.70 SOL at -30% and 148.2M tokens for 26.36 SOL at -50%, all pre-fee. At the end of the original curve the PumpSwap pool opens with about 206.9M base tokens and 84.99 SOL, at a price within 0.03% of the curve's last price (4.1088e-7 against 4.1078e-7 SOL per token, computed). For DLMM a move of `-X` spans `ln(1/(1-X))/ln(1+bin_step/10000)` bins: 10.6 bins for -10% and 35.8 for -30% at bin step 100, 52.7 and 178.5 at step 20, and 210.8 and 713.5 at step 5 (computed). Fee drag is separate and substantial for fresh launches: at the 2025 tier of 1.25% a flat-price round trip costs about 2.5%, against about 0.6% at the 0.30% top tier (inference from the fee tables above).

The bot features that follow are `exit10` and `exit30` (depth), `ownImpact` (the cost of exiting the actual position), `depthRatio = exit10 / position value`, and a stress figure that sells the combined insider, bundle and top-holder balance into the pool. Aggregate across a token's pools only with each pool capped at its own liquidity, because routers split sells: a live Jupiter quote showed a 99%/1% split across two venues. A depth that its owner can withdraw is not depth, so the LP-control column above is a separate gate, and the control that matters differs by venue. Pump curves and canonical PumpSwap pools have none; Raydium v4 and CPMM LP can be burned or locked; Orca and CLMM liquidity is locked only by freezing or escrowing a position NFT; DLMM supports only a time lock; DAMM v2 has a pool-level permanent-lock counter. The broader rug context is that in the largest independent Solana measurement 79.0% of flagged rugs were pump-and-dump cliffs, 20.4% liquidity withdrawals and 0.6% freeze abuse, on Orca, Raydium and Meteora pools only ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24625)); LP control therefore guards a minority tail, while depth and the stress figure guard the common case.

## Token-2022 dominates the tape, and its extensions decide what a trade delivers

A mint is an 82-byte base struct (optional mint authority, supply, decimals, initialised flag, optional freeze authority); a Token-2022 mint adds an account-type byte at index 165 and a TLV extension list after it ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/basics/create-mint), [token-2022 interface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/solana-program/token-2022/main/interface/src/extension/mod.rs)). One `getAccountInfo(mint, jsonParsed)` returns the owning token program, both authorities, supply, decimals and the decoded extension list, and in the audit's samples public RPC decoded `transferFeeConfig`, `permanentDelegate`, `pausableConfig`, `defaultAccountState` and `scaledUiAmountConfig` without hand parsing ([audit](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/research_notes/Pool%20and%20contract%20intelligence/godseye_current_understanding.md)). That sampling also settles how much this matters in GodsEye's data: **250 of 300 swap-weighted mints (83%), 84 of the top 100 by recent swap count, and 290 of 300 random `token_info` mints (97%) are Token-2022**. In the swap-weighted sample the extensions were metadataPointer plus tokenMetadata 250, transferHook 24, confidentialTransferMint 23, permanentDelegate 23, defaultAccountState 23, pausableConfig 22, scaledUiAmountConfig 22, transferFeeConfig 11 and mintCloseAuthority 1. GodsEye's own code reads the extension list but acts on only four of them: it blocks `permanentDelegate`, `pausableConfig` with an authority and default-frozen accounts, and warns on a hook with a program id ([rugcheck.ts](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/market/rugcheck.ts)).

| Extension | What it does to a trade | Venue support (vendor docs, Raydium source) | GodsEye today; suggested treatment (design) |
|---|---|---|---|
| TransferFeeConfig | Fee `min(amount*bps/10,000, maximum_fee)` is withheld from the amount received; new rates take effect two epochs after being set | Raydium CPMM and CLMM allow it; Orca allows it without a badge | Ignored; read both fee structs, pick by epoch, price the fee, warn above a threshold |
| TransferHook | Token-2022 calls the hook program on every transfer; a hook error fails the trade; the hook program can be changed by the hook authority | Raydium rejects at pool creation; Orca needs a TokenBadge; Jupiter routing reported poor by one community source only | Warns when a program id is set; read the hook program's upgrade authority, since a live authority can turn a benign token into a trap |
| PermanentDelegate | Delegate can move or burn from any holder account and from pool vaults, and holders cannot revoke it | Raydium rejects; Orca needs a badge | Blocks; keep, and also use it as the tokenized-equity signal |
| Pausable | While paused, transfers, mints and burns fail | Raydium rejects as unreviewed; Orca unsupported | Blocks when an authority is set; keep |
| DefaultAccountState | New accounts can start frozen, breaking vault and escrow creation | Raydium rejects Frozen; Orca allows only Initialized, with a badge | Blocks Frozen; keep |
| NonTransferable | Tokens cannot move; owner can still burn and close | Raydium rejects, Orca unsupported | Unread; hard block |
| MintCloseAuthority | Mint can be closed at zero supply and re-initialised at the same address with new rules | Raydium rejects; Orca needs a badge | Unread; block or flag |
| ConfidentialTransfer | Amounts are encrypted and will not appear in plain `uiTokenAmount` deltas | Raydium rejects; Orca allows non-confidential transfers | Unread; flag, since the delta parser would miss movements |

Raydium's allow-list is source-verified: `is_supported_mint` accepts legacy mints, mints with an initialised support PDA, or mints whose every extension is TransferFeeConfig, MetadataPointer, TokenMetadata, InterestBearingConfig or ScaledUiAmount ([cp-swap token.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raydium-io/raydium-cp-swap/master/programs/cp-swap/src/utils/token.rs)). Orca's table is vendor documentation and was not checked against its source. Raydium's own docs say the check happens only at pool creation, so a pool existing is a weak safety signal once an authority can swap a hook program or rotate a delegate.

### Transfer fees change the arithmetic in both directions

A transfer-fee mint hands the recipient the amount minus the fee, so the wallet's balance delta is net of the fee on a buy and the pool receives less than the wallet sent on a sell. Raydium CPMM's math is `vault_received = amount_in - fee_in`, then the pool fee on that, then `user_receives = amount_out_gross - fee_out`, and its slippage check compares against the gross figure the pool sends; Raydium's documentation puts a 1% fee on both sides of a 0.25% pool at about 2.25% per swap ([Raydium docs](https://docs.raydium.io/algorithms/token-2022-transfer-fees), vendor, and [Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/transfer-fees)). Orca defines exact-in `amount` as fee-included and `otherAmountThreshold` as fee-excluded, so slippage lands on real account deltas ([Orca](https://docs.orca.so/developers/architecture/token-extensions)). For GodsEye the observed fees were **100 bps and 300 bps, with `maximumFee` of 10^15 base units, which makes the cap irrelevant**, so a copy round trip costs about 2% at 100 bps and up to roughly 6% at 300 bps (the audit's inference, not a measured fill). The same few config authorities recur across the printed mints, which suggests one operator or factory; that reading is the researchers' inference. The newer fee takes effect two epochs after `SetTransferFee`, and the sources disagree on labels: Raydium's docs describe older and newer in the reverse sense to the Solana docs, so the rule is to read both structs and choose by their `epoch` field, using `newerTransferFee` once its epoch is at or before the current epoch. The program-level events carry the same truth: Raydium CPMM's `SwapEvent` has `input_transfer_fee` and `output_transfer_fee`, CLMM's has `transfer_fee_0/1`, Whirlpool's `Traded` has input and output transfer fees, and DAMM v2 reports included and excluded transfer-fee amounts, so an event decoder can reconcile fees that a delta parser can only absorb. Whether Jupiter's quote nets the fee is unverified: Jupiter said in 2023 it supported transfer fees across its stack ([archived forum post](https://discuss.jup.ag/t/archived-jupiter-token-2022-support/21711), vendor), while one open issue reports Manifest legs overstating `outAmount` by the fee ([issue 65](https://github.com/jup-ag/jupiter-swap-api-client/issues/65), single source).

### Authorities, metadata and launchpad quirks

Authority checks need to cover more than the mint and freeze keys. A Token-2022 mint can carry a transfer-fee config authority, a withheld-withdraw authority, a permanent delegate, a pausable authority, a hook authority, a rate authority, a close authority and a metadata update authority, and each is a lever someone holds after the buy. Neodyme's practical rule is an allow-list of extensions plus an authority check on every accepted mint ([Neodyme](https://neodyme.io/en/blog/token-2022/)). In the audit sample 43 of 300 swap-weighted mints had a mint authority, about half of them the tokenized equities, while none of 300 random `token_info` mints had either authority. Sources disagree on pump.fun: Helius says launchpads null the mint authority, whereas the `create_v2` documentation says it is initialised to a Pump PDA and does not say it is later revoked ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/docs/orb/explore-authorities), [create_v2 doc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/main/docs/instructions/COIN_CREATION.md)); no live `create_v2` mint was read. GodsEye's rug-check header promises a mutable-metadata warning but nothing reads `tokenMetadata.updateAuthority`. Mutable metadata is a reputational lever (rebrand or swap the URI), not a balance lever. Mayhem Mode coins, per Chainstack, mint 2 billion tokens with the extra held by an agent and burned if unsold after 24 hours, so a supply drop is not a creator burn and holder percentages need the curve account and the agent vault excluded ([Chainstack](https://chainstack.com/trading-bot-update-full-mayhem-mode-support-for-pump-fun/), a vendor-adjacent source; the vault location is inferred from an account name). GodsEye's holder check also divides scaled `uiAmount` by unscaled supply on `scaledUiAmountConfig` mints, which misstates percentages for the equity tokens ([holders.ts](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/market/holders.ts)).

### Programs: upgrade authority and how to verify a program id

Under the current default loader (BPF Loader Upgradeable, `BPFLoaderUpgradeab1e11111111111111111111111`) a program id is a small account pointing to a ProgramData account that stores the bytecode, the deploy slot and the upgrade authority; `solana program set-upgrade-authority --final` removes it, and an immutable program can never be updated or closed ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/programs/deploying)). An upgrade takes effect in the slot after deployment. Neodyme's review found most programs run on a single hot-wallet authority, which means a trading program with a live key can be replaced and resting liquidity taken regardless of audits ([Neodyme](https://neodyme.io/blog/solana_upgrade_authority/)). For a short-horizon copy trade this is a tail risk and does not change per-trade math, but a launchpad whose curve program is upgradeable can retroactively change fees, curve and migration rules, which pump.fun has done several times, and a hook program with a live authority is more dangerous than a DEX program. Whole-repo search shows GodsEye reads no upgrade authority anywhere, and the researchers did not query the pump, Raydium or Orca authorities, so their state is unknown.

Verifying that a program id is the DEX it claims to be is a ladder with no single authority. The id should appear in a pinned allow-list copied from the DEX's own docs or repo; Jupiter's label map should name it the same way (this check passed for eight of GodsEye's nine ids, with Jupiter v6 absent because it is an aggregator); OtterSec's verified-build status should show a rebuild from the DEX's public repository (`https://verify.osec.io/status/<program-id>`), noting that verification only means the source matches the binary, not that the code is safe, and that an upgrade un-verifies a program until the API re-verifies it within 24 hours ([Solana verified builds](https://solana.com/docs/programs/verified-builds)); and the upgrade authority should be a known multisig or none. Loader v4 status and the exact `jsonParsed` shape of ProgramData were not established.

### Accounts, rent, lookup tables and inner instructions

An associated token account (ATA) is derived from the wallet, the token program id and the mint, so a Token-2022 mint needs the Token-2022 program id in the seed; GodsEye's executor already reads the mint's owning program for this ([verify.ts](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/copy/verify.ts)). Rent is where the notes conflict. The Solana docs figure for a 165-byte account is 2,039,280 lamports, and 2,074,080 for a 170-byte Token-2022 account with ImmutableOwner ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/basics/create-token-account)), which is the constant GodsEye uses; live `getMinimumBalanceForRentExemption` on 2026-10-27 returned 1,488,440 for 165 bytes, 1,513,840 for 170 and 1,574,800 for 182, and a real PumpSwap transaction created a 170-byte Token-2022 account with 1,513,840 lamports ([audit, Solana RPC samples](https://api.mainnet-beta.solana.com)). The live measurement wins; why the docs page is out of date was not established, and the robust fix is to read the lamports from the `createAccount` instruction or the account's balance rather than hold any constant. Wrapped SOL is lamports inside a token account whose `amount` is stale until `SyncNative`; closing it returns all lamports including rent; native SOL pump curve legs appear only in lamport balances, so wSOL token deltas alone miss them.

To know which programs a transaction really called, build the full key list as static keys, then ALT-loaded writable keys, then ALT-loaded readonly keys ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/core/transactions/versioned-transactions)), set `maxSupportedTransactionVersion` or v0 transactions error, and take each instruction's `programIdIndex` into that list. Fee payer and signers are always static, and Jito tip accounts should not be loaded through lookup tables, so tips are visible in static keys. Version-1 transactions drop lookup tables entirely, so a decoder that assumes ALTs mishandles them. Aggregators and bots call DEX programs by CPI, so the real venue exists only in `meta.innerInstructions`, grouped by the parent instruction `index` and ordered by `stackHeight`; a Jupiter swap shows one top-level Jupiter instruction and the AMM as an inner instruction, and the AMM's own event self-CPI one level deeper. A Jito tip is a system transfer to one of eight tip accounts with a 1,000-lamport minimum ([Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)), but tips to other relays and bots exist, which is why a recognised-list approach under-counts (see the errors section). Classifying an "owner" as wallet or program-controlled needs the account's own owner and curve test; pools, curve accounts, escrows and the incinerator are not holders, and a smart-wallet PDA should be labelled "program-controlled", not "insider".

## Decode by program id plus discriminator, and let balance deltas keep the books

Anchor instruction data starts with the first 8 bytes of `sha256("global:<snake_name>")`, accounts with `sha256("account:<Name>")`, events with `sha256("event:<Name>")` ([Anchor IDL docs](https://www.anchor-lang.com/docs/basics/idl)). Events travel two ways. `emit!` writes a base64 "Program data:" log line that data providers may truncate; `emit_cpi!` makes the program call itself with instruction data `e445a52e51cb9a1d` followed by the 8-byte event discriminator and the Borsh payload, visible only in inner instructions and less likely to be truncated ([Anchor events docs](https://www.anchor-lang.com/docs/features/events)). By counting `event_authority` accounts in each IDL, the researchers found pump.fun, PumpSwap, Meteora DLMM, DAMM v2, DBC and the Jupiter `SwapEvent` use CPI events, while Raydium CPMM, Raydium CLMM and Orca Whirlpool use log events (confirmed in source), and DAMM v1 most likely does but this is not verified because its source is not public. Raydium AMM v4 is not Anchor at all: instructions are a one-byte tag (1 Initialize2, 3 Deposit, 4 Withdraw, 9 SwapBaseIn, 11 SwapBaseOut, 16 and 17 the V2 swaps), and logs are bincode structs, base64-encoded, with `LogType` 0 to 4 for init, deposit, withdraw and the two swaps ([raydium-amm source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raydium-io/raydium-amm/master/program/src/log.rs); the `ray_log:` text prefix itself was not read). Practically, CPI-event programs need inner instructions in the stream and survive log truncation, and log-event programs break if a provider truncates logs.

### The collision problem

The same instruction or event name yields the same discriminator in every program that uses it, so a global `discriminator -> name` map is wrong. **`swap` (`f8c69e91e17587c8`) is identical in six programs (Raydium CLMM, Whirlpool, DLMM, DAMM v1, DAMM v2, DBC); PumpSwap `buy` equals pump `buy` (`66063d1201daebea`), so the program id is the only separator; `SwapEvent` (`40c6cde8260871e2`) is shared by Raydium CPMM, CLMM and Jupiter; the `Pool` account discriminator is shared by PumpSwap, DAMM v1 and DAMM v2; `PoolState` is shared by CPMM, CLMM and LaunchLab; and DBC and DAMM v2 share `EvtSwap2` and `EvtInitializePool`.** The rule is to key every decoder on `programId:discriminatorHex`, take the program id from the instruction's own `programIdIndex` (the inner instruction's, for CPI), and for accounts check `account.owner` before the discriminator. Whenever a CPI event matches, also confirm the instruction's accounts include the program's `__event_authority` PDA, which prevents false matches on look-alike data.

### Deltas for amounts, decoding for attribution

Balance deltas are the only venue-agnostic ground truth for who received what, which is why GodsEye's parser works across routers. They cannot say which pool, they absorb fees, tips, rent and transfer fees into the price, and they miss native-SOL legs and confidential amounts. Instruction and event decoding gives the exact pool, direction, fee split and post-trade reserves, but is brittle to layout churn and silent when a program or variant is unknown. The hybrid the research supports has five steps. Resolve the key list once and compute per-(owner, mint) token deltas and per-owner lamport deltas, stripping the network fee, priority fee, tips and rent. Walk top-level and inner instructions in order and dispatch each by program and discriminator, attaching CPI events found beneath the AMM call and log events found between that program's `invoke` and `success` lines. Reconcile every decoded swap event against the wallet delta (amount in plus transfer fee plus fees) and, on mismatch, keep the delta and mark the record low confidence. Use events for what deltas cannot give: pool address, direction, fee split, reserves, curve progress. Emit an "unclassified token movement" for unknown programs rather than dropping the transaction, and key ordering on the transaction `index` that Yellowstone provides. The audit found the current parser is delta-only and drops anything it cannot sign, so the dropped population (LP and position transactions, USD1-quoted pools) is unmeasurable today.

### Jupiter routes

Jupiter's instruction data holds the route plan (an enum naming the AMM type per step, a percent split, input and output indices), `in_amount`, `quoted_out_amount`, slippage and platform fee, but not pool addresses; those sit in the remaining accounts, and the actual AMM calls are inner instructions ([jupiter-cpi IDL](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jup-ag/jupiter-cpi/main/idl.json)). The readable public IDL is old (39 variants, no `route_v2`), so the plan decoding is unreliable and should serve only for amounts and slippage. The cleaner per-hop record is the Jupiter `SwapEvent {amm, input_mint, input_amount, output_mint, output_amount}`, emitted as a CPI event, followed by the child AMM instruction and the inner token transfers beneath it; consecutive events chain output mint to input mint, and splits share an input mint. A defensive decoder treats any Jupiter instruction with an unknown discriminator as route-like and falls back to inner instructions and events, and logs the unknown discriminator. On the quote side, `routePlan[].swapInfo` carries `ammKey`, `label`, mints and amounts per leg; `ammKey` equalled the indexer's pool address for PumpSwap and Raydium CLMM in live checks and is opaque for prop AMMs ([Jupiter docs](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/swap-api/get-quote)). The unit of `priceImpactPct` is undocumented, but both the audit and the pool research read live magnitudes (0.000399 at 5,000 SOL, 0.0971% at 100 SOL, 19.9% for a 5 SOL buy into a small pool) as fractions, so GodsEye's `*100` is right. Third-party sources say the v1 quote API is being replaced by `/swap/v2/order`; this is unconfirmed by an official page, and the agent's dependence on the unauthenticated `lite-api` v1 endpoint is a latent risk.

### Liquidity events, migrations and live state over gRPC

Classify by program and discriminator first (the names are unambiguous: withdraw, remove_liquidity and decrease_liquidity against swap), confirm with the program's event, and sanity-check with deltas: in LP-token pools a removal lowers both vaults together, burns LP and pays the user both tokens, whereas a swap moves one side in and the other out and shifts the reserve ratio; position-based pools (CLMM, Whirlpool, DLMM, DAMM v2) have no LP supply, so rely on the position event or the pool's `liquidity` field. Migrations are explicit for pump (`CompleteEvent` and `CompletePumpAmmMigrationEvent`, then PumpSwap `CreatePoolEvent`) and LaunchLab (`migrate_to_cpswap`), but DBC emits no migration event, so the signal is the `migration_damm_v2` instruction at top level or inner, and the stored `is_migrated` flag. The curve's no-sell window between `complete` and migration should be treated as untradable. In the DBC sample from the earlier research, 52 of 100 high-volume tokens graduated with a median 5.6 minutes to graduate and a median **$0.49 of post-migration liquidity**, so a DAMM v2 pool reached from DBC is not exit liquidity until its depth and position holders are read. For a bot holding a small set of tokens, the supported design is a transaction filter on the held pools' addresses, which returns full transactions and costs in proportion to swap traffic, plus an account subscription on the LP mint for LP-token pools, which is written only when supply changes, with a data slice on the supply field to keep messages tiny ([Triton filter docs](https://docs.triton.one/project-yellowstone/dragons-mouth-grpc-subscriptions), [geyser.proto](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rpcpool/yellowstone-grpc/master/yellowstone-grpc-proto/proto/geyser.proto)). Filters combine by AND across fields and OR within arrays; account updates carry `txn_signature`, so join them to transactions by signature rather than arrival order, dedupe on signature because a processed subscriber can receive two blocks for one slot told apart by `bank_id`, and expect no published bandwidth figures. Message and byte rates per program must be measured by instrumenting the existing stream. Reserves can be kept current for free from the events already received (pump `TradeEvent` and PumpSwap `BuyEvent` carry post-trade reserves, DAMM v2 `EvtSwap2` carries reserves, DBC `EvtSwap2` carries curve progress), then seeded and reconciled with `getMultipleAccounts` of up to 100 accounts.

### Which open-source decoders fit a TypeScript codebase

No maintained library decodes all these programs at today's layouts. The lightest robust path is IDL code generation: pin the programs' IDLs in the repository with dates, generate TypeScript clients with Codama (MIT, `codama` 1.11.0 published 2026-09-15, generates `@solana/kit` codecs, no `web3.js`), or hand-write the few small Borsh decoders (swap events are 5 to 15 fields), and put one dispatch table `programId -> discriminator -> decoder` on top ([Codama](https://github.com/codama-idl/codama)). Orca's own client is Codama-generated, which is a point in the approach's favour. Carbon is the best-engineered full pipeline (Rust, MIT, v2.0.0 on 2026-09-05, 63 decoder crates including every program here, Yellowstone datasource), but it would be a sidecar or rewrite for a TypeScript agent, and on `main` its pump decoder lacks `buy_v3`, `sell_v3` and `multi_hop_curve_swap` and its PumpSwap decoder lacks `buy_v2`, `sell_v2` and `multi_hop_swap`, so regenerate from current IDLs before use ([Carbon](https://github.com/sevenlabs-hq/carbon)). `solana-dex-parser` (TypeScript, MIT, wide coverage) was last released in October 2025, which predates pump v2/v3, multi-hop and the Meteora `*2` variants, so use it as a reference for output shapes and bot routers, not as the source of 2026 layouts ([solana-dex-parser](https://github.com/cxcx-ai/solana-dex-parser)). The vendor SDKs track their programs closely (`@pump-fun/pump-sdk` 4.3.1, `@pump-fun/pump-swap-sdk` 2.2.0, `@meteora-ag/cp-amm-sdk` 1.5.1, `@meteora-ag/dynamic-bonding-curve-sdk` 2.0.0) but pull in `@coral-xyz/anchor` and `web3.js`, the DLMM SDK depends on `express`, and the Raydium SDK is GPL-3.0, so use them offline for fixtures and cross-checks rather than as parsing dependencies. Helius Parsed Streams is a managed alternative at a per-event cost, with `decoded: null` for versions it has not indexed. Whatever is chosen, keep three to five fixture transactions per instruction variant captured from the live stream, and a canary that logs any program and discriminator seen in a venue-program transaction that is not in the table, so a layout change is noticed the day it ships; re-diff the IDLs weekly.

## What GodsEye gets wrong today, from venue blindness to missing pool state

Seven errors change numbers or decisions, and a handful of smaller ones follow them. They share three roots: the stream and parser identify too few programs, the agent stores no facts about the mint, and nothing reads the pool.

**Venue blindness.** `VENUES` holds nine ids and the live gRPC filter is built from the same list, so a swap that touches only DAMM v2, DBC or LaunchLab is never streamed, and in the database **255,623 swaps (8.95%) have `venue IS NULL`**; in 46 sampled null-venue transactions 44 called DAMM v2, and the daily null share ran 16.75% on 10-17, 36.71% on 10-18 and 0.09% on 10-25, a launch wave rather than a steady background ([audit](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/research_notes/Pool%20and%20contract%20intelligence/godseye_current_understanding.md), [programs.ts](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/chain/programs.ts)). One nuance the audit does not mention: programs.ts as read at report time already carries a `LABEL_ONLY_VENUES` map naming DBC and DAMM v2, deliberately kept out of the stream because subscribing to Meteora's bonding-curve launches "floods wallet promotion with volume-farm wallets". So the stored NULLs are most likely rows written before those labels existed (the commit is dated 2026-10-27, the null-venue rows run 10-17 to 10-26), they cannot be relabelled from the table because `wallet_swaps` has no program or pool column, and the real open decision is a trade-off the audit did not weigh: widening the stream restores visibility but imports the farms the owner excluded. LaunchLab (`LanMV9s...`), Heaven, Moonit, Boop.fun and the prop AMMs remain unlabelled and unmeasurable. The consequence is concrete. Mint `FVzXko3DbK5bKPVf8xFwogoGFJXqLktBVsBYCs9RkyEp` (`michi`) recorded 3,114 null-venue swaps from 21 wallets, each making about 90 buys and 90 sells with net SOL about zero, 7,512 SOL gross in 16 minutes, while RugCheck lists one DAMM v2 market with 0% LP locked and about $54 liquidity. **63 of the 68 wallets with over half their swaps null-venue are `insider` tier**, against 1,340 insiders among 6,959 wallets, and the classifier credits "direct" venue use only for five named venues, so these wallets pick up neither the direct-AMM bonus nor a penalty. This does not argue for dropping insiders, which stays the owner's decision; it argues for flagging wash loops at the token and swap level so they stop counting as skill. How many of the 68 are farms and how many genuinely trade a DAMM v2 token was not measured, since only `michi` was examined.

**Transfer fees ignored on Token-2022 mints.** There is no rule for `transferFeeConfig`, so a copy buy passes the screen and pays the fee both ways. The parser's stored price is net on buys and gross on sells, and the replay then divides again by `1 +/- feeBps` and fits depth from prices that are already fee-contaminated, so the wallet-level price is worse than the pool's in both directions and the model double counts ([model.ts](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/replay/model.ts)). The post-simulation check could also fail if Jupiter's `otherAmountThreshold` ignores the fee, which is untested.

**Stale rent constant.** `2_039_280` against a live 1,488,440 (165 bytes) and 1,513,840 (170 bytes). On a real PumpSwap buy that opened a Token-2022 account the code stored `sol_amount = 0.056434` where the rent-corrected figure is `0.056959`, understating the buy by 0.000525 SOL, or 0.93%; the same constant over-credits the refund on a full exit, and the residual can exceed `SOL_NOISE` and produce a spurious `SOL+USDC` label ([parse.ts](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/chain/parse.ts)). GodsEye's own recorded fills use the same parser, so its fill accounting inherits the error ([executor.ts](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/agent/src/copy/executor.ts)).

**Tips priced into swaps.** Only the eight Jito accounts are recognised. In 12 sampled direct Raydium, Orca and Meteora swaps carrying the mixed `SOL+USDC` label, all 12 had 0.0005 to 0.0039 SOL of unexplained native SOL, six of them at about 1,000,000 to 1,000,100 lamports, that is neither a Jito account nor rent. The sample was chosen by label, so prevalence is unknown. The consequence is a wrong quote label, a hidden bot-tip signal for the AI-versus-human classifier, and an inflated cost that matters most on small trades.

**Tokenized stocks counted as memecoins.** The 23 permanent-delegate mints in the swap-weighted sample (xStocks such as `TSLAx` and "Backpack Securities" tokens such as `AMD`, `LLY`, `MU`) account for 18,844 swaps, 701 wallets and 46,391 SOL, and `isNonMemecoinSymbol` catches only the `/^[A-Z]{1,5}x$/` pattern and a fixed list ([constants.ts](file:///Users/srinjoydas/GodsEye/shared/src/constants.ts)). Win rate, P&L and early-buy metrics for those wallets therefore mix equity trading, with market-hours price moves, into memecoin skill. The copy-time rug check would block them, so the damage is to ranking, not to funds. The extension signature (permanent delegate plus scaled UI amount, with freeze and mint authorities set) is a cheap, on-chain flag.

**The LP-lock rule misfires on concentrated-liquidity pools.** The gate blocks when RugCheck's liquidity-weighted `lpLockedPct` is below `LP_MIN_LOCKED_PCT` (default 90). For PUMP, RugCheck's 440 markets show about 2e-6 on the main PumpSwap pool and exactly 0 on every Orca and DLMM pool, on $29.6M of liquidity ([RugCheck report](https://api.rugcheck.xyz/v1/tokens/pumpCmXqMfrsAkQ5r49WcJnRayYRqmXz6ae8H7H9Dfn/report), third-party service). The metric assumes an LP token, which Orca, CLMM, DLMM and DAMM v2 do not have, and for canonical PumpSwap pools the burn is at the program level. The same rule passes with only a warning when RugCheck has no pool data. The header comment claiming pump curves and graduated pools count as locked is not implemented. The rule has never run: `rug_checks` is empty.

**No on-chain pool state.** Whole-repo grep finds no pool, vault, LP-mint, position or program account read; `accounts: {}` in the subscription; no pool column in `wallet_swaps`; and `token_info.pool_address` is GeckoTerminal's highest-reserve pool for only 16.7% of tokens, used only for candles. Nothing can say what the reserves, fee tier or LP supply were at the leader's swap, whether a pool was drained, or whether a token has several liquid pools. The replay model consequently assumes a single constant-product pool with a fixed 100 bps fee and fits an "effective SOL reserve" from consecutive tape swaps clamped to 1 to 5,000 SOL, defaulting to 30 SOL, grouped by mint only; 1,554 of 9,297 mints on 2026-10-22 traded across at least two venue families. Replay-derived copy P&L, delay cost and wallet ranking carry model error of unknown sign, and wash-traded tapes will overstate depth because large fake flows barely move price.

**Smaller errors.** Quote assets other than SOL, USDC and USDT are dropped before insertion (USD1, PYUSD and USDS sit on the exclusion list), so leaders on USD1-paired pools look inactive; the rate is unmeasurable. SOL/USD history comes from the hourly close of the Raydium v4 SOL/USDC pool, which had $2.83M 24-hour volume against $100.2M on the Orca pool, and the hourly close is applied inside its hour. Execution knows programs but not routes: the allowlist covers top-level programs, the sell quote and price impact are log-only, the simulation slack is a flat 0.01 SOL, and nothing requires a sell route to exist before a buy. Token age is NULL on 22% of swaps, launch time comes from the first transaction or GeckoTerminal page 1, and crash candles use today's deepest pool rather than where the wallet sold. Token-to-token detection can be fooled by unrelated SOL movers in the same transaction (not observed).

## Build order: mint facts first, then parser fixes, then pool state

The ranking weighs measured impact, how cheaply the evidence can be tested, and dependencies. "Evidence-backed" means the failure was measured in GodsEye's data or chain; "design" means the researchers' proposal built on source facts.

## Conclusion

Two things change how the research reads. First, the cheapest errors are the most certain ones: the rent constant, tips, extensions and the equity flag are all one-account reads or one-line fixes with measured effects, so they should land before anything that needs a pool model. Second, the disagreement between the audit and the code on DAMM v2 and DBC shows the real design question is not "add the ids" but how to see these venues without importing the farms; counting their flow per mint for market existence and wash detection, while keeping it out of wallet promotion, answers both the audit's blindness finding and the owner's earlier reason for exclusion, and it fits the standing rule that insider-pattern wallets stay copyable and the public site stays redacted.

The larger shift is from third-party liquidity numbers to chain-derived ones. Most venues share one formula, so a small registry that records the pool at its creation or migration event, reads two or three accounts, and returns a 10% and a 30% exit figure would replace the replay model's fitted depth and RugCheck's LP percentage with quantities that mean the same thing across venues. Its limits are real: offsets are untested, prop AMMs stay opaque, and routers split sells, so Jupiter's quote remains the cross-check rather than the replacement. Everything in the last table can be answered with a handful of live account reads and a day of stream instrumentation, which makes "measure, then build" cheaper than guessing.
